\section{Nixie tube characteristics}
\label{sec:tubes}

The board carries six cold-cathode gas-discharge indicator tubes: four
IN-12B (\textsc{nx1}--\textsc{nx4}) forming the HH:MM digit display,
and two IN-6 (\textsc{nx5}--\textsc{nx6}) forming the colon separator.
Both are Soviet-made parts documented only in scanned factory sheets;
the figures below are transcribed directly from those scans, with the
original Russian parameter names noted where a term's meaning is not
self-evident from context. Neither tube's datasheet is a
Western-style datasheet with guaranteed min/typ/max columns throughout
--- several entries below are single-sided limits (``not more than''
or ``not less than'') rather than full min/typ/max triples, and that
asymmetry is preserved rather than papered over, since it matters for
how conservatively the anode supply must be sized.

\paragraph{Not specified in either source.} A combined (total) anode
current limit distinct from the per-cathode figures; an interelectrode
or anode-cathode capacitance; a manufacturer-recommended ballast
resistor value or target anode current within the permissible 2.5--3.5~mA
band.

\paragraph{Open question: anode supply voltage.} The datasheet's own
minimum anode supply figure (\SI{200}{\volt}, recommended
\SI{250}{\volt}) sits \emph{above} the board's full \SIrange{160}{196}{\volt}
trim range --- the design never reaches even the stated minimum, let
alone the recommended value. The most likely explanation is that this
factory figure is written for the tube's other stated supply mode,
unfiltered half-wave-rectified AC (\S\ref{sec:in12b}, ``Cathode current,
average''): an unsmoothed half-wave supply spends roughly half of every
mains cycle near zero volts, so its \emph{peak} voltage must be pushed
well above the \SI{145}{\volt} typical maintaining voltage to sustain
adequate \emph{average} current and fast, reliable strike --- a
requirement that does not carry over unchanged to a continuously
present, regulated DC rail such as this board's. This interpretation
is plausible but not confirmed by the source, which does not explain
the discrepancy directly. \Cref{sec:hv-converter} carries this forward
as an explicit open item rather than a silently resolved one; the
existing bench-tuning procedure (raising the trim potentiometer until
every digit of the actual tube stock strikes reliably, then setting
the rail roughly \SI{10}{\volt} above that point) is an empirical
check against exactly this uncertainty, independent of which
interpretation is correct.

\subsection{IN-6 (colon tubes, NX5--NX6)}
\label{sec:in6}

Source: \texttt{IN-6.pdf} (original Soviet factory label sheet, GOST
17821-75). The tube has three electrode leads: lead 1 is the anode
(A), physically set apart from the other two and used as the
numbering reference; lead 2 is the indicator cathode (K); lead 3 is
the auxiliary priming cathode (KA). This assignment is confirmed
directly against the datasheet's electrode table.

\paragraph{Not specified.} The datasheet gives no textual explanation
of the auxiliary cathode's (KA) function or of its intended handling
if left unconnected; its rating is otherwise symmetric with the
indicator cathode (K). The manufacturer's example circuit routes one
cathode to a direct ground reference and the other through an
RC network to a sensed node, which is \emph{consistent with} KA
serving a priming/keep-alive role, but this is inferred from the
circuit diagram, not stated in prose, and is not confirmed with full
confidence. No interelectrode capacitance is given. Two maintaining-voltage
figures appear for the same \SI{0.7}{\milli\ampere} test current
(\SI{88}{\volt} under ``basic parameters'' vs.\ \SI{90}{\volt} under
``permissible operating modes''); the source does not explain the
2~V discrepancy, which is small enough to be a nominal-vs.-rated
distinction rather than a substantive conflict.

\paragraph{Maximum switching frequency vs.\ intended use.} The
\SI{0.5}{\hertz} maximum discharge switching frequency is a tube
rating, not a clock-design choice; it bounds how fast this tube's gap
can be safely toggled and is noted here for
\cref{sec:hv-converter} and later blink-rate/firmware decisions, since
a colon indicator blinking faster than \SI{0.5}{\hertz} on this tube
would exceed its rated switching behavior. It does not affect the
converter's DC current budget, which uses only the permissible anode
current above.

\subsection{Handling of the auxiliary cathode (KA)}

Per the existing project notes on this part, the KA electrode is
unused in this design's always-on service and is left unconnected. The
datasheet does not describe a failure mode for leaving KA floating, so
this is noted as an inherited design decision rather than one verified
against the source in this document.
