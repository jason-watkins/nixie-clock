\section{5~V converter derivation}\label{sec:5v-converter}

This section derives the required characteristics of the 5~V rail and specifies
component values that satisfy them, built around the TPS629203
(\cref{sec:tps629203}). The converter is the only device of its kind on the
rail, so it is referred to by part number throughout.
\Cref{sec:bcd-driver} already designates the four K155ID1 packages U1--U4; this
document assigns the remaining designators used throughout this section as
follows:

The rail's topology comprises: the converter with its filter components
(\des{L}{5V}, \des{C}{5VIN}, \des{C}{5VO1}, \des{C}{5VO2}), the two
configuration resistors that program it, and the four
K155ID1 $V_{CC}$ pins (U1--U4, pin 5) with their bypass
capacitors C1--C4. The rail has no connection to the MCU or any other subsystem.
No bootstrap capacitor appears in that list because the part requires none
(\cref{sec:tps629203}).

\subsection{Load budget}\label{sec:5v-load}

Each K155ID1 draws $I_{CC} = 16$~mA typical, \SI{25}{\milli\ampere} maximum
(\cref{sec:bcd-driver}; specified at $V_{CC}=$ max, inputs grounded, outputs open). The
cathode current sunk by the selected output does \emph{not} add to this: the driver output
is an open-collector TTL stage sinking current that arrives from the 170~V rail through
the tube, into the output pin, and out the ground pin; it never passes through
$V_{CC}$. The rail load is therefore the four packages' logic supply current only:
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{load,typ}} &= 4 \times \SI{16}{\milli\ampere} = \SI{64}{\milli\ampere}, &
  I_{\mathrm{load,max}} &= 4 \times \SI{25}{\milli\ampere} = \SI{100}{\milli\ampere},
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
  P_{\mathrm{out,typ}} &= 5 \times 0.064 = \SI{0.32}{\watt}, &
  P_{\mathrm{out,max}} &= 5 \times 0.100 = \SI{0.50}{\watt}.
\end{align*}
Bipolar TTL supply current is set by the internal bias networks, not by output state, so
this load is static: it does not change with which digit is lit, and the only
input transitions are the once-per-minute (and slower) BCD updates. There is no transient
load profile to size against. The \SI{100}{\nano\farad} per-package bypass capacitors
C1--C4 are standard one-per-TTL-package practice and contribute no DC load.

\subsection{Operating point}\label{sec:5v-input}

The input is $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, the USB-PD-negotiated rail (9 or \SI{12}{\volt} nominal
contracts; analysis window \SIrange{8.5}{13}{\volt}), tied directly to both VIN and EN with
no series element. The front end also admits a \SI{5}{\volt} contract
(\cref{sec:pd-window}), which puts this converter outside the window everything
below is derived at; \cref{sec:5v-limited} treats that case separately and
nothing else in this section depends on it. The window lies inside the part's recommended
\SIrange{3}{17}{\volt} operating range (\cref{sec:tps629203}). Against the
\SI{18}{\volt} absolute maximum shared by VIN, EN, PG, and MODE/S-CONF, the
\SI{13}{\volt} maximum uses \SI{72}{\percent} of the rating, inside the
\SI{80}{\percent} derating this document applies to voltage ratings throughout.
That is the tightest input-side margin in the low-voltage design and the one
figure that would need revisiting if the bus were ever specified higher.

The ideal buck duty cycle $D = V_{OUT}/V_{IN}$ spans
\begin{align*}
  D_{\min} &= \frac{5}{13} = 0.385 \quad (V_{IN}=\SI{13}{\volt}), &
  D_{\max} &= \frac{5}{8.5} = 0.588 \quad (V_{IN}=\SI{8.5}{\volt}).
\end{align*}
Conduction-loss corrections to $D$ are of order $I_{\mathrm{load}} R_{DS(\mathrm{on})}
\approx \SI{25}{\milli\volt}$ against a 5~V output (below 0.5\,\%) and are ignored
throughout. The \SI{100}{\percent}-duty mode the part enters as $V_{IN}$ approaches
$V_{OUT}$ is never reached: it requires
$V_{IN} \leq V_{OUT} + I_{OUT}(R_{DS(\mathrm{on})} + R_L)
= 5 + 0.1\,(0.25+0.10) = \SI{5.04}{\volt}$ at the DCR bound of
\cref{sec:5v-inductor}, against a \SI{8.5}{\volt} minimum input.

\subsection{Output voltage and device configuration}\label{sec:5v-vset}

Two resistors program the converter, and both are read once during start-up
rather than interpreted continuously (\cref{sec:tps629203}).

\paragraph{Output voltage.} The part offers an internal feedback divider selected
by a resistor from the VSET pin to ground, with \SI{5.0}{\volt} among its
eighteen preset options at $R = \SI{80.6}{\kilo\ohm}$. Specification:
$\des{R}{5VSET} = \SI{80.6}{\kilo\ohm}$, E96 1\,\% with a temperature coefficient no worse
than $\pm200$~ppm/\si{\celsius}, as the selection table requires. This is
preferred over the external-divider configuration the same pin also supports,
because it keeps the rail's accuracy at the part's own $\pm1\,\%$ specification.
An external divider would put the feedback reference's tolerance and the divider
ratio's tolerance in series, and the ratio error alone would consume a
comparable share of the K155ID1's supply window (\cref{sec:5v-cout}). With VSET
selected, the output is
sensed through the VOS pin, which connects directly to the positive terminal of
the output capacitance.

\paragraph{Mode, frequency, and discharge.} A resistor from MODE/S-CONF to ground
selects, in one value, whether the feedback is internal or external, the
switching-frequency mode, whether output discharge is enabled, and whether the
converter runs forced PWM or automatic power save. The combination this rail
requires is internal feedback with automatic power save, since the load is far
below the power-save entry threshold at every corner (\cref{sec:5v-op}) and there
is no reason to force continuous conduction into a static load. Automatic
efficiency enhancement is taken with it, which adjusts the switching frequency
with the conversion ratio. Output discharge is enabled so the rail ramps down in
a defined way when the bus falls away; without it the rail decays through
whatever leakage the drivers present. Specification:
$\des{R}{5VMODE} = \SI{27.4}{\kilo\ohm}$, to the same E96 1\,\% and $\pm200$~ppm/\si{\celsius}
requirement.

\subsection{Inductor}\label{sec:5v-inductor}

The part is designed around a nominal \SI{2.2}{\micro\henry} inductor, with a
recommended range of \SIrange{1.0}{4.7}{\micro\henry}
(\cref{sec:tps629203}). Values below \SI{2.2}{\micro\henry} are explicitly not
recommended where the voltage across the inductor is large, which is this rail's
condition at the top of the bus range, because they raise both the peak current
at full load and the negative inductor current in forced PWM. Values above it
lower ripple at the cost of efficiency and transient response. The recommended
filter table pairs \SI{2.2}{\micro\henry} with the output capacitance specified
in \cref{sec:5v-cout} and marks that pairing as the standard combination.
Specification: $\des{L}{5V} = \SI{2.2}{\micro\henry}$.

\paragraph{Ripple current.} In automatic power save with efficiency enhancement,
the on-time is set by the conversion ratio,
$t_{ON} = \SI{100}{\nano\second}\times V_{IN}/(V_{IN}-V_{OUT})$, and the ripple
follows from it:
\begin{align*}
  \Delta I_L &= \frac{V_{IN}-V_{OUT}}{L}\,t_{ON}
  &&\SI{8.5}{\volt}: \frac{3.5}{\SI{2.2}{\micro\henry}}\times\SI{243}{\nano\second}
     = \SI{0.39}{\ampere}, \\
  &&&\SI{13}{\volt}: \frac{8}{\SI{2.2}{\micro\henry}}\times\SI{162}{\nano\second}
     = \SI{0.59}{\ampere},
\end{align*}
reproducing the datasheet's own ripple-versus-input curve at this inductance. The
part carries an explicit caution that ripple must not exceed \SI{1.6}{\ampere},
above which the negative current limit can run continuously at no load; the worst
case here is \SI{0.59}{\ampere}, a factor of \num{2.7} inside that bound.

\paragraph{Saturation current.} The largest current the converter can drive into
the inductor is set by its own current limit, extended by the propagation delay
before the high-side switch actually opens:
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{peak}} &= I_{LIMH} + \frac{V_{IN}-V_{OUT}}{L}\times\SI{50}{\nano\second}
    = 1.4 + \frac{8}{\SI{1.76}{\micro\henry}}\times\SI{50}{\nano\second}
    = \SI{1.63}{\ampere},
\end{align*}
evaluated at the high-side limit's \SI{1.4}{\ampere} maximum and the inductance
at its $-20\,\%$ tolerance extreme, both of which push the figure up.
Specification: $I_{\mathrm{sat}} \geq \SI{1.7}{\ampere}$, DCR
$\leq\SI{100}{\milli\ohm}$. Normal operation never approaches this: the largest
current the control loop commands is the \SI{0.59}{\ampere} power-save peak
above, so the requirement is set by fault capability rather than by the load. At
\SI{100}{\milli\ampere} the DCR bound costs under \SI{1}{\milli\watt}, so it
constrains nothing and exists only to exclude parts whose resistance would be
pathological.

\subsection{Conduction mode and switching frequency}\label{sec:5v-op}

With efficiency enhancement selected, the switching frequency follows the
conversion ratio:
\begin{align*}
  f_{SW} &= 10\,V_{OUT}\frac{V_{IN}-V_{OUT}}{V_{IN}^2}
  &&\SI{8.5}{\volt}: \SI{2.42}{\mega\hertz}; \quad
    \SI{10}{\volt}: \SI{2.50}{\mega\hertz}; \quad
    \SI{13}{\volt}: \SI{2.37}{\mega\hertz},
\end{align*}
in megahertz with the voltages in volts. For a 5~V output the expression is
nearly flat across this rail's entire input window and sits at or just below the
\SI{2.5}{\mega\hertz} ceiling throughout, so the rail behaves as a fixed
\SI{2.4}{\mega\hertz} converter for every purpose in this section.

The converter runs in power save mode whenever the load falls below half the
ripple current (\cref{sec:5v-inductor}):
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{load(PSM)}} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta I_L
  &&\SI{8.5}{\volt}: \SI{193}{\milli\ampere}; \quad
    \SI{13}{\volt}: \SI{296}{\milli\ampere}.
\end{align*}
The actual load, \SIrange{64}{100}{\milli\ampere}, sits below that boundary at
every corner, so the rail is in power save at all times. Power save is also
available at every corner: the part suspends it when the input falls within
about \SI{15}{\percent} of the output, which for a 5~V rail is
\SI{5.75}{\volt}, well below the \SI{8.5}{\volt} minimum input. Crossing that
boundary is glitchless in either direction, and no component choice depends on
where it lies.

\subsection{Minimum on-time}\label{sec:5v-ontime}

The on-time in power save is the $t_{ON}$ of \cref{sec:5v-inductor},
\SI{243}{\nano\second} at the minimum input falling to \SI{162}{\nano\second} at
the maximum, against a \SI{40}{\nano\second} floor: a margin of \num{4.1} at the
binding corner. In forced pulse-width modulation the on-time would be
$(V_{OUT}/V_{IN})/f_{SW} = \SI{163}{\nano\second}$ at the same corner, the same
figure to within the frequency's own variation. The complementary
constraint, a minimum off-time of about \SI{80}{\nano\second}, binds at the
opposite end: at \SI{8.5}{\volt} input and \SI{2.42}{\mega\hertz} the off-time is
$1/f_{SW} - t_{ON} = \SI{170}{\nano\second}$, a margin of \num{2.1}. Neither
constrains the design, and the off-time is the tighter of the two only because
the duty cycle is highest at minimum input.

\subsection{Current-limit margin}\label{sec:5v-ilim}

The high-side current limit is \SI{0.8}{\ampere} minimum
(\cref{sec:tps629203}). The largest peak the control loop commands is the
power-save peak of \cref{sec:5v-inductor}:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{I_{LIM}^{\min}}{I_{\mathrm{peak}}} &= \frac{0.8}{0.59} = 1.4.
\end{align*}
Accounting for the propagation delay that extends the actual trip point, the
effective minimum is $0.8 + (8/\SI{2.2}{\micro\henry})\times\SI{50}{\nano\second}
= \SI{0.98}{\ampere}$, giving \num{1.7}. This is a smaller margin than an
oversized converter would show, a direct consequence of matching the part to the
load. The limit is a fault threshold, set to sit near but above the largest
current normal operation produces. Were it reached, the high-side switch would
turn off and the low-side switch would ramp the inductor down until the current
fell below the low-side threshold --- cycle-by-cycle behavior, with no fault
latch.

\subsection{Output capacitance and rail accuracy}\label{sec:5v-cout}

The part requires effective output capacitance between \SIrange{10}{100}{\micro\farad}
(\cref{sec:tps629203}), and the recommended filter table pairs the
\SI{2.2}{\micro\henry} inductance with nominal values from \SIrange{10}{100}{\micro\farad}.
\emph{Assumption:} X5R or X7R ceramics rated $\geq\SI{10}{\volt}$ lose no more
than \SI{50}{\percent} of nominal capacitance to bias and tolerance at
\SI{5}{\volt}, the derating the datasheet itself applies when tabulating those
combinations. Specification: \des{C}{5VO1}, \des{C}{5VO2} $= 2\times\SI{22}{\micro\farad}$,
\SI{44}{\micro\farad} nominal and \SI{22}{\micro\farad} effective, sitting in the
middle of the permitted range. A single \SI{22}{\micro\farad} part is the
datasheet's standard combination and would also satisfy the table, but under the
same derating it lands at \SI{11}{\micro\farad} effective, \SI{10}{\percent}
above the floor, with no margin for a part whose bias characteristic is worse
than assumed.

\paragraph{Ripple.} Output ripple in power save mode is
\begin{align*}
  \Delta V &= \frac{L\,V_{IN}^2}{200\,C}
    \left(\frac{1}{V_{IN}-V_{OUT}}+\frac{1}{V_{OUT}}\right)
  &&\SI{8.5}{\volt}: \SI{17.5}{\milli\volt}; \quad
    \SI{13}{\volt}: \SI{27.5}{\milli\volt},
\end{align*}
with the inductance in \si{\micro\henry} and the effective capacitance in
\si{\micro\farad}. Ripple grows with input voltage, so the maximum bus voltage is
the binding corner.

\paragraph{Accuracy stack against the load's window.} The internal divider holds
the output to $\pm1\,\%$ over the full junction-temperature range, so regulation
alone spans \SIrange{4.95}{5.05}{\volt}. Adding the worst-case ripple to both
ends:
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{rail}} &\in [\,4.95-0.028,\; 5.05+0.028\,]
    = [\,\SI{4.92}{\volt},\ \SI{5.08}{\volt}\,]
\end{align*}
against the K155ID1's recommended \SIrange{4.75}{5.25}{\volt} supply window
(\cref{sec:bcd-driver}): approximately \SI{170}{\milli\volt} of margin on each
side. This is the figure the internal divider protects. An external divider built
from 1\,\% resistors would add roughly $\pm1.7\,\%$ of ratio error on top of the
reference's own tolerance, consuming about half the margin above for no
benefit, since \SI{5.0}{\volt} is already one of the preset options.

\subsection{Input capacitance}\label{sec:5v-cin}

The part requires at least \SI{3}{\micro\farad} of effective input capacitance
with \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} nominal recommended (\cref{sec:tps629203}). At the
\SI{13}{\volt} top of the bus window a \SI{25}{\volt}-rated ceramic loses roughly
half its nominal capacitance to bias, so a \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} nominal part
would land at the requirement with no margin above it. Specification:
\des{C}{5VIN} $= \SI{10}{\micro\farad}$, giving about \SI{5}{\micro\farad} effective at the
worst bias, \num{1.7} times the floor. The buck's input RMS ripple current at
the worst point ($D$ nearest 0.5, i.e.\ $V_{IN}=\SI{8.5}{\volt}$):
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{RMS}} &\approx I_{\mathrm{out}}\sqrt{D(1-D)}
    = 0.100\sqrt{0.588 \times 0.412} = \SI{49}{\milli\ampere},
\end{align*}
trivially inside any ceramic \SI{10}{\micro\farad}'s ampere-class rating. The
capacitor's voltage rating must be specified separately: on a \SI{13}{\volt} rail
it must be $\geq\SI{16}{\volt}$, and \SI{25}{\volt} is preferred for
bias-derating headroom.

\subsection{Enable, soft start, and dissipation}\label{sec:5v-ss}

EN ties directly to $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$. The pin must be terminated, and its
\SI{18}{\volt} rating matches VIN's, so no divider or
level shift is required; the part's own undervoltage lockout, \SI{3}{\volt}
maximum rising, releases far below the \SI{8.5}{\volt} minimum input. The
precise enable threshold would permit a programmed lockout at a higher voltage,
but nothing in this rail's requirements asks for one. The power-good output is
unused and left unconnected.

Start-up is internally timed: up to \SI{1.8}{\milli\second} of delay from enable
to the first switching pulse in the internal-divider configuration, then a
soft-start ramp of up to \SI{700}{\micro\second} to the target. The charging
current that ramp implies is
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{chg}} &= C_{\mathrm{out}}\frac{dV}{dt}
    = \SI{22}{\micro\farad}\times\frac{\SI{5}{\volt}}{\SI{600}{\micro\second}}
    = \SI{183}{\milli\ampere},
\end{align*}
which even summed with the full \SI{100}{\milli\ampere} load stays a factor of
\num{2.8} below the minimum current limit: no inrush interaction with the limit.
The drivers' 5~V therefore arrives about \SI{2.5}{\milli\second} after the bus
does. No sequencing constraint exists among this rail, the 3.3~V rail, or the
flyback at this level; cross-rail power-up ordering is a system-level property,
not a property of this converter.

Dissipation, using the conservative $\eta = 0.85$ carried for the low-voltage
rails:
\begin{align*}
  P_{\mathrm{diss}} &= P_{\mathrm{out,max}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-1\right)
    = 0.50 \times 0.176 = \SI{88}{\milli\watt}, &
  \Delta T &= \theta_{JA} P_{\mathrm{diss}} = 120 \times 0.088 \approx \SI{11}{\kelvin},
\end{align*}
so $T_J \approx \SI{51}{\celsius}$ at a \SI{40}{\celsius} ambient, far below the
\SI{125}{\celsius} recommended junction limit and further still below the
\SI{170}{\celsius} thermal shutdown. The \SI{120}{\celsius\per\watt} figure is
the datasheet's JEDEC-board value; its evaluation-board figure is
\SI{60}{\celsius\per\watt}, so the result above is the pessimistic of the two.
No thermal management beyond the footprint's own copper is required.

\subsection{Limited operation}\label{sec:5v-limited}

A \SI{5}{\volt} contract (\cref{sec:pd-classes}) puts $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ between
\SI{4.75}{\volt} and \SI{5.5}{\volt}, below the input this rail is derived
against and below its own output over most of that range. The converter does not
fail there; it enters the \SI{100}{\percent} duty mode
\cref{sec:5v-input} shows is unreachable above \SI{5.04}{\volt} of input,
holding the high-side switch on so the output follows the input less
\begin{align*}
  \Delta V &= I_{\mathrm{load}}\left(R_{DS(\mathrm{on})}+R_L\right)
    = 0.1\,(0.25+0.10) = \SI{35}{\milli\volt}
\end{align*}
at the \SI{100}{\milli\ampere} bound. The rail therefore lands between
\SI{4.72}{\volt} and \SI{5.47}{\volt} and is unregulated, tracking whatever the
bus does.

At the bottom of that range the K155ID1 packages sit below the
\SI{4.75}{\volt} floor of their recommended supply window
(\cref{sec:bcd-driver}), by at most \SI{30}{\milli\volt}. Two things bound what
that costs. The parts are idle: the \SI{170}{\volt} rail is inhibited in this
class (\cref{sec:hv-inhibit}), so every output is an open collector with no
anode supply behind it and no cathode current to switch. And an undervolted
bipolar TTL supply shifts input thresholds and output levels without stressing
anything; the recommended window is a specification boundary, and the rating
lies further out. The rail's accuracy result of \cref{sec:5v-cout} is therefore
conditional on a \SI{9}{\volt} or \SI{12}{\volt} contract, and no other result
in this section is.

\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:5v-summary}

Assumptions carried through this section: inductor $I_{\mathrm{sat}} \geq
\SI{1.7}{\ampere}$ and DCR $\leq\SI{100}{\milli\ohm}$ (commodity
\SI{2.2}{\micro\henry} part; a specific part remains to be selected); ceramics
retain at least \SI{50}{\percent} of nominal capacitance at their working bias,
the derating the datasheet applies to its own filter tables; converter efficiency
0.85. Each appears at its point of use above.

The rail's one open assumption is \des{L}{5V}'s saturation rating, inferred from
commodity-part norms for its value class instead of from a specific
manufacturer datasheet; a part meeting it remains to be selected. Everything
else resolves to a specified value with margin. The section's substantive result
is that matching the converter's rating to the load removes the two consequences
oversizing carried: the inductor's saturation requirement now follows from a
\SI{1.4}{\ampere} current limit, well below what a multi-ampere part would
demand, and the manufacturer's own filter-selection table applies as written
without calling for an inductance outside the part's recommended range.
