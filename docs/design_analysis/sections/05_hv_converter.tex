\section{170~V flyback converter derivation}\label{sec:hv-converter}

This section derives, from first principles and the component data established in
\cref{sec:in12b,sec:in6,sec:bcd-driver,sec:lm5155,sec:da2032}, every electrical
characteristic the 170~V flyback converter must have, and compares each derived value
against the populated Rev-A schematic. The schematic connectivity used here was taken
from the exported netlist of the \texttt{HV} sheet, not from prose notes; reference
designators below refer to that sheet. Where a populated value disagrees with the
derivation, or agrees only with thin margin, the disagreement is stated as a flag
rather than reconciled away.

Two operating points recur throughout:
\begin{description}
  \item[Nominal] $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = \SI{9}{\volt}$ (the lower USB-PD contract),
    output trimmed to \SI{170}{\volt}, tube currents as set by the populated ballasts.
  \item[Design-max envelope] $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = \SI{8.5}{\volt}$ (bottom of the input
    window, worst case for primary current), output at \SI{180}{\volt} (the trim
    ceiling recommended in \cref{sec:hv-setpoint}), and every tube simultaneously at
    its datasheet-maximum current. This envelope is deliberately conservative; no
    real operating condition reaches all of its corners at once.
\end{description}

\subsection{Load requirement and ballast verification}\label{sec:hv-load}

\subsubsection{Digit tubes (NX1--NX4, ballasts R1--R4)}

Each IN-12B anode is fed from the rail through its ballast; the selected cathode
returns through a K155ID1 output. The ballast equation is
\begin{align*}
  R_A &= \frac{V_{\mathrm{rail}} - V_M - V_K}{I_K},
\end{align*}
with maintaining voltage $V_M = \SI{145}{\volt}$ (typical, no tabulated spread;
\cref{sec:in12b}) and driver on-state drop $V_K$. The driver's \SI{2.5}{\volt}
maximum is specified at \SI{7}{\milli\ampere} (\cref{sec:bcd-driver}), more than
double the current here, so \SI{2.5}{\volt} serves as the conservative bound and
\SI{1.0}{\volt} as the expected value at \SIrange{2.5}{3.5}{\milli\ampere}
(assumption: saturation drop falls roughly with collector current; the true value is
bounded below by a few hundred millivolts).

At the \SI{170}{\volt} setpoint, the drop across the ballast is
$170 - 145 - 2.5 = \SI{22.5}{\volt}$ (conservative) to \SI{24}{\volt} (expected).
The DC cathode-current window \SIrange{2.5}{3.5}{\milli\ampere} (\cref{sec:in12b})
then requires
\begin{align*}
  R_A &\in \left[\frac{\SI{22.5}{\volt}}{\SI{3.5}{\milli\ampere}},\;
               \frac{\SI{22.5}{\volt}}{\SI{2.5}{\milli\ampere}}\right]
       = [\SI{6.43}{\kilo\ohm},\, \SI{9.00}{\kilo\ohm}].
\end{align*}
The populated \SI{8.2}{\kilo\ohm} sits inside this band, toward the low-current end:
\begin{align*}
  I_K &= \frac{\SI{22.5}{\volt}}{\SI{8.2}{\kilo\ohm}} = \SI{2.74}{\milli\ampere}
  \quad (V_K = \SI{2.5}{\volt}), &
  I_K &= \frac{\SI{24}{\volt}}{\SI{8.2}{\kilo\ohm}} = \SI{2.93}{\milli\ampere}
  \quad (V_K = \SI{1.0}{\volt}).
\end{align*}
Ballast dissipation is $I_K^2 R_A \approx \SI{70}{\milli\watt}$, trivial for the
populated DIN0207 axial parts ($\approx\SI{0.6}{\watt}$ rating). At \SI{170}{\volt}
the populated value is a match. Its behavior at a raised setpoint is deferred to
\cref{sec:hv-setpoint}, where it becomes the principal tension of this section.

The IN-12B decimal-point cathode is unconnected in this design (no driver signal
exists for it anywhere in the schematic or BOM) and contributes zero current.

\subsubsection{Colon tubes (NX5--NX6, ballasts R5--R6)}

For the IN-6, the conservative maintaining figure is \SI{90}{\volt}
(\cref{sec:in6}; the higher of the two datasheet figures is used because it
minimizes the computed current, which is the pessimistic direction for verifying the
lower current bound). With the permissible anode-current window
\SIrange{0.6}{0.85}{\milli\ampere}:
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{drop}} &= 170 - 90 - 2.5 = \SI{77.5}{\volt}, \\
  R_A &\in \left[\frac{\SI{77.5}{\volt}}{\SI{0.85}{\milli\ampere}},\;
               \frac{\SI{77.5}{\volt}}{\SI{0.6}{\milli\ampere}}\right]
       = [\SI{91.2}{\kilo\ohm},\, \SI{129}{\kilo\ohm}].
\end{align*}
The populated \SI{120}{\kilo\ohm} lies inside the band:
$I = \SIrange{0.646}{0.658}{\milli\ampere}$ depending on the assumed driver drop,
comfortably above the \SI{0.25}{\milli\ampere} minimum indication current. At
\SI{180}{\volt} the band shifts to \SIrange{103}{146}{\kilo\ohm} and the populated
value still falls inside it ($I = \SIrange{0.73}{0.74}{\milli\ampere}$): unlike the
digit ballasts, the colon ballasts tolerate the whole recommended trim range.
Match.

\subsection{Output setpoint, trim range, and ignition margin}\label{sec:hv-setpoint}

\subsubsection{Feedback divider and trim range}

The divider runs $+170\,\mathrm{V} \rightarrow R203\,(\SI{866}{\kilo\ohm})
\rightarrow R204\,(\SI{866}{\kilo\ohm}) \rightarrow \mathrm{FB} \rightarrow
R205\,(\SI{8.87}{\kilo\ohm}) \rightarrow RV201 \rightarrow \mathrm{GND}$, with
RV201 (Bourns 3296W, \SI{2}{\kilo\ohm}) in rheostat connection (wiper and one end
grounded), contributing \SIrange{0}{2}{\kilo\ohm}. With
$V_{\mathrm{REF}} = \SI{1.00}{\volt}$ (\cref{sec:lm5155}):
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{out}} &= V_{\mathrm{REF}}
    \left(1 + \frac{R_{203} + R_{204}}{R_{205} + R_{V}}\right)
    = 1 + \frac{1732}{8.87 + R_{V}[\si{\kilo\ohm}]}\ \si{\volt},\\[2pt]
  V_{\mathrm{out}}(R_V = 0)
    &= 1 + \frac{1732}{8.87} = \SI{196.3}{\volt} \quad\text{(maximum)},\\
  V_{\mathrm{out}}(R_V = \SI{2}{\kilo\ohm})
    &= 1 + \frac{1732}{10.87} = \SI{160.3}{\volt} \quad\text{(minimum)}.
\end{align*}
The \SI{170}{\volt} nameplate sits at $R_V = 1732/169 - 8.87 = \SI{1.38}{\kilo\ohm}$
(69\,\% of travel) and \SI{180}{\volt} at $R_V = \SI{0.81}{\kilo\ohm}$ (40\,\%).
Local sensitivity at mid-range is $\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}R_V =
1732/R_B^2 \approx \SI{16.5}{\volt}$ per \si{\kilo\ohm}; the 25-turn element gives
roughly \SI{1.3}{\volt} per turn --- adequate resolution for setting the rail to
within about $\pm\SI{1}{\volt}$. Divider current is
$(V_{\mathrm{out}}-1)/\SI{1.732}{\mega\ohm} \approx \SI{98}{\micro\ampere}$, large
against FB input bias, small against the load.

Static tolerance (1\,\% resistors, $\pm1\,\%$ reference) shifts the endpoints by
roughly $\pm2.7\,\%$ worst case, but the trimmer calibrates all static error out;
what tolerance does affect is range coverage. Even at the unfavorable corner the
achievable maximum exceeds \SI{190}{\volt} and the achievable minimum is below
\SI{165}{\volt}, so the range \SIrange{170}{185}{\volt} needed below is covered under
all tolerance combinations. Post-trim setpoint uncertainty reduces to the reference
tolerance and drift, about $\pm\SI{2}{\volt}$, plus trim granularity
$\approx\pm\SI{0.7}{\volt}$.

\subsubsection{Divider element stress}

Pre-strike leakage aside, R203 and R204 split $(V_{\mathrm{out}}-1)$ evenly:
\SI{84.5}{\volt} each at \SI{170}{\volt}, \SI{97.7}{\volt} each at the
\SI{196.3}{\volt} trim ceiling. The populated parts are DIN0207 axial metal film,
whose standard limiting element voltage is \SI{250}{\volt} (assumption: generic
DIN0207 rating; not recorded in the schematic). Margin exceeds $2.5\times$ even at
the trim ceiling, and dissipation is
$97.7^2/\SI{866}{\kilo\ohm} = \SI{11}{\milli\watt}$. The two-resistor split is
therefore not strictly required for voltage rating with these parts, but it keeps
each element far from its limit and is retained. R205 and RV201 see about
\SI{1}{\volt}. Match.

\subsubsection{Ignition margin}\label{sec:hv-ignition}

Before ignition a tube draws no current, so no voltage drops across ballast or
driver and the full rail appears across the selected gap. The IN-12B ignition
voltage is guaranteed $\le\SI{170}{\volt}$ (\cref{sec:in12b}; this verified figure
supersedes the earlier unsourced $\sim$\SI{200}{\volt} aged-tube assumption in the
project notes). The IN-6 strikes at $\le\SI{140}{\volt}$ and is never limiting.

A setpoint of exactly \SI{170}{\volt} therefore provides \emph{zero} margin against
a worst-case tube, and negative margin once post-trim setpoint uncertainty
($\approx\pm\SI{2}{\volt}$) is counted. The required margin above the
\SI{170}{\volt} guarantee is built from identifiable terms rather than asserted:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item post-trim setpoint uncertainty and drift: $\pm\SI{2}{\volt}$, plus trim and
    measurement granularity $\approx\SI{1}{\volt}$;
  \item strike-voltage growth over life (cathode sputtering, gas cleanup) and the
    dark effect (statistical ignition delay without ambient photoionization): not
    quantified by the IN-12B datasheet; an allowance of \SI{5}{\volt} is adopted,
    consistent with the widely used hobbyist rule of thumb of roughly \SI{10}{\volt}
    total margin, which this budget approximately reproduces rather than assumes;
  \item failure mode if marginal: a digit intermittently failing to strike at cold
    power-on in darkness --- annoying and hard to reproduce, which argues for
    covering the unquantified terms generously.
\end{itemize}
Total: $\ge\SI{8}{\volt}$, rounded to \SI{10}{\volt}. The setpoint should be
\SI{180}{\volt}, which the derived trim range reaches with \SI{16}{\volt} to spare.

\textbf{Flag (principal tension of this section).} At \SI{180}{\volt} the digit
ballast current becomes
\begin{align*}
  I_K = \frac{180 - 145 - V_K}{\SI{8.2}{\kilo\ohm}}
      = \SIrange{3.96}{4.15}{\milli\ampere}
  \quad (V_K = \SIrange{2.5}{1.0}{\volt}),
\end{align*}
exceeding the \SI{3.5}{\milli\ampere} DC maximum of \cref{sec:in12b}. The populated
\SI{8.2}{\kilo\ohm} and the ignition-margin requirement are mutually inconsistent:
either the rail stays near \SI{170}{\volt} (correct tube current, no worst-case
ignition margin) or it moves to \SI{180}{\volt} (correct margin, over-current
digits). The intermediate \SI{175}{\volt} gives only \SI{5}{\volt} of margin while
already grazing the current limit ($\SIrange{3.35}{3.54}{\milli\ampere}$). The
consistent resolution is $R1$--$R4 \rightarrow$ \SIrange{10}{11}{\kilo\ohm} with the
rail trimmed to \SI{180}{\volt}, giving \SIrange{2.9}{3.3}{\milli\ampere} and full
margin; the colon ballasts need no change. This is a Rev-B value change, not a
topology change. Until then, the practical mitigation is to trim to
\SI{175}{\volt} and accept reduced margin, on the argument that typical (not
worst-case) IN-12B ignition sits well below \SI{170}{\volt}.

\subsubsection{The datasheet's 200/250~V anode-supply figures}

The IN-12B datasheet separately states a minimum anode supply of \SI{200}{\volt} and
a recommended \SI{250}{\volt}~DC, both above this design's rail. The working
hypothesis of \cref{sec:in12b} is that these figures serve the tube's other
specified supply mode --- unfiltered half-wave-rectified AC --- in which the tube
must re-ignite every mains cycle and conducts only near the crest, so the supply
must carry substantial peak headroom above the ignition voltage merely to achieve a
usable conduction angle and average brightness. A regulated, continuously present DC
rail faces neither constraint: ignition happens once per power-up, and the operating
point is fixed by the ballast. On that reading, the physically grounded DC
requirements are exactly the ones derived above --- guaranteed strike
($\ge V_{\mathrm{ign,max}}$ plus margin) and cathode current inside the specified
band --- both of which a \SI{180}{\volt} rail satisfies. This interpretation is not
confirmed by the source document, and is carried as an accepted-with-rationale
assumption: if the \SI{200}{\volt} minimum in fact encodes an additional DC-mode
requirement, the design is relying on the verified $\le\SI{170}{\volt}$ ignition
line instead of it. The off-state cathode-voltage exposure of the unselected
K155ID1 outputs at this rail voltage was analyzed and accepted in
\cref{sec:bcd-voltage-risk}; the rail range derived here
(\SIrange{170}{180}{\volt}) is within the envelope that acceptance was conditioned
on.

\subsection{Switching frequency and RT}\label{sec:hv-fsw}

For the given \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz} target (an aesthetic-driven choice, accepted as
input), the RT equation of \cref{sec:lm5155} gives
\begin{align*}
  R_T &= \frac{2.21\times10^{10}}{f_{RT}} - 955
       = \frac{2.21\times10^{10}}{1.5\times10^{5}} - 955
       = \SI{146.4}{\kilo\ohm}.
\end{align*}
The nearest E96 value is the populated $R209 = \SI{147}{\kilo\ohm}$, giving
\begin{align*}
  f_{RT} &= \frac{2.21\times10^{10}}{147\,000 + 955} = \SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz}
\end{align*}
($-0.4\,\%$ from target; period $T = \SI{6.695}{\micro\second}$). All subsequent
arithmetic uses \SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz}. Match.

The DA2032-AL is characterized only at a \SI{100}{\kilo\hertz} test point
(\cref{sec:da2032}); operating it at \SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz} is an extrapolation.
For a DCM flyback the inductance and saturation figures translate directly, but
core loss at the higher frequency is uncharacterized; it is carried below as an
explicit allowance, and this extrapolation is listed as an assumption in the
summary.

\subsection{Operating point and efficiency}\label{sec:hv-op}

In DCM the converter transfers a fixed packet of magnetizing energy each cycle:
\begin{align*}
  P_{\mathrm{in}} = \tfrac{1}{2} L_p I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  I_{\mathrm{pk}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 P_{\mathrm{in}}}{L_p f_{\mathrm{sw}}}},
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{in}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{out}}}{\eta}.
\end{align*}
Efficiency is not assumed; it is iterated: guess $\eta$, compute $P_{\mathrm{in}}$
and $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$, evaluate each loss mechanism, recompute $\eta$, repeat to
convergence. The loss models and their inputs:
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[Primary conduction] Triangular current over $t_{\mathrm{on}}$:
    $I_{\mathrm{rms}} = I_{\mathrm{pk}}\sqrt{D/3}$ through
    $R_{DS(\mathrm{on})} + R_{201} + R_{\mathrm{DCR,pri}}
     = 55 + 39 + 13 = \SI{107}{\milli\ohm}$.
    IRL540N on-resistance is specified \SI{53}{\milli\ohm} maximum at
    $V_{GS}=\SI{5}{\volt}$; \SI{55}{\milli\ohm} is used as a warm estimate at the
    \SI{6.85}{\volt} gate rail (commodity-part typical figures; assumption).
  \item[Turn-off overlap] The dominant and least certain term. The IRL540N is a
    large TO-220 die ($Q_{GD}\approx\SI{25}{\nano\coulomb}$ typical) discharged
    through $R211=\SI{10}{\ohm}$ plus driver resistance at a Miller plateau near
    \SI{2}{\volt}, so the voltage-transition interval is tens to $\sim$200
    nanoseconds depending on which capacitance-versus-voltage regime dominates. An
    effective overlap $t_{\mathrm{ovl}} = \SI{100}{\nano\second}$ is adopted, with
    the commutation voltage $V_{\mathrm{com}} = V_{\mathrm{BUS}} +
    V_{\mathrm{refl}}$:
    $E_{\mathrm{off}} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} I_{\mathrm{pk}} V_{\mathrm{com}}
    t_{\mathrm{ovl}}$. \textbf{This estimate is uncertain by a factor of
    $\sim$2 either way} and dominates the efficiency error bar. Turn-on is
    near-lossless in DCM (zero current; $C_{oss}$ discharge from the post-ring
    drain voltage contributes only a few milliwatts).
  \item[Gate drive and controller] $P = (Q_G f_{\mathrm{sw}} + I_Q) V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$
    with $Q_G \approx \SI{40}{\nano\coulomb}$ at the \SI{6.85}{\volt} VCC rail
    (scaled from the \SI{10}{\volt} datasheet point; assumption) and controller
    operating current $I_Q \le \SI{480}{\micro\ampere}$ (\ds{lm5155.pdf}{1}). Gate
    charge is drawn through the internal VCC regulator from BIAS
    ($=V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$), so the full input voltage is charged to this loss.
  \item[RCD clamp] Leakage energy $\tfrac{1}{2} L_{\mathrm{lk}} I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2$
    per cycle, dissipated in R212 with the clamp-voltage multiplier derived in
    \cref{sec:hv-clamp}.
  \item[Rectifier] $V_F \approx \SI{1.0}{\volt}$ (ultrafast-diode class figure at
    $\sim$\SI{0.2}{\ampere}) times $I_{\mathrm{out}}$; recovery loss is negligible
    because in DCM the secondary current reaches zero before the switch turns on.
  \item[Secondary conduction] $I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}^2 \times \SI{1.60}{\ohm}$ DCR.
  \item[Capacitor ESR] Input ($\approx\SI{30}{\milli\ohm}$ polymer bulk plus
    ceramics) and output ($\approx\SI{5}{\ohm}$ small high-voltage electrolytic)
    ESR estimates; both small.
  \item[Core] Uncharacterized at this frequency; a \SI{50}{\milli\watt} allowance
    is carried (assumption, flagged).
\end{description}

Iterating at the design-max envelope ($P_{\mathrm{out}} = \SI{2.91}{\watt}$,
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = \SI{8.5}{\volt}$, $V_{\mathrm{out}} = \SI{180}{\volt}$,
reflected voltage $V_{\mathrm{refl}} = (180+1)/10 = \SI{18.1}{\volt}$), from a seed
of $\eta_0 = 0.85$ the loop converges in three passes to $\eta = 0.796$. The
converged cycle:
\begin{align*}
  P_{\mathrm{in}} &= \frac{2.914}{0.796} = \SI{3.66}{\watt}, &
  E_{\mathrm{cyc}} &= \frac{3.66}{149.4\times10^{3}} = \SI{24.5}{\micro\joule},\\
  I_{\mathrm{pk}} &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 24.5\,\si{\micro\joule}}
                                 {\SI{10}{\micro\henry}}} = \SI{2.21}{\ampere}, &
  t_{\mathrm{on}} &= \frac{L_p I_{\mathrm{pk}}}{V_{\mathrm{BUS}}}
                   = \frac{10\,\si{\micro\henry} \times 2.21}{8.5}
                   = \SI{2.61}{\micro\second},\\
  D &= t_{\mathrm{on}} f_{\mathrm{sw}} = 0.39, &
  I_{\mathrm{rms,pri}} &= I_{\mathrm{pk}}\sqrt{D/3} = \SI{0.80}{\ampere}.
\end{align*}
Converged loss budget at this point:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
Mechanism & Loss \\
\midrule
MOSFET turn-off overlap ($t_{\mathrm{ovl}}=\SI{100}{\nano\second}$) & \SI{440}{\milli\watt} \\
RCD clamp (R212) & \SI{88}{\milli\watt} \\
Primary conduction (FET 35, R201 25, DCR 8) & \SI{68}{\milli\watt} \\
Gate drive + controller & \SI{55}{\milli\watt} \\
Core allowance (uncharacterized) & \SI{50}{\milli\watt} \\
Capacitor ESR (input + output) & \SI{24}{\milli\watt} \\
Rectifier D201 conduction & \SI{16}{\milli\watt} \\
Secondary DCR & \SI{5}{\milli\watt} \\
\midrule
Total & \SI{746}{\milli\watt} \\
$\eta = 2.914/(2.914+0.746)$ & $0.796$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The same procedure at the nominal point ($\SI{2.29}{\watt}$, \SI{9}{\volt},
\SI{170}{\volt}) converges to $\eta = 0.78$, $P_{\mathrm{in}} = \SI{2.94}{\watt}$,
$E_{\mathrm{cyc}} = \SI{19.7}{\micro\joule}$, $I_{\mathrm{pk}} = \SI{1.99}{\ampere}$,
$I_{\mathrm{in}} = \SI{0.33}{\ampere}$ average. Nominal efficiency is slightly
\emph{lower} than at full load because the dominant turn-off loss scales as
$I_{\mathrm{pk}} \propto \sqrt{P}$, not $P$.

Two observations. First, the prior project note assumed 85\,\%; the converged figure
is \SIrange{78}{80}{\percent}, and the difference is almost entirely the turn-off
overlap of a large, slowly discharged gate --- a direct consequence of the
through-hole IRL540N plus \SI{10}{\ohm} $R211$ at \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz}. Second,
because $t_{\mathrm{ovl}}$ carries a factor-2 uncertainty, the honest efficiency
band is roughly \SIrange{72}{85}{\percent}; every derived quantity downstream
(notably peak current and current-limit margin) is evaluated at the pessimistic end
where it matters. A bench efficiency measurement would collapse this band and is
the single most informative measurement to make on this converter.

With the minimum-tolerance inductance ($L_p = \SI{9}{\micro\henry}$ at rated peak
current, \cref{sec:da2032}), the same energy packet requires
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(L\min)} &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 24.5\,\si{\micro\joule}}
                                          {\SI{9}{\micro\henry}}}
                             = \SI{2.33}{\ampere},
\end{align*}
the worst-case commanded peak used in \cref{sec:hv-cs}.

\subsection{DCM verification}\label{sec:hv-dcm}

DCM requires $t_{\mathrm{on}} + t_{\mathrm{dem}} < T$ with margin for the drain
ring-down. The demagnetization time, referred through the 1:10 ratio, is
\begin{align*}
  t_{\mathrm{dem}} &= \frac{n L_p I_{\mathrm{pk}}}{V_{\mathrm{out}} + V_F}.
\end{align*}
The binding corner is the one that maximizes $t_{\mathrm{on}} + t_{\mathrm{dem}}$:
maximum inductance ($\SI{11}{\micro\henry}$, $+10\,\%$), minimum input
(\SI{8.5}{\volt}), design-max load. There
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{pk}} &= \sqrt{\frac{2\times24.5\,\si{\micro\joule}}{\SI{11}{\micro\henry}}}
                   = \SI{2.11}{\ampere},\\
  t_{\mathrm{on}} &= \frac{11\,\si{\micro\henry}\times 2.11}{8.5}
                   = \SI{2.73}{\micro\second}, &
  t_{\mathrm{dem}} &= \frac{10 \times 11\,\si{\micro\henry} \times 2.11}{181}
                   = \SI{1.28}{\micro\second},\\
  t_{\mathrm{on}} + t_{\mathrm{dem}} &= \SI{4.01}{\micro\second}
    = 0.60\,T, &
  t_{\mathrm{idle}} &= \SI{2.68}{\micro\second}.
\end{align*}
DCM holds with a 40\,\% idle fraction at the worst corner; at \SI{13}{\volt} input
or lighter load the margin only grows (at the minimum trim setpoint the load the
ballasts can draw falls faster than the demagnetization slope degrades). The duty
cycle never exceeds 0.41, far under the controller's $\approx$0.91 maximum near
this frequency, and $t_{\mathrm{on}} \ge \SI{2.6}{\micro\second}$ is more than an
order of magnitude above the $\approx\SI{165}{\nano\second}$ minimum on-time
(\cref{sec:lm5155}). Since $D < 0.5$ in DCM everywhere, no slope compensation is
required for stability, consistent with the omission of $R_{SL}$
(\cref{sec:hv-cs}). Verified.

\subsection{Current sensing and current limit}\label{sec:hv-cs}

\subsubsection{Sense resistor and limit thresholds}

R201 (\SI{39}{\milli\ohm}, 1\,\%, 2512) carries the switch current to ground; the
CS pin taps it through $R213 = \SI{100}{\ohm}$ with $C210 = \SI{470}{\pico\farad}$
to ground. With the \SI{100}{\milli\volt} (93--107) current-limit threshold of
\cref{sec:lm5155} and no $R_{SL}$ populated (confirmed against the netlist: only
R213 sits between the sense node and the CS pin, and the current-limit comparator's
threshold is then duty-independent, which is TI's own recommendation for accurate
limiting):
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\mathrm{typ}} &= \frac{\SI{100}{\milli\volt}}{\SI{39}{\milli\ohm}}
    = \SI{2.56}{\ampere},\\
  I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\min} &= \frac{\SI{93}{\milli\volt}}{39\,\si{\milli\ohm}\times1.01}
    = \SI{2.36}{\ampere}, &
  I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\max} &= \frac{\SI{107}{\milli\volt}}{39\,\si{\milli\ohm}\times0.99}
    = \SI{2.77}{\ampere}.
\end{align*}

The sense resistor must satisfy two opposing constraints:
\begin{align*}
  \text{power delivery:}\quad
    R_{S} &< \frac{\SI{93}{\milli\volt}}{I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(L\min)}}
          = \frac{93\,\si{\milli\volt}}{2.33\,\si{\ampere}} = \SI{39.9}{\milli\ohm},\\
  \text{core protection:}\quad
    R_{S} &> \frac{\SI{107}{\milli\volt}}{I_{\mathrm{sat}} - \Delta I_{\mathrm{ovs}}}
          = \frac{107\,\si{\milli\volt}}{3.0 - 0.14\,\si{\ampere}} = \SI{37.4}{\milli\ohm},
\end{align*}
where $\Delta I_{\mathrm{ovs}}$ is the post-threshold overshoot estimated below.
The admissible window is \SIrange{37.4}{39.9}{\milli\ohm}; the populated
\SI{39}{\milli\ohm} is essentially the only standard value inside it. Match --- but
with thin margin on both sides:

\textbf{Flag (current-limit margin).} At the fully stacked pessimistic corner
(design-max load, \SI{8.5}{\volt} input, converged $\eta$, $L_p$ at $-10\,\%$,
threshold at minimum, R201 at $+1\,\%$) the required peak \SI{2.33}{\ampere} sits
$1.2\,\%$ below the minimum limit \SI{2.36}{\ampere}: the converter would graze
its cycle-by-cycle limit and the rail would sag slightly at that corner. The load
envelope is deliberately unreachable in practice (expected-load requirement is
\SI{2.08}{\ampere} at the same corner, a 14\,\% margin), so this is a
documentation-grade flag rather than a fault, but it should be re-examined if the
load ever grows (e.g.\ decimal points wired in a later revision). On the other
side, the maximum clamped peak plus overshoot reaches
$2.77 + 0.14 = \SI{2.91}{\ampere}$ against the \SI{3.0}{\ampere} transformer
rating --- a 3\,\% margin, acceptable only because the \SI{3.0}{\ampere} figure is
a soft-saturation point ($L$ already specified at \SI{9}{\micro\henry} there), not
a cliff.

\subsubsection{CS filter}

$\tau = R_{213} C_{210} = 100 \times 470\,\si{\pico\farad} = \SI{47}{\nano\second}$.
Against the controller's criteria (\ds{lm5155.pdf}{21}): the limit is valid for
on-times above $2 R_F C_F = \SI{94}{\nano\second}$, and the operating
$t_{\mathrm{on}} \ge \SI{2.6}{\micro\second}$ exceeds that by $28\times$; the
off-time discharge criterion $(1-D)/f_{\mathrm{sw}} > 3 R_F C_F$ gives
$\SI{3.9}{\micro\second} \gg \SI{141}{\nano\second}$; and both component values sit
inside TI's recommended ranges (\SIrange{10}{200}{\ohm},
\SIrange{100}{2000}{\pico\farad}). Functionally, \SI{47}{\nano\second} attenuates
the leading-edge spike (gate current and $C_{oss}$ discharge returning through the
sense resistor, duration tens of nanoseconds) while adding only
$\Delta I_{\mathrm{delay}} = (V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/L_p)\,\tau \approx
0.85\,\si{\ampere\per\micro\second} \times \SI{47}{\nano\second} \approx
\SI{40}{\milli\ampere}$ of effective limit shift --- under half the threshold
tolerance band. Combined with an assumed \SI{50}{\nano\second} comparator/driver
delay, total overshoot past the threshold at the fastest slope
(\SI{13}{\volt}, \SI{9}{\micro\henry}: \SI{1.44}{\ampere\per\micro\second}) is
$\Delta I_{\mathrm{ovs}} \approx \SI{0.14}{\ampere}$, the figure used above.
The filter is neither too slow (limit distortion) nor pointless (spike passes).
Match.

R201 dissipation is $I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2 R_S = 0.80^2 \times 0.039 =
\SI{25}{\milli\watt}$, trivial in a 2512.

\subsection{Switch (Q201) stress and losses}\label{sec:hv-switch}

Drain stress is set by the clamp, not the rating-free ring:
\begin{align*}
  V_{DS,\mathrm{pk}} &= V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}} + V_{\mathrm{cl}} + V_{F(D202)}
    \approx 13 + 48.6 + 0.9 \approx \SI{63}{\volt}
\end{align*}
(clamp voltage from \cref{sec:hv-clamp}, which is load- but not line-dependent
because $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$ in DCM is line-independent). Against the IRL540N's
\SI{100}{\volt} rating: 37\,\% margin, comfortable, and the part is
avalanche-rated besides. Peak current \SIrange{2.2}{2.9}{\ampere} against
\SI{36}{\ampere}: unstressed. The \SI{6.85}{\volt} VCC gate rail fully enhances a
logic-level gate specified at \SI{5}{\volt}; $V_{GS}$ rating ($\pm\SI{16}{\volt}$)
is untouched.

Total switch dissipation at design-max: conduction \SI{35}{\milli\watt} + turn-off
\SI{440}{\milli\watt} + $C_{oss}$ few \si{\milli\watt} $\approx \SI{0.48}{\watt}$.
A free-standing vertical TO-220 at $\theta_{JA} \approx \SI{62}{\kelvin\per\watt}$
rises $\approx\SI{30}{\kelvin}$ --- warm to the touch, thermally unremarkable.

\textbf{Flag (efficiency lever, not a fault).} The turn-off overlap term is the
single largest loss in the converter and is set almost entirely by $R211 =
\SI{10}{\ohm}$ discharging a $Q_{GD}\approx\SI{25}{\nano\coulomb}$ gate at a
$\approx\SI{2}{\volt}$ plateau. If Rev B wants the efficiency back, an
anti-parallel turn-off diode across R211 (or splitting R211 into separate
source/sink paths) would roughly halve the dominant loss at the cost of one small
part; the present network is functional and thermally safe as populated.

\subsection{Output rectifier (D201)}\label{sec:hv-rect}

Reverse stress occurs during the on-time:
\begin{align*}
  V_R &= V_{\mathrm{out}} + n V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}}
       = 196.3 + 10 \times 13 = \SI{326}{\volt}
\end{align*}
at the trim ceiling, plus secondary-side ring --- under \SI{350}{\volt} against
the UF4005's \SI{600}{\volt}: margin $\ge 42\,\%$. Forward stress:
$I_{F,\mathrm{avg}} = \SI{16}{\milli\ampere}$ and
$I_{F,\mathrm{pk}} = I_{\mathrm{pk}}/n = \SI{0.22}{\ampere}$ against a
\SI{1}{\ampere} average rating. Conduction loss $\approx V_F I_{\mathrm{out}}
= \SI{16}{\milli\watt}$. The $\approx\SI{75}{\nano\second}$ recovery is immaterial:
in DCM the diode current reaches zero, and the junction sweeps out, during the idle
interval, so the switch never hard-commutates it. The generic-ultrafast class is
exactly right for this position; a Schottky is unavailable at this voltage and
unnecessary at this current. Match.

\subsection{RCD clamp}\label{sec:hv-clamp}

At turn-off the leakage inductance ($L_{\mathrm{lk}} \le \SI{0.150}{\micro\henry}$,
\cref{sec:da2032}) carries $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$ and must be absorbed. D202 routes it
into C209 (\SI{10}{\nano\farad}) $\parallel$ R212 (\SI{27}{\kilo\ohm}), the pair
returned to $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ rather than ground --- so the capacitor stands off
only the clamp voltage $V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ (not $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} + V_{\mathrm{cl}}$)
and the resistor's discharge current is recycled into the input rail rather than
dumped to ground.

Per cycle, at the design-max point:
\begin{align*}
  E_{\mathrm{lk}} &= \tfrac{1}{2} L_{\mathrm{lk}} I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2
    = \tfrac{1}{2}\times\SI{0.15}{\micro\henry}\times 2.21^2
    = \SI{0.37}{\micro\joule},
  &
  E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}} &= \SI{55}{\milli\watt}.
\end{align*}
While D202 conducts, the transformer keeps feeding the clamp from the magnetizing
current, so the clamp absorbs more than the bare leakage energy by the standard
multiplier $V_{\mathrm{cl}}/(V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}})$. Equating clamp
input to R212 dissipation $V_{\mathrm{cl}}^2/R_{212}$:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{V_{\mathrm{cl}}^2}{R_{212}}
    &= E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}}
       \frac{V_{\mathrm{cl}}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}}
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  V_{\mathrm{cl}}^2 - V_{\mathrm{refl}} V_{\mathrm{cl}}
    - E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}} R_{212} = 0,\\
  V_{\mathrm{cl}} &= \frac{18.1 + \sqrt{18.1^2 + 4 \times 0.055 \times 27\,000}}{2}
    = \SI{48.6}{\volt}.
\end{align*}
This is $2.7\times$ the reflected voltage --- inside the conventional
$2$--$3\times$ band where the clamp absorbs the leakage spike without continuously
loading the flyback interval, and it independently reproduces the prior design
note's ``about \SI{48}{\volt} worst case'' estimate. Checks:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item R212 dissipation $48.6^2/27\,\si{\kilo\ohm} = \SI{88}{\milli\watt}$,
    15\,\% of a DIN0207's \SI{0.6}{\watt}; steady \SI{49}{\volt} across it is far
    under the element's \SI{250}{\volt}-class rating.
  \item C209 per-cycle ripple $E_{\mathrm{lk}}/(C_{209} V_{\mathrm{cl}}) =
    \SI{0.76}{\volt}$ (1.6\,\%), and $\tau = R_{212} C_{209} =
    \SI{270}{\micro\second} = 40$ switching periods: the clamp is a quiet
    quasi-DC node, as intended. Any $\ge\SI{100}{\volt}$ dielectric suffices
    (rating not recorded in the schematic; verify at BOM release).
  \item D202 sees reverse voltage $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} + V_{\mathrm{cl}} \approx
    \SI{62}{\volt}$ during the on-time and carries brief \SI{2.2}{\ampere} pulses,
    both trivial for a UF4005; its \SI{75}{\nano\second} recovery is fast on the
    \SI{270}{\micro\second} clamp time scale.
\end{itemize}
The earlier prose note specified ``a fast \SI{100}{\volt} diode'' here; the
populated part is the same \SI{600}{\volt} UF4005 as D201. A \SI{600}{\volt}
ultrafast part strictly dominates the \SI{100}{\volt} requirement (its higher
forward drop costs nothing measurable at \SI{55}{\milli\watt} scale), and sharing
the line item with D201 is sound BOM practice. Deliberate substitution, verified
adequate --- not an error. Match.

\subsection{Bleeder (R202) and minimum load}\label{sec:hv-bleeder}

R202 (\SI{470}{\kilo\ohm} across the rail) serves two derivable functions.

\textbf{Minimum load.} The controller cannot deliver less than one minimum-width
pulse per cycle. At \SI{13}{\volt} input and the $\approx\SI{165}{\nano\second}$
minimum on-time:
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{\min} &= \frac{V_{\mathrm{BUS}} t_{\mathrm{on,min}}}{L_p}
    = \frac{13 \times 165\,\si{\nano\second}}{\SI{10}{\micro\henry}}
    = \SI{0.21}{\ampere},\\
  P_{\min} &= \tfrac{1}{2} L_p (I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{\min})^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}\,\eta
    \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\times\SI{10}{\micro\henry}\times 0.046 \times
      149.4\,\si{\kilo\hertz} \times 0.79
    \approx \SI{27}{\milli\watt}.
\end{align*}
Below this load the output would creep upward (or force pulse skipping). The fixed
preload --- bleeder $170^2/\SI{470}{\kilo\ohm} = \SI{61}{\milli\watt}$ plus divider
\SI{17}{\milli\watt} --- totals \SI{78}{\milli\watt}, a $2.9\times$ margin over
$P_{\min}$. The rail therefore stays regulated even before the tubes strike (during
start-up the tubes draw nothing until the rail approaches ignition). Match.

\textbf{Discharge.} Stored energy is modest,
$\tfrac{1}{2} C_{\mathrm{out}} V^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\times\SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}\times
170^2 = \SI{69}{\milli\joule}$, but the node must still bleed down after
shutdown. The tubes themselves do most of the work: they hold the rail near their
maintaining voltages while conducting, collapsing it to $\approx\SI{148}{\volt}$
(digit extinction) in $\sim$\SI{10}{\milli\second} and to $\approx\SI{92}{\volt}$
(colon extinction) in $\sim$\SI{0.2}{\second}. Below that only the resistive path
remains: $R_{202} \parallel R_{\mathrm{div}} = 470\,\si{\kilo\ohm} \parallel
1.74\,\si{\mega\ohm} = \SI{370}{\kilo\ohm}$, so
$\tau = 370\,\si{\kilo\ohm} \times \SI{4.8}{\micro\farad} = \SI{1.8}{\second}$:
\begin{align*}
  t_{92\rightarrow 60\,\mathrm{V}} = \tau \ln\frac{92}{60} = \SI{0.8}{\second},
  \qquad
  t_{92\rightarrow 42\,\mathrm{V}} = \tau \ln\frac{92}{42} = \SI{1.4}{\second}.
\end{align*}
The node is below \SI{60}{\volt} about one second after the colons go out, and
below \SI{42}{\volt} in under three seconds total --- acceptable for a bench
board with exposed high-voltage nodes. Dissipation \SI{61}{\milli\watt}
(\SI{82}{\milli\watt} at the trim ceiling) and \SI{170}{\volt} standing voltage are
both well inside the DIN0207 element's ratings. Match.

\subsection{Loop compensation}\label{sec:hv-comp}

\subsubsection{Plant}

A peak-current-mode DCM flyback is, to first order, a controlled current source
into $C_{\mathrm{out}} \parallel R_L$. At the nominal point $R_L =
V_{\mathrm{out}}/I_{\mathrm{out}} = 170/\SI{13.5}{\milli\ampere} =
\SI{12.6}{\kilo\ohm}$ and $C_{\mathrm{out}} = C204 + C205 = \SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}$.

Modulator gain: the controller's PWM comparator sees COMP through its internal
attenuation $G_{\mathrm{COMP}} = 0.142$ and compares it against the sensed current
plus the fixed internal slope ramp of slope $S_e = \SI{40}{\milli\volt}\times
f_{\mathrm{sw}} = \SI{5975}{\volt\per\second}$ (\ds{lm5155.pdf}{20}). Against the
sensed on-slope $S_n = R_S V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/L_p = 0.039\times9/\SI{10}{\micro\henry}
= \SI{35100}{\volt\per\second}$:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\partial I_{\mathrm{pk}}}{\partial v_c}
    &= \frac{G_{\mathrm{COMP}}}{R_S\,(1 + S_e/S_n)}
     = \frac{0.142}{0.039 \times 1.17}
     = \SI{3.11}{\ampere\per\volt}.
\end{align*}
Since $I_{\mathrm{out}} = \tfrac{1}{2} L_p I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}
\eta / V_{\mathrm{out}}$, the plant transconductance and its output resistance
follow:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\partial I_{\mathrm{out}}}{\partial I_{\mathrm{pk}}}
    &= \frac{2 I_{\mathrm{out}}}{I_{\mathrm{pk}}}
     = \frac{2 \times 13.5\,\si{\milli\ampere}}{1.99}
     = 13.6\,\si{\milli\ampere\per\ampere},\\
  g_{ps} &= 13.6\,\si{\milli\ampere\per\ampere} \times \SI{3.11}{\ampere\per\volt}
          = \SI{42.2}{\milli\siemens} \text{ (per volt of COMP)},
\end{align*}
and because $I_{\mathrm{out}} \propto 1/V_{\mathrm{out}}$ at fixed peak current,
the converter presents its own output resistance equal to $R_L$, so the loaded
plant sees $R_L/2$ and one dominant pole:
\begin{align*}
  G_0 &= g_{ps}\,\frac{R_L}{2} = 0.0422 \times 6301 = 266\ (\SI{48.5}{\decibel}),
  &
  f_p &= \frac{2}{2\pi R_L C_{\mathrm{out}}}
       = \frac{2}{2\pi \times 12.6\,\si{\kilo\ohm} \times \SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}}
       = \SI{5.3}{\hertz}.
\end{align*}
The DCM right-half-plane zero and the output capacitor's ESR zero
($\approx\SI{7}{\kilo\hertz}$ at the assumed \SI{5}{\ohm}) both lie far above the
crossover derived below and are neglected.

\subsubsection{Compensator and loop}

The populated network on COMP --- $R210 = \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm}$ in series with
$C207 = \SI{100}{\nano\farad}$, all shunted by $C208 = \SI{220}{\pico\farad}$ ---
with the \SI{2}{\milli\siemens} transconductance error amplifier
(\ds{lm5155.pdf}{7}) forms the standard Type-II response:
\begin{align*}
  f_z &= \frac{1}{2\pi R_{210} C_{207}} = \SI{159}{\hertz},
  &
  f_{hp} &= \frac{1}{2\pi R_{210}\,\frac{C_{207}C_{208}}{C_{207}+C_{208}}}
          = \SI{72.5}{\kilo\hertz},
\end{align*}
with integrator behavior below $f_z$ (the amplifier's
$g_m R_O = 2\,\si{\milli\siemens} \times 10\,\si{\mega\ohm} = \SI{86}{\decibel}$
DC gain, \ds{lm5155.pdf}{21}, makes static regulation error negligible). The divider contributes
$H = V_{\mathrm{REF}}/V_{\mathrm{out}} = 1/170$.

Solving $|T(f)| = 1$ for the product of divider, compensator, and plant
(numerically, with all three break frequencies in place) gives
\begin{align*}
  f_c &\approx \SI{210}{\hertz} \approx \frac{f_{\mathrm{sw}}}{700},\\
  \varphi_m &= 180^\circ - \arctan\frac{f_c}{f_p} - 90^\circ
    + \arctan\frac{f_c}{f_z} - \arctan\frac{f_c}{f_{hp}}
    = 180 - 88.5 - 90 + 52.4 - 0.2 \approx 54^\circ.
\end{align*}
The zero sits at $0.77 f_c$ --- the textbook placement --- and the
high-frequency pole is $345\times$ above crossover, where it scrubs switching
ripple off COMP without costing phase. Gain margin is effectively unbounded (the
phase never approaches $-180^\circ$ until far past $f_{hp}$, where loop gain is
tens of dB down). A \SI{210}{\hertz} bandwidth is slow by SMPS standards but
entirely adequate here: the load is quasi-static (no multiplexing --- each tube has
a dedicated driver, so digit changes move milliamps at seconds cadence), and the
input rail is pre-regulated USB-PD. Match: the populated network is a coherent,
correctly placed design. The modulator constants ($G_{\mathrm{COMP}}$, $S_e$,
$g_m$) are datasheet typicals without tolerance bands, so a bench transient or
Bode check remains worthwhile, but nothing in the derivation flags it as at-risk.

\subsection{Soft start}\label{sec:hv-ss}

The datasheet's soft-start formulas cover boost and SEPIC only; the boost formula
assumes an input-to-output pass-through path a flyback lacks, so neither is
force-fit here. From the pin's fundamental behavior (\cref{sec:lm5155}) --- C206 is
charged by $I_{SS} = \SI{10}{\micro\ampere}$, and the error amplifier regulates FB
to the lower of $V_{SS}$ and $V_{\mathrm{REF}}$ --- the reference-ramp time is
\begin{align*}
  t_{SS} &= \frac{C_{206}\, V_{\mathrm{REF}}}{I_{SS}}
          = \frac{47\,\si{\nano\farad} \times 1.00}{\SI{10}{\micro\ampere}}
          = \SI{4.7}{\milli\second}
  \qquad (\SIrange{4.3}{5.2}{\milli\second} \text{ over } I_{SS}
  \text{ tolerance}).
\end{align*}
The output cannot follow that ramp: tracking it would demand
$C_{\mathrm{out}}\,\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}t \approx \SI{170}{\milli\ampere}$ of
output current, an order of magnitude beyond the converter's capability. The
actual rise is power-limited at the current limit. Energy balance:
\begin{align*}
  t_{\mathrm{rise}} &\approx
  \frac{\tfrac{1}{2} C_{\mathrm{out}} V_{\mathrm{out}}^2}
       {\tfrac{1}{2} L_p I_{\mathrm{lim}}^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}\,\eta}
  = \frac{\SI{69}{\milli\joule}}{\SI{3.9}{\watt}}
  \approx \SI{18}{\milli\second},
\end{align*}
so the converter runs at its cycle-by-cycle limit for roughly
\SI{13}{\milli\second} ($\sim$2000 cycles) after the SS ramp completes, and the
rail reaches its setpoint \SIrange{20}{30}{\milli\second} after enable (the low
end of the range treats the early, deep-CCM interval optimistically). The tubes,
drawing nothing until strike, do not slow this. Two qualifications:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item This is an approximation built from the SS pin mechanism plus an energy
    balance, not a datasheet formula; the early portion of the charge (output far
    below the reflected voltage, incomplete demagnetization) transfers somewhat
    less than the DCM-limit power. \textbf{Flagged for bench verification} (scope
    the rail and the CS pin at power-up).
  \item Sustained current limiting during start-up is benign specifically because
    the populated LM5155 has no hiccup overload protection --- that feature exists
    only in the LM51551 variant (\ds{lm5155.pdf}{22}). If the hiccup variant were
    ever substituted, its 64-cycle fault counter (armed during soft start) would
    trip long before the rail arrived, and C206 would need to grow to
    $\gtrsim\SI{470}{\nano\farad}$ so that the commanded ramp, not the current
    limit, paces the start. With the LM5155 as populated, \SI{47}{\nano\farad} is
    adequate. Match, with the substitution caveat recorded.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Input UVLO}\label{sec:hv-uvlo}

R207 (\SI{143}{\kilo\ohm}) and R208 (\SI{32.4}{\kilo\ohm}) divide
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ onto the UVLO/SYNC pin (thresholds \SI{1.5}{\volt} rising /
\SI{1.45}{\volt} falling; \SI{5}{\micro\ampere} hysteresis current sourced from the
pin while enabled; \cref{sec:lm5155}):
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{on}} &= 1.5 \times \frac{143 + 32.4}{32.4} = 1.5 \times 5.414
                  = \SI{8.12}{\volt},\\
  V_{\mathrm{off}} &= \left(1.45 - I_{\mathrm{hys}}
      (R_{207}\!\parallel\!R_{208})\right)\times 5.414
    = (1.45 - 5\,\si{\micro\ampere}\times 26.4\,\si{\kilo\ohm}) \times 5.414
    = \SI{7.14}{\volt},
\end{align*}
with $V_{\mathrm{off}}$ spanning \SIrange{6.99}{7.28}{\volt} over the
\SIrange{4}{6}{\micro\ampere} hysteresis-current range: about \SI{1}{\volt} of
hysteresis. The rising threshold lands on the intended $\approx\SI{8.1}{\volt}$
enable point, \SI{0.38}{\volt} below the \SI{8.5}{\volt} minimum operating input
(and below a sagging \SI{9}{\volt} USB-PD contract at its $-5\,\%$ limit,
\SI{8.55}{\volt}), so the converter is guaranteed enabled across the specified
window and disabled before the input falls into duty-cycle-starved territory.
Match.

The same pin carries the separate, lower EN comparator
($\approx$\SI{0.52}{\volt} rising). Through this divider that corresponds to
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = 0.52 \times 5.414 = \SI{2.8}{\volt}$: on any monotonic input
ramp EN is satisfied \SI{5.3}{\volt} before UVLO releases, so the single populated
divider serves both comparators and no separate EN network is needed. Verified.

\subsection{Controller housekeeping (BIAS, VCC, PGOOD)}\label{sec:hv-house}

BIAS ties to $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ (\SIrange{8.5}{13}{\volt}), inside the
\SIrange{3.5}{45}{\volt} recommended range with a $3.5\times$ margin to the
\SI{50}{\volt} absolute maximum. C201 (\SI{2.2}{\micro\farad}) is the VCC
regulator's output capacitor, inside the datasheet's
\SIrange{1}{4.7}{\micro\farad} recommendation (\ds{lm5155.pdf}{14}); C211 (\SI{1}{\micro\farad}) is
local BIAS decoupling. Match.

PGOOD is pulled up by R206 (\SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}) to the +3V3 logic rail. Against
the pin's own ratings (\cref{sec:lm5155}): \SI{3.3}{\volt} is far under the
\SI{18}{\volt} absolute maximum (and under $V_{\mathrm{BIAS}}+\SI{0.3}{\volt}$,
relevant because of the pin's internal diode to BIAS, \ds{lm5155.pdf}{22}); asserted-low sink current
is $3.3/\SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}=\SI{33}{\micro\ampere}$, well under the
\SI{1}{\milli\ampere} limit; and \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm} satisfies the
$\ge\SI{10}{\kilo\ohm}$ recommendation. Which upstream rail PGOOD should report to
is an MCU-integration decision outside this section's scope; the pin-level
electrical check passes. Match.

\subsection{Input and output capacitance}\label{sec:hv-caps}

\subsubsection{Input}

Roles, per standard flyback practice: C202 (\SI{100}{\micro\farad}, radial
polarized --- the repository's KEMET A759 polymer datasheet is presumed to
correspond to this part; assumption, as the schematic records no series or rating)
is the bulk reservoir for the pulsed primary current; C203
(\SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} ceramic) closes the high-frequency primary loop; C211
serves the BIAS pin as above. The primary draws triangular pulses of RMS
\SI{0.80}{\ampere} against a \SI{0.43}{\ampere} DC average, leaving
$\sqrt{0.80^2 - 0.43^2} = \SI{0.67}{\ampere}$ RMS of ripple in the capacitor bank.
A polymer part absorbs this trivially (multi-ampere ripple ratings at
milliohm ESR); \textbf{if C202 is instead a general-purpose aluminum electrolytic,
its \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz} ripple rating (typically \SIrange{0.3}{0.5}{\ampere}
for a small \SI{100}{\micro\farad} can) becomes marginal even with the ceramic
sharing current --- confirm the part series at BOM release.} Bus ripple:
\begin{align*}
  \Delta V \approx
  \frac{(I_{\mathrm{pk}}/2 - I_{\mathrm{in}})\,t_{\mathrm{on}}}{C_{\mathrm{in}}}
  + I_{\mathrm{pk}}\,\mathrm{ESR}
  \approx \frac{\SI{1.8}{\micro\coulomb}}{\SI{105}{\micro\farad}} + 2.2\times0.03
  \approx \SI{85}{\milli\volt}\ \text{pk--pk},
\end{align*}
unobjectionable on a USB-PD input.

\subsubsection{Output}

$C_{\mathrm{out}} = C204\,(\SI{4.7}{\micro\farad}\ \text{polarized radial}) +
C205\,(\SI{100}{\nano\farad}\ \text{ECQ-E film})$. Requirement check: the load is
constant-current tubes behind large ballasts, so the rail needs neither hold-up
nor transient reservoir --- only switching-ripple control. Capacitive ripple per
cycle:
\begin{align*}
  \Delta V_{\mathrm{cap}} &=
  \frac{I_{\mathrm{out}}\,(T - t_{\mathrm{dem}})}{C_{\mathrm{out}}}
  = \frac{16.2\,\si{\milli\ampere} \times 5.5\,\si{\micro\second}}
         {\SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}}
  = \SI{19}{\milli\volt},
\end{align*}
dwarfed by the ESR step $I_{s,\mathrm{pk}}\times\mathrm{ESR} \approx 0.22 \times 5
\approx \SI{1.1}{\volt}$ pk--pk ($0.6\,\%$ of the rail) at the assumed
\SI{5}{\ohm} ESR of a small high-voltage electrolytic, with the film part shunting
the fast edges. Through an \SI{8.2}{\kilo\ohm} ballast this modulates tube current
by $\sim$\SI{0.13}{\milli\ampere} at \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz} --- optically
invisible. The small capacitance is thus sufficient, and beneficially so: it is
what keeps the stored energy at \SI{69}{\milli\joule}, the start-up time at
\SI{20}{\milli\second} (\cref{sec:hv-ss}), and the bleed-down inside seconds
(\cref{sec:hv-bleeder}).

\textbf{Flag (voltage rating).} The schematic records neither dielectric nor
voltage rating for C204, and the reference designator alone cannot distinguish a
\SI{200}{\volt} from a \SI{250}{\volt} part. The derived trim ceiling is
\SI{196.3}{\volt}: a \SI{200}{\volt} electrolytic would retain 2\,\% headroom at
full trim --- unacceptable for an electrolytic's surge behavior --- while a
standard \SI{250}{\volt} part (available as \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} in the
populated 8\,mm can size) keeps $\ge$21\,\%. Polymer types (the input-side A759
series) do not exist at this voltage, so C204 must be a conventional aluminum
electrolytic rated \SI{250}{\volt}; C205's ECQ-E film series is \SI{250}{\volt}
by default. Confirm both ratings at BOM release.
