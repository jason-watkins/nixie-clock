The input, $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, is \SIrange{4.75}{13}{\volt}: the span of every
contract the board's USB-PD front end negotiates (\cref{sec:pd-classes}),
bounded by the \SI{5}{\volt} contract at $-5\,\%$ and the \SI{12}{\volt}

point.

Running from \SI{4.75}{\volt} drives three requirements a design for the
\SI{9}{\volt} contract alone would not have: the auxiliary bias supply of
\cref{sec:hv-house-pump}, the UVLO threshold of \cref{sec:hv-uvlo}, and a
display-inhibit condition (\cref{sec:hv-inhibit}) that must test the negotiated
\emph{current} allowance rather than the contract voltage.

\subsection{Preliminaries}\label{sec:hv-prelim}

\subsubsection{Switching frequency}\label{sec:hv-fsw-choice}

In DCM the energy packet ties frequency to peak current, $L_p f_{\mathrm{sw}}
I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2 = 2 P_{\mathrm{in}}$, so with $L_p$ fixed by the transformer
(\cref{sec:da2032}) and $P_{\mathrm{in}}$ by the load (\cref{sec:hv-budget}),
choosing a frequency is choosing how much of the \SI{3.0}{\ampere} saturation
rating the commanded peak occupies and how much remains for the current-limit
band. Two constraints bracket it, both scaling as $\sqrt{f_{\mathrm{sw}}}$.

$t_{\mathrm{on}}$ and $t_{\mathrm{dem}}$ both follow $I_{\mathrm{pk}} \propto
f_{\mathrm{sw}}^{-1/2}$ while the period falls as $f_{\mathrm{sw}}^{-1}$, so the
conducting fraction of the period grows as $\sqrt{f_{\mathrm{sw}}}$: from
$0.89\,T$ at the worst corner of \cref{sec:hv-dcm}, DCM fails at
\SI{185}{\kilo\hertz} and idle is down to $5\,\%$ by \SI{168}{\kilo\hertz}. At
the low end the sense-resistor window closes (\cref{sec:hv-cs-sense}): core
protection holds \des{R}{HVCS} at or above \SI{37.4}{\milli\ohm} at any
frequency, while the power-delivery ceiling,
$\SI{93}{\milli\volt}/I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(L\min)}$, rises as
$\sqrt{f_{\mathrm{sw}}}$; the two cross at \SI{126}{\kilo\hertz}, and the
specified \SI{39}{\milli\ohm} reaches its own ceiling at \SI{138}{\kilo\hertz}.

Nothing inside the bracket selects a value: design-max efficiency runs
\SI{83.8}{\percent} at \SI{126}{\kilo\hertz} against \SI{82.5}{\percent} at
\SI{185}{\kilo\hertz}. \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz} is adopted, near the geometric
centre that equalizes the two relative margins (\SI{153}{\kilo\hertz} across
  \SIrange{126}{185}{\kilo\hertz}, \SI{152}{\kilo\hertz} across the tighter
\SIrange{138}{168}{\kilo\hertz}). At the \SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz} realized below
the commanded peak is \SI{2.18}{\ampere}, $73\,\%$ of $I_{\mathrm{sat}}$;
raising the frequency buys current-limit margin and spends DCM idle.

\subsubsection{RT resistor}\label{sec:hv-fsw}

For the \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz} target of \cref{sec:hv-fsw-choice}, the RT
equation of \cref{sec:lm5156h} gives



\begin{align*}
  R_T &= \frac{2.21\times10^{10}}{f_{RT}} - 955
       = \frac{2.21\times10^{10}}{1.5\times10^{5}} - 955
       = \SI{146.4}{\kilo\ohm}.
\end{align*}
The nearest standard value is $\des{R}{HVRT} = \SI{147}{\kilo\ohm}$, giving
\begin{align*}
  f_{RT} &= \frac{2.21\times10^{10}}{147\,000 + 955} = \SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz}
\end{align*}
All subsequent arithmetic uses \SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz}.


The DA2032-AL is characterized only at \SI{100}{\kilo\hertz}
(\cref{sec:da2032}). For a DCM flyback its inductance and saturation figures
translate directly to \SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz}; core loss does not, and is
carried as an allowance in \cref{sec:hv-op}.

\subsubsection{Input UVLO}\label{sec:hv-uvlo}

UVLO must release below the converter's rated \SI{4.75}{\volt} input minimum,
with margin against the LM51561H's threshold tolerance: an enable point sitting
at \SI{4.75}{\volt} would risk failing to enable at an input the converter is
specified to run from. A target of \SI{4.4}{\volt}, a few hundred millivolts
below, gives that margin without pushing the enable point needlessly low.

The UVLO/SYNC pin's thresholds (\SI{1.5}{\volt} rising / \SI{1.45}{\volt} falling
typical, \cref{sec:lm5156h}) are set by an external divider from $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$
to ground: $V_{\mathrm{on}} = V_{th,\mathrm{on}}(\des{R}{HVUV1}+\des{R}{HVUV2})/\des{R}{HVUV2}$,
requiring a ratio $(\des{R}{HVUV1}+\des{R}{HVUV2})/\des{R}{HVUV2} \approx 4.4/1.5 = 2.93$.

\des{R}{HVUV2}, the lower leg, is a free parameter within that ratio. Tens of
kilohms is standard for a sense divider on this class of controller: low enough
that its quiescent draw off $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ is negligible, high enough that
the \SI{5}{\micro\ampere} hysteresis current the pin sources while enabled
(\cref{sec:lm5156h}) produces a meaningful hysteresis voltage across
$\des{R}{HVUV1}\!\parallel\!\des{R}{HVUV2}$. \SI{32.4}{\kilo\ohm} is adopted,
giving
\begin{align*}
  \des{R}{HVUV1} &= \des{R}{HVUV2}\left(\frac{4.4}{1.5} - 1\right)
  = 32.4 \times 1.933 = \SI{62.6}{\kilo\ohm}
  \;\rightarrow\; \SI{63.4}{\kilo\ohm}.
\end{align*}



The same pin carries a lower EN comparator ($\approx$\SI{0.52}{\volt} rising).
Through this divider that corresponds to $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = 0.52 \times 2.957 =
\SI{1.54}{\volt}$: on any monotonic input ramp EN is satisfied \SI{2.9}{\volt}
before UVLO releases, so one divider serves both comparators.

\subsubsection{Display inhibit}\label{sec:hv-inhibit}

UVLO keeps the converter from enabling before the power rail is established, but
it does not guarantee enough current to drive the tubes. To accomplish this, the
MCU reads the negotiated contract from the USB-PD chip and provides a
display-inhibit signal to the converter. \des{Q}{HVINH}, an N-channel MOSFET
across \des{R}{HVUV2}, grounds the UVLO/SYNC pin when on; the MCU releases it.
Two requirements bound how its gate is biased.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{The inhibit is asserted before the converter can run, on every
    ramp.} The converter and the MCU are both supplied from
    $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, but the MCU's 3.3~V rail is the output of a converter of
    its own (\cref{sec:3v3-converter}) and reaches regulation some time after
    $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ appears. A gate bias referred to that rail would make the
    default state depend on how two independent converters' start-up times
    happen to order, a quantity no part of this design controls. Referred to
    $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ instead, the gate drive and the divider it shunts rise
    together and the ordering is fixed by resistor ratios alone.
  \item \textbf{The commanding pin does not sit on that node.} A gate biased
    from $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ reaches most of \SI{13}{\volt}, while the module's
    pins are recommended to \(V_{DD}+0.3\)~V (\cref{sec:esp32}). The gate and
    the pin that commands it are in different domains and cannot be the same
    node.
\end{itemize}

Together these specify the network. \des{R}{HVINHPU} and \des{R}{HVINHPD}
divide $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ onto \des{Q}{HVINH}'s gate, and a second N-channel
MOSFET, \des{Q}{HVREL}, pulls that gate to ground when the MCU asserts its
enable. \des{R}{HVRELPD} holds \des{Q}{HVREL} off otherwise, so the MCU pin sees
only a gate and a pull-down, and the inhibited state is the one a
high-impedance pin produces with no rail present anywhere. That is the same
requirement \cref{sec:esp32-usb} places on the data-switch enable, and it is met
here without reference to a supply.

\paragraph{Gate divider.} $\des{R}{HVINHPU} = \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}$ and
$\des{R}{HVINHPD} = \SI{1}{\mega\ohm}$ put $0.909\,V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ on the
gate: \SI{4.32}{\volt} at the bottom of the input window and \SI{11.8}{\volt} at
the top. The upper figure specifies the gate rating, $V_{GS} \geq
\pm\SI{12}{\volt}$; the threshold ceiling comes from the ordering requirement
below. The pair draws $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/\SI{1.1}{\mega\ohm}$,
\SI{11.8}{\micro\ampere} at the top of the window.

\paragraph{Ordering.} The divider of \cref{sec:hv-uvlo} cannot carry the
UVLO/SYNC pin to the comparator's \SI{1.425}{\volt} minimum rising threshold
until $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = 1.425 \times 2.957 = \SI{4.21}{\volt}$, where the gate
above stands at \SI{3.83}{\volt}. Establishing the inhibit first therefore
bounds the shunt at $V_{GS(th)} \leq \SI{3.83}{\volt}$ worst case. The
$V_{GS(th)} \leq \SI{2}{\volt}$ that the release switch below requires
independently sits well inside that bound and places the assertion point at
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = 2.0/0.909 = \SI{2.20}{\volt}$: two volts of input below the
earliest release the tolerance stack permits, at any slew rate the source
presents. The EN comparator, whose
\SIrange{0.4}{0.7}{\volt} rising range maps through the same divider to
\SIrange{1.18}{2.07}{\volt} of input, may be satisfied first; between it and
\SI{2.20}{\volt} the controller sits in standby, where the CS pin is grounded
and the GATE output does not switch (\cref{sec:lm5156h}).

\paragraph{Shunt conduction.} \des{Q}{HVINH} sinks the
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/\des{R}{HVUV1} = \SI{205}{\micro\ampere}$ the divider delivers
at the top of the input window and must hold the pin below its \SI{1.45}{\volt}
falling threshold, so the channel need only be under
$\SI{1.45}{\volt}/\SI{205}{\micro\ampere} = \SI{7.1}{\kilo\ohm}$. Against the
\SI{4.32}{\volt} minimum gate drive derived above, a part specified in single
ohms clears that by three orders.

\paragraph{Release switch.} \des{Q}{HVREL} blocks $0.909\,V_{\mathrm{BUS}} =
\SI{11.8}{\volt}$ across its channel when off and sinks
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/\des{R}{HVINHPU} = \SI{130}{\micro\ampere}$ when on, neither
demanding. What selects it is conduction at the \SI{3.3}{\volt} the MCU drives
its gate with: $V_{GS(th)} \leq \SI{2}{\volt}$ maximum, which the shunt's
\SI{3.83}{\volt} bound also admits, so one part serves both positions. Holding the shunt
gate below \des{Q}{HVINH}'s \SI{0.6}{\volt} minimum threshold at that
\SI{130}{\micro\ampere} allows up to \SI{4.6}{\kilo\ohm} of channel, which no
part meeting the drive requirement approaches. $\des{R}{HVRELPD} =
\SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}$ holds \des{Q}{HVREL} off against leakage alone and costs
the MCU pin \SI{33}{\micro\ampere} to overcome.

\subsubsection{Controller housekeeping (BIAS, VCC, PGOOD)}\label{sec:hv-house}

\paragraph{BIAS.}\label{sec:hv-house-bias}
BIAS feeds the internal regulator that produces the \SI{6.85}{\volt} VCC
gate-driver rail. The datasheet specifies that \SIrange{6.5}{7}{\volt} output at
a single condition, $V_{\mathrm{BIAS}} = \SI{8}{\volt}$ (\cref{sec:lm5156h}).
Tied straight to $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, BIAS satisfies that only above about
\SI{8}{\volt} of input; below, the regulator drops out and the gate rail follows
the input down. BIAS is therefore fed from the charge pump of
\cref{sec:hv-house-pump}, diode-ORed with a start-up path from
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$.

\paragraph{VCC regulator capacitor.}\label{sec:hv-house-vcc} The datasheet
requires \SIrange{1}{4.7}{\micro\farad} (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{14}). \des{C}{HVVCC} $=
\SI{2.2}{\micro\farad}$ leaves margin above the \SI{1}{\micro\farad} floor
against the DC-bias derating of a small ceramic at the \SI{6.85}{\volt} VCC
rail.

\paragraph{PGOOD pull-up.}\label{sec:hv-house-pgood} PGOOD is open-drain
(\cref{sec:lm5156h}) and needs an external pull-up, bounded on both sides: the
datasheet's $\ge\SI{10}{\kilo\ohm}$ recommendation keeps the sink current small,
and the pull-up must charge the pin's parasitic capacitance well inside the
\SI{25}{\micro\second} deglitch window built into the assert condition
(\cref{sec:lm5156h}). \des{R}{HVPG} $= \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}$ to the +3V3 logic
rail gives $\tau=RC\approx\SI{2}{\nano\second}$ against even a generous
\SI{20}{\pico\farad} of stray capacitance. Against the pin's own ratings
(\cref{sec:lm5156h}): \SI{3.3}{\volt} is far under the \SI{18}{\volt} absolute
maximum, and under $V_{\mathrm{BIAS}}+\SI{0.3}{\volt}$, which the pin's internal
diode to BIAS makes relevant (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{25}); asserted-low sink current
is $3.3/\SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}=\SI{33}{\micro\ampere}$, well under the
\SI{1}{\milli\ampere} limit.

\paragraph{Spread spectrum (DITHOFF).}\label{sec:hv-house-dithoff}
DITHOFF selects the controller's dual random spread spectrum
(\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{3}). The modulation spans $0.156 f_{\mathrm{sw}}$, about
\SI{23}{\kilo\hertz} here, as a low-frequency triangular sweep combined with a
cycle-by-cycle random perturbation (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{17}).

The dither costs DCM idle time. The loop holds output power constant, so
$I_{\mathrm{pk}}$ falls only as $\sqrt{f_{\mathrm{sw}}}$: at the top of the
dither range $t_{\mathrm{on}}+t_{\mathrm{dem}}$ shrinks $3.7\,\%$ while the
period shrinks $7.2\,\%$, taking the worst-corner DCM idle fraction from
$10.5\,\%$ to $7.0\,\%$ (\cref{sec:hv-dcm}), with no offsetting benefit to this
design. DITHOFF ties to VCC; dithering is disabled.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary bias charge pump}\label{sec:hv-house-pump}

\paragraph{The requirement.} \des{Q}{HVSW} turns off through the difference
between the gate rail and its own Miller plateau: the plateau
(\SI{3}{\volt}, \cref{sec:hv-op-losses}) and \des{D}{HVGOFF}'s drop subtract
\SI{3.5}{\volt}, and what remains drives $\des{R}{HVGOFF}+R_{\mathrm{int}} =
\SI{4.8}{\ohm}$, giving $t_{\mathrm{ovl}} = Q_{GD}/I_{\mathrm{gate}}$. Overlap
is the largest term in the loss budget, and at the design-max envelope that
budget closes on itself: loss raises input power, input power raises the energy
packet, and the packet raises the commanded peak current, which must stay inside
the \SI{2.36}{\ampere} guaranteed-minimum current limit
(\cref{sec:hv-cs-sense}) for the converter to deliver its rated load at all.
Solving that loop for the gate rail bounds overlap at \SI{118}{\nano\second},
which takes \SI{1.02}{\volt} of drive above the plateau and therefore a rail of
at least \SI{4.52}{\volt}. A BIAS pin fed from $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ cannot hold
that at the bottom of the input window: a Schottky drop into the pin and the
VCC regulator's own dropout leave \SI{4.20}{\volt} of gate drive there, which
puts the commanded peak past the limit.

BIAS is therefore fed from the switch node. During the off-time that node
stands one tenth of the output above
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ (\cref{sec:hv-op-converge}), a shelf of
$\approx$\SI{24}{\volt} at the bottom of the input window, from which a charge
pump holds BIAS above the \SI{8}{\volt} at which the datasheet specifies the
regulated \SI{6.85}{\volt} VCC output.

\paragraph{Topology.} \des{C}{HVPUMP} couples \des{Q}{HVSW}'s drain to the pump
node through \des{R}{HVPUMP}; the delivery diode \des{D}{HVPUMP} passes charge
from there into BIAS, where \des{C}{HVBIAS} is the reservoir; the reset diode
\des{D}{HVRESET} returns the node to ground each on-time; and \des{D}{HVSTART}
feeds BIAS from $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ before the converter switches.

During the on-time the drain sits near ground and \des{D}{HVRESET} holds the
pump node one diode drop below it, leaving \des{C}{HVPUMP} charged to
$V_{DS(\mathrm{on})} + V_F \approx \SI{0.9}{\volt}$. At turn-off the drain steps
to the reflected shelf $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} + V_{\mathrm{refl}}$ and carries the
node with it; \des{D}{HVPUMP} conducts until the node falls back to
$V_{\mathrm{BIAS}} + V_F$.

The pump needs no regulation loop, because the controller fixes the charge it
must deliver:
\begin{align*}
  Q_{\mathrm{BIAS}} &= Q_G + \frac{I_Q}{f_{\mathrm{sw}}}
  = 40 + \frac{580\,\si{\micro\ampere}}{149.4\,\si{\kilo\hertz}}
  = \SI{43.9}{\nano\coulomb}
  \quad (I_{\mathrm{BIAS}} = \SI{6.56}{\milli\ampere}),
\end{align*}
so BIAS settles wherever the delivered packet equals that demand:
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{BIAS}} &= (V_{\mathrm{BUS}} + V_{\mathrm{refl}})
  - \SI{0.9}{\volt} - V_{F} - \frac{Q_{\mathrm{BIAS}}}{k\,\des{C}{HVPUMP}},
  &
  k &= 1 - e^{-t_{\mathrm{dem}}/(\des{R}{HVPUMP}\des{C}{HVPUMP})},
\end{align*}
with $k$ the fraction of the packet transferred inside the demagnetization
window. \des{D}{HVSTART} reverse-biases once the pump lifts BIAS past
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, and must be a Schottky: at \SI{4.75}{\volt} a silicon drop
would leave only \SI{0.55}{\volt} above the controller's \SI{3.5}{\volt} BIAS
minimum during start-up.

\paragraph{Sizing.} \des{R}{HVPUMP}'s time constant must be long against the
\SI{10}{\nano\second} leakage spike (\cref{sec:hv-clamp}) and short against
$t_{\mathrm{dem}}$, so the packet completes inside the shelf. $\des{R}{HVPUMP} =
\SI{82}{\ohm}$ gives $\tau = \SI{385}{\nano\second}$, between that spike and a
$\ge\SI{940}{\nano\second}$ window, and limits the current drawn from the switch
node to \SI{118}{\milli\ampere}.

\des{C}{HVPUMP} is set by the worst delivery corner, the bottom of the input
window at the lowest trim setpoint (\SI{164.8}{\volt},
\cref{tab:hv-ballast-divider}), where the reflected shelf and the load are both
smallest, shortening $t_{\mathrm{dem}}$ to \SI{940}{\nano\second} and $k$ to
\num{0.92}. BIAS must stay at or above \SI{8}{\volt}, the condition at which the
datasheet specifies the \SI{6.85}{\volt} VCC output, and $\des{C}{HVPUMP} =
\SI{4.7}{\nano\farad}$ holds \SI{9.09}{\volt} there even at the $-5\,\%$ limit
of a C0G tolerance. The dielectric is specified because the pump meters charge:
a class-II part's voltage coefficient would change the delivered packet with
operating point.

Across the rest of the window BIAS rises with the input, reaching
\SI{20.79}{\volt} at \SI{13}{\volt} in, well inside the pin's \SI{60}{\volt}
recommended range. \des{D}{HVZ}, a \SI{30}{\volt} zener, bounds a fault that
drives the reflected voltage above that range and conducts nothing in normal
operation. \des{Q}{HVSW} bounds the fault in turn: the drain reaches its
\SI{100}{\volt} rating at roughly \SI{520}{\volt} out, where the shelf is
\SI{65}{\volt} and the pump meters $k\,\des{C}{HVPUMP}(V_{\mathrm{shelf}} -
\SI{1.6}{\volt} - V_Z) = \SI{149}{\nano\coulomb}$ against the
\SI{43.9}{\nano\coulomb} demand. The zener sinks the \SI{105}{\nano\coulomb}
surplus, $30 \times 105\,\si{\nano\coulomb} \times 149.4\,\si{\kilo\hertz} =
\SI{471}{\milli\watt}$, and a \SI{0.5}{\watt} part covers it as a transient:
charging $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ to \SI{520}{\volt} at the current limit takes about
\SI{140}{\milli\second}.

\des{C}{HVBIAS} carries the controller between packets, at a droop of
$I_{\mathrm{BIAS}}/(\des{C}{HVBIAS} f_{\mathrm{sw}}) = \SI{88}{\milli\volt}$ for
\SI{0.5}{\micro\farad}, negligible against the regulator's \SI{2.2}{\volt} of
headroom at the worst corner, so \SI{0.5}{\micro\farad} \emph{effective} is the
floor. A class-II ceramic at \SI{21}{\volt} of bias retains well over half its
value on a \SI{50}{\volt} rating, so \SI{1}{\micro\farad} X7R keeps that floor.
Should the pump stop delivering, as it does when the converter skips pulses at
light load, BIAS falls back onto \des{D}{HVSTART} and the converter runs at the
reduced gate rail until load returns, where turn-off loss is proportional to a
peak current light load has already made small.

Neither pump diode sees the shelf voltage: working against each other they hold
the pump node between $-V_F$ and $V_{\mathrm{BIAS}} + V_F$, so each blocks at
most \SI{21.5}{\volt} in normal operation and \SI{30.7}{\volt} in the fault
\des{D}{HVZ} bounds, a $\ge\SI{40}{\volt}$ requirement under the
\SI{20}{\percent} derating. Both switch every cycle, so each must recover before
the next edge.

\paragraph{Cost.} The pump's overhead is fixed by conservation.
$Q_{\mathrm{BIAS}}$ enters at the shelf potential and leaves at the
\SI{6.85}{\volt} rail, so the loss is
$Q_{\mathrm{BIAS}} f_{\mathrm{sw}} (V_{\mathrm{shelf}} - \SI{6.85}{\volt})$
whatever the component values are; against feeding BIAS from $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$
at the same gate rail, the increment is
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{BIAS}} V_{\mathrm{refl}}
  &= \SI{6.56}{\milli\ampere} \times \SI{19.25}{\volt}
  = \SI{126}{\milli\watt},
\end{align*}
constant across the input window because the shelf tracks $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$.
\des{C}{HVPUMP} redistributes that loss without changing it: a larger value
settles BIAS higher, moving dissipation out of \des{R}{HVPUMP} and into the
controller's internal regulator. At \SI{13}{\volt} in, the split is
\SI{91}{\milli\watt} inside the package and the remainder in \des{R}{HVPUMP}.
Gate charge is the one term that reduces it, the pump supplying that charge from
the shelf potential well above $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$; \cref{sec:hv-summary} carries
it as the design's largest identified efficiency lever.

\subsection{Output Voltage}\label{sec:hv-setpoint}

\subsubsection{Output setpoint}\label{sec:hv-ignition}

The IN-12B's guaranteed $\le\SI{170}{\volt}$ ignition ceiling (\cref{sec:in12b})
is the zero-margin floor for the converter's output (the INS-1's \SI{90}{\volt}
ceiling is never limiting). A flat \SI{15}{\volt} margin is added to cover
strike-voltage spread and other uncertainty, so the converter must be tunable
across a \SIrange{170}{185}{\volt} band. A single
fixed ballast and divider holds the tube's current limits over only a few volts
(\cref{sec:hv-load-digit}), so \cref{sec:hv-divider-split} tabulates
ballast/\des{R}{HVD3} pairs to cover the range while keeping the IN-12 tubes
within their rated current band. The INS-1 tubes carry no rated current band at
all, so a single ballast serves them across the trim range.

\subsubsection{Interpretation of the datasheet's 200/250~V anode-supply figures}\label{sec:hv-anode-figures}

The IN-12B datasheet states a minimum anode supply of \SI{200}{\volt} and a
recommended \SI{250}{\volt}~DC, both above the margin band derived above. The
working hypothesis of \cref{sec:in12b} is that these figures serve the tube's
other specified supply mode, unfiltered half-wave-rectified AC, in which the
tube re-ignites every mains cycle and conducts only near the crest, so the
supply needs substantial peak headroom above the ignition voltage merely to
achieve a usable conduction angle and average brightness.

\subsubsection{Operating points used throughout this section}\label{sec:hv-points}

Two operating points recur, bracketing the trim range:
\begin{description}
  \item[Nominal] $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = \SI{9}{\volt}$, the intermediate USB-PD
  contract; output at \SI{170}{\volt}, the IN-12B's guaranteed ignition ceiling
  (\cref{sec:hv-ignition}); tube currents at their expected values.
  \item[Design-max envelope] $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} = \SI{4.75}{\volt}$ (bottom of
    the input window, worst case for duty cycle and primary conduction), output
    at \SI{191.5}{\volt}, and every digit tube simultaneously at its
    datasheet-maximum \SI{3.5}{\milli\ampere}, which is what the widest-ballast
    row of \cref{tab:hv-ballast-divider} reaches at exactly that current.
\end{description}

Peak current in DCM is set by the energy packet rather than by the line
($I_{\mathrm{pk}} = \sqrt{2P_{\mathrm{in}}/L_p f_{\mathrm{sw}}}$), so the two
points differ in load far more than in line. Where a check does depend on line,
both ends of the window are evaluated: \SI{4.75}{\volt} maximizes duty cycle,
conduction loss, and on-time, \SI{13}{\volt} the commutation voltage, switch and
diode reverse stress, and current slew at the sense resistor.

\paragraph{Achieved tuning range.}\label{sec:hv-achieved-range}
The digit ballast and the feedback-divider resistor it pairs with are chosen
together from \cref{tab:hv-ballast-divider}, and each row's rated-current window
(\SIrange{2.5}{3.5}{\milli\ampere}, \cref{sec:in12b}) maps to a different
voltage, so the range the design must tolerate is wider than the
\SIrange{170}{185}{\volt} requirement: \textbf{$V_{\mathrm{HV}}$ may be
deliberately tuned anywhere from \SI{164.8}{\volt} to \SI{191.5}{\volt}}. Checks
on component \emph{safety} (rectifier and capacitor voltage ratings, switch and
clamp stress) use this wider envelope; checks that something \emph{works as
intended}, such as a tube current sitting inside its rated band, need hold only
across \SIrange{170}{185}{\volt}.

\subsection{Ballast sizing}\label{sec:hv-load}

\subsubsection{Digit tubes (NX1--NX4, ballast \des{R}{BALDIG})}\label{sec:hv-load-digit}

Each IN-12B anode is fed from $V_{\mathrm{HV}}$ through its ballast; the
selected cathode returns through a K155ID1 output, sinking the tube's rated DC
cathode current $I_K \in \SIrange{2.5}{3.5}{\milli\ampere}$ (\cref{sec:in12b}).
The ballast equation is
\begin{align*}
  R_A &= \frac{V_{\mathrm{rail}} - V_M - V_K}{I_K},
\end{align*}
with maintaining voltage $V_M = \SI{145}{\volt}$ (typical, no tabulated spread;
\cref{sec:in12b}) and $V_K$ the K155ID1's on-state drop at the selected output.
The driver's \SI{2.5}{\volt} maximum $V_K$ is specified at \SI{7}{\milli\ampere}
(\cref{sec:bcd-driver}), at least double $I_K$'s range, so it overestimates
$V_K$ here; \SI{1.0}{\volt} is the expected value, the saturation drop falling
with collector current. Sizing uses \SI{1.0}{\volt}, since the resulting
worst-case current error, $\Delta V_K/R_{A,\min} \approx
\SI{1.5}{\volt}/\SI{7.50}{\kilo\ohm} \approx \SI{0.20}{\milli\ampere}$ at the
smallest ballast, is small against the rated window's own
\SI{0.5}{\milli\ampere} half-width.

The required trim range (\SIrange{170}{185}{\volt}, \cref{sec:hv-ignition}) is
too wide for a single ballast value: one that holds the cathode current inside
its rated \SIrange{2.5}{3.5}{\milli\ampere} band at the low end of that range
pushes it outside at the high end. \Cref{sec:hv-divider-split} therefore derives
a table of ballast/\des{R}{HVD3} pairs, each with \emph{tuning targets}
$V_{\mathrm{min}}$--$V_{\mathrm{max}}$: the voltages that drive exactly
\SIrange{2.5}{3.5}{\milli\ampere} through that row's ballast. The trimmer's
travel extends past both ($V_{\mathrm{floor,wc}}$/$V_{\mathrm{ceil,wc}}$
columns), so the targets stay reachable with worst-case part tolerances.

\des{R}{BALDIG} may be any value in \cref{tab:hv-ballast-divider}
(\SIrange{7.50}{13.00}{\kilo\ohm}) paired with the matching \des{R}{HVD3}, and
every calculation that depends on the ballast is checked across the table's full
range. Worst-case dissipation grows with $R_A$ at the shared
\SI{3.5}{\milli\ampere} rated maximum, so the largest entry bounds every row:
\begin{align*}
  I_K^2 R_A = 3.5^2 \times 13.00 \approx \SI{159}{\milli\watt},
\end{align*}
still well inside a DIN0207 axial part's $\approx\SI{0.6}{\watt}$ rating
($\approx$27\,\%).

The IN-12B decimal-point cathode has no driver signal and carries no current.

\subsubsection{Colon tubes (NX5--NX6, ballast \des{R}{BALCOL})}\label{sec:hv-load-colon}

Each INS-1 anode is fed from $V_{\mathrm{HV}}$ through its ballast, and its
cathode returns directly to ground. The colons carry no driver, so they light
whenever the rail is up, and the ballast equation has no on-state drop to
subtract. Unlike the digit ballasts, one resistor value serves.

The INS-1's maintaining voltage is given only as a ceiling,
$V_M \leq \SI{55}{\volt}$ (\cref{sec:ins1}). Taking the ceiling minimizes the
drop available across the ballast and so minimizes the computed current, which
is the pessimistic choice for the lower current bound. The datasheet states no
operating-current band either, only that \SI{0.5}{\milli\ampere} is the optimum
for brightness, so the ballast is sized to place the current there at the middle
of the \SIrange{170}{185}{\volt} design range (\cref{sec:hv-ignition}). At
\SI{177.5}{\volt}:
\begin{align*}
  R_A &= \frac{\SI{177.5}{\volt} - \SI{55}{\volt}}{\SI{0.5}{\milli\ampere}}
       = \SI{245}{\kilo\ohm}.
\end{align*}
Specification: \des{R}{BALCOL} $= \SI{240}{\kilo\ohm}$, the nearest standard
value. Across the design range that gives $I = \SI{0.479}{\milli\ampere}$ at
\SI{170}{\volt} and $I = \SI{0.542}{\milli\ampere}$ at \SI{185}{\volt}, and
across the full \SIrange{164.8}{191.5}{\volt} achieved range
(\cref{sec:hv-achieved-range}) \SIrange{0.458}{0.569}{\milli\ampere}: from
$8.5\,\%$ under the optimum to $14\,\%$ over it, everywhere the trimmer can
reach.

The datasheet bounds that spread. It does not bound the maintaining voltage: a
tube maintaining below \SI{55}{\volt} leaves more drop across a fixed ballast
and draws proportionally more current, \SI{0.61}{\milli\ampere} at the top of
the achieved range if $V_M$ were \SI{45}{\volt}. No maximum current is published
to check that against, and the \SI{0.5}{\milli\ampere} optimum is the only
figure the sheet offers, so the excursion is recorded here and left unbounded.
It compounds the trim range's own spread, since both effects raise the current
as the drop across the ballast grows.

\paragraph{Ballast tolerance.} \des{R}{BALCOL} is specified at $5\,\%$, looser
than every other resistor in this section, because no requirement here rewards a
tighter part. A $5\,\%$ part moves the current $\mp5\,\%$, widening the
achieved-range spread from \SIrange{0.458}{0.569}{\milli\ampere} to
\SIrange{0.436}{0.599}{\milli\ampere}, which crosses no rated band; the
datasheet states none. Tolerance matters for one thing here: matching between
the two lamps. Parts at opposing $5\,\%$ extremes differ by
\SI{10.5}{\percent} of current, against $2\,\%$ for $1\,\%$ parts. The datasheet
bounds neither the brightness difference that produces nor the tube-to-tube
spread it competes with, and \SI{5}{\volt} of $V_M$ difference between two lamps
is itself $4\,\%$ of current at mid-trim. These two resistors sit on the visible
face of the clock, where \cref{sec:hv-parts}'s construction preference selects a
carbon film body.

\subsection{Feedback divider design}\label{sec:hv-divider}

\subsubsection{Divider current selection}\label{sec:hv-divider-current}

Two bounds constrain the divider current.

\textbf{Lower bound on resistance: power cost.} The divider loads
$V_{\mathrm{HV}}$ whenever the converter runs, tube state notwithstanding.
Against the \SIrange{10.6}{15.1}{\milli\ampere} of tube current in
\cref{sec:hv-load}, a divider current a hundred times smaller costs nothing
worth recovering.

\textbf{Upper bound on resistance: feedback-node integrity.} Raising the
resistance to shave that cost pushes setpoint error the wrong way twice: FB bias
or leakage current becomes a larger fraction of the divider current, and the
node's higher impedance couples more readily to the switching noise
\des{Q}{HVSW} and \des{T}{HV} radiate a few millimeters away.
\Cref{sec:lm5156h} reports no FB leakage figure, so the bound rests on the
low-nanoamp leakage typical of feedback pins on this class of controller;
\SI{100}{\micro\ampere} of divider current keeps that four to five orders of
magnitude below, wide enough that its exact value is not critical.

At the \SI{170}{\volt} reference point that sets a target total divider
resistance of $(170-1)\,\si{\volt}/\SI{100}{\micro\ampere} \approx
\SI{1.69}{\mega\ohm}$.

\subsubsection{Resistor split and trim range}\label{sec:hv-divider-split}

The divider runs $R_{\mathrm{upper}} \rightarrow \mathrm{FB} \rightarrow
R_{\mathrm{lower}} \rightarrow \mathrm{GND}$, with $V_{\mathrm{out}} =
V_{\mathrm{REF}}(1+R_{\mathrm{upper}}/R_{\mathrm{lower}})$ and
$V_{\mathrm{REF}} = \SI{1.00}{\volt}$ (\cref{sec:lm5156h}), so the split is
$R_{\mathrm{upper}}/R_{\mathrm{lower}} = V_{\mathrm{out}}/V_{\mathrm{REF}} - 1 =
169$ at the \SI{170}{\volt} reference point. $R_{\mathrm{lower}}$ therefore
contributes under $1\,\%$ of the series resistance, negligible for the divider
current though it sets the trim range, and $R_{\mathrm{upper}}$ takes the
\SI{1.69}{\mega\ohm} target of \cref{sec:hv-divider-current} exactly, split
across two equal resistors to halve the voltage each element withstands
(\cref{sec:hv-divider-stress}): \des{R}{HVD1} = \des{R}{HVD2} =
\SI{845}{\kilo\ohm}.

$R_{\mathrm{lower}}$ comprises a fixed \des{R}{HVD3} in series with a trimmer
\des{RV}{HVTRIM} ($R_{\mathrm{lower}} = \des{R}{HVD3} + R_V$, $0 \le R_V \le
R_{V,\mathrm{tot}}$). $R_{V,\mathrm{tot}} = \SI{2}{\kilo\ohm}$ is sized so the
trimmer's travel spans $V_{\min}$ to $V_{\max}$ for every row of
\cref{tab:hv-ballast-divider} across the worst-case tolerances of every divider
component, and \des{R}{HVD3} is chosen per row so that the trimmer's electrical
midpoint lands at that row's $V_{\mathrm{typ}}$.

\subsubsection{Divider element stress}\label{sec:hv-divider-stress}

\des{R}{HVD1} and \des{R}{HVD2} split $(V_{\mathrm{out}}-1)$ evenly:
\SI{84.5}{\volt} each at the \SI{170}{\volt} reference point, \SI{95.25}{\volt}
at the \SI{191.5}{\volt} design-max envelope (\cref{sec:hv-achieved-range}),
against the \SI{250}{\volt} limiting element voltage of the specified DIN0207
axial metal film parts. Dissipation is $95.25^2/\SI{845}{\kilo\ohm} =
\SI{10.7}{\milli\watt}$. \des{R}{HVD3} and \des{RV}{HVTRIM} see about
\SI{1}{\volt}.

\subsection{Operating point and efficiency}\label{sec:hv-op}

\subsubsection{Component values assumed for the loss model}\label{sec:hv-op-inputs}

The loss model below uses component values whose own sizing depends on this
section's converged peak current, each derived at its cross-referenced point:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \des{Q}{HVSW} is an IRL540N-type commodity through-hole logic-level power MOSFET;
  \item $\des{R}{HVGON} = \SI{10}{\ohm}$, governing turn-on;
  \item \des{D}{HVGOFF} (SB240S-E3/54) and $\des{R}{HVGOFF} = \SI{3.3}{\ohm}$,
    the turn-off speedup network, sized in
    \cref{sec:hv-op-losses,sec:hv-switch};
  \item $\des{R}{HVCS} = \SI{39}{\milli\ohm}$, the current-sense resistor,
    derived in \cref{sec:hv-cs};
  \item $\des{R}{HVCL} = \SI{30}{\kilo\ohm}$, $\des{C}{HVCL} =
    \SI{12}{\nano\farad}$, the RCD clamp network, sized in
    \cref{sec:hv-clamp}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Loss mechanisms}\label{sec:hv-op-losses}

In DCM the converter transfers a fixed packet of magnetizing energy each cycle:
\begin{align*}
  P_{\mathrm{in}} = \tfrac{1}{2} L_p I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  I_{\mathrm{pk}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 P_{\mathrm{in}}}{L_p f_{\mathrm{sw}}}},
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{in}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{out}}}{\eta}.
\end{align*}
Efficiency is derived by iteration: guess $\eta$, compute $P_{\mathrm{in}}$
and $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$, evaluate each loss mechanism, recompute $\eta$, repeat to
convergence. The loss models and their inputs:
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[Primary conduction] Triangular current over $t_{\mathrm{on}}$:
    $I_{\mathrm{rms}} = I_{\mathrm{pk}}\sqrt{D/3}$ through
    $R_{DS(\mathrm{on})} + \des{R}{HVCS} + R_{\mathrm{DCR,pri}}
     = 55 + 39 + 13 = \SI{107}{\milli\ohm}$. The datasheet tabulates
    $R_{DS(\mathrm{on})}$ at only two $V_{GS}$ points, both at
    $T_J=\SI{25}{\celsius}$: \SI{53}{\milli\ohm} at $V_{GS}=\SI{5.0}{\volt}$ and
    \SI{44}{\milli\ohm} at $V_{GS}=\SI{10}{\volt}$
    (\ds{infineon-irl540n-datasheet-en.pdf}{2}), so interpolating to the
    \SI{6.85}{\volt} gate rail gives a cold value of
    $\approx\SI{49.7}{\milli\ohm}$. It then rises with junction temperature
    (\ds{infineon-irl540n-datasheet-en.pdf}{3}, Fig.\,4, read as a linear
    approximation between its $1.0\times$-at-\SI{25}{\celsius} and
    $2.4\times$-at-\SI{175}{\celsius} anchors), which \des{Q}{HVSW}'s own
    dissipation sets. Solved self-consistently against the design-max loss
    budget below (\SI{25}{\celsius} ambient assumed), the converged point is
    $T_J \approx \SI{37}{\celsius}$, $R_{DS(\mathrm{on})} \approx
    \SI{55}{\milli\ohm}$.

  \item[Turn-off overlap]
    The IRL540N datasheet (\ds{infineon-irl540n-datasheet-en.pdf}{2}) specifies
    the gate-drain (Miller) charge only as a guaranteed maximum, $Q_{GD} \le
    \SI{38}{\nano\coulomb}$, with no typical value tabulated; its gate-charge
    curve (\ds{infineon-irl540n-datasheet-en.pdf}{4}, Fig.\,6) shows the Miller
    plateau sitting at $V_{\mathrm{plateau}} \approx \SI{3}{\volt}$. The same
    datasheet's resistive-load switching-time table gives $t_f =
    \SI{62}{\nano\second}$ typical at its own test condition
    ($R_G=\SI{5.0}{\ohm}$, $V_{GS}$ pulse $=\SI{5.0}{\volt}$), which implies a
    typical $Q_{GD} \approx t_f \times (5.0-3.0)/5.0\,\si{\ohm} \approx
    \SI{25}{\nano\coulomb}$ at that test point. The LM51561H's output stage adds
    an internal pull-down resistance in series with $\des{R}{HVGON}$. Its datasheet
    (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{6}, MOSFET DRIVER) gives the low-state output's voltage
    drop under an externally forced \SI{100}{\milli\ampere} as \SI{0.15}{\volt},
    implying $R_{\mathrm{int}} \approx \SI{1.5}{\ohm}$ for this pull-down path
    (the table's two rows are ambiguous about which pin state pairs with
    which current direction, so this is the best available reading).
    Turn-off overlap dominates the loss budget, so that edge is given a faster
    discharge path of its own. \des{D}{HVGOFF} in series with
    \des{R}{HVGOFF} bypasses $\des{R}{HVGON}$ for the discharge direction only:
    its anode sits at the gate, its cathode at the
    $\des{R}{HVGON}$/driver-output node, so it blocks during turn-on, leaving
    that edge unchanged, and conducts during turn-off. \des{R}{HVGOFF} is sized
    against the driver's \SI{1.5}{\ampere} peak source/sink capability
    (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{29}), the binding current being the brief pre-plateau
    discharge from the fully-enhanced gate, which exceeds the plateau current:
    \begin{align*}
      I_{\mathrm{gate}}^{\mathrm{peak}} &= \frac{V_{\mathrm{drive}} -
        V_{F(\des{D}{HVGOFF})}}{\des{R}{HVGOFF} + R_{\mathrm{int}}}
        = \frac{6.85 - 0.5}{3.3+1.5} \approx \SI{1.32}{\ampere}
        \quad (88\,\% \text{ of the } \SI{1.5}{\ampere} \text{ rating}),
    \end{align*}
    using $V_{F(\des{D}{HVGOFF})} \approx \SI{0.5}{\volt}$, interpolated below
    the sole tabulated point ($\SI{0.55}{\volt}$ at \SI{2}{\ampere}). The
    \SI{12}{\percent} headroom rests on that interpolation and on the
    $R_{\mathrm{int}}$ estimate; both are derived, neither measured.
    At the lower Miller-plateau voltage, the turn-off gate current is
    $I_{\mathrm{gate}} = (6.85-3.0-0.5)/(3.3+1.5) \approx \SI{698}{\milli\ampere}$,
    giving
    \begin{align*}
      t_{\mathrm{ovl}}^{\mathrm{typ}} &= \frac{Q_{GD}^{\mathrm{typ}}}{I_{\mathrm{gate}}}
        \approx \SI{36}{\nano\second}, &
      t_{\mathrm{ovl}}^{\max} &= \frac{Q_{GD}^{\max}}{I_{\mathrm{gate}}}
        \approx \SI{54}{\nano\second}.
    \end{align*}
    With the commutation voltage $V_{\mathrm{com}} = V_{\mathrm{BUS}} +
    V_{\mathrm{refl}}$, $E_{\mathrm{off}} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} I_{\mathrm{pk}}
    V_{\mathrm{com}} t_{\mathrm{ovl}}$. The convergence below uses
    $t_{\mathrm{ovl}}^{\mathrm{typ}}$; \cref{sec:hv-switch} checks
    \des{Q}{HVSW}'s dissipation at $t_{\mathrm{ovl}}^{\max}$. Turn-on is
    near-lossless in DCM (zero current; $C_{oss}$ discharge contributes a few
    milliwatts) and is governed by $\des{R}{HVGON}$ alone.
    \item[Gate drive and controller]
    $P = (Q_G f_{\mathrm{sw}} + I_Q)(V_{\mathrm{BUS}} + V_{\mathrm{refl}})$
    with $Q_G \approx \SI{40}{\nano\coulomb}$ at the \SI{6.85}{\volt} VCC rail
    (scaled from the \SI{10}{\volt} datasheet point; assumption) and controller
    operating current $I_Q \le \SI{580}{\micro\ampere}$ (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{5}). Gate
    charge is drawn through the internal VCC regulator from BIAS, which the
    charge pump of \cref{sec:hv-house-pump} lifts from the switch node, so the
    charge is drawn at the reflected shelf, well above $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$. It is
    the largest single term in the budget below.

  \item[RCD clamp] Leakage energy $\tfrac{1}{2} L_{\mathrm{lk}} I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2$
    per cycle, dissipated in \des{R}{HVCL} with the clamp-voltage multiplier
    derived in \cref{sec:hv-clamp}.
  \item[Rectifier] $V_F \approx \SI{1.0}{\volt}$ (ultrafast-diode class figure at
    $\sim$\SI{0.2}{\ampere}) times $I_{\mathrm{out}}$; recovery loss is negligible
    because in DCM the secondary current reaches zero before the switch turns on.
  \item[Secondary conduction] $I_{s,\mathrm{rms}}^2 \times \SI{1.60}{\ohm}$ DCR.
  \item[Capacitor ESR] Input $\approx\SI{40}{\milli\ohm}$ and output
  $\approx\SI{0.5}{\ohm}$, both the polymer parts' own; \des{C}{HVI2}'s
  impedance stays above the input figure below \SI{850}{\kilo\hertz}, past the
  ripple's dominant harmonics.
  \item[Core] Uncharacterized at this frequency; a \SI{50}{\milli\watt} allowance
    is carried.
\end{description}

\subsection{DCM verification}\label{sec:hv-dcm}

DCM requires $t_{\mathrm{on}} + t_{\mathrm{dem}} < T$ with margin for the drain
ring-down. The demagnetization time, referred through the 1:10 ratio, is
\begin{align*}
  t_{\mathrm{dem}} &= \frac{n L_p I_{\mathrm{pk}}}{V_{\mathrm{out}} + V_F}.
\end{align*}
The binding corner maximizes $t_{\mathrm{on}} + t_{\mathrm{dem}}$: maximum
inductance ($\SI{11}{\micro\henry}$, $+10\,\%$), minimum input
(\SI{4.75}{\volt}), design-max load:
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{pk}} &= \sqrt{\frac{2\times23.7\,\si{\micro\joule}}{\SI{11}{\micro\henry}}}
  = \SI{2.07}{\ampere},\\
  t_{\mathrm{on}} &= \frac{11\,\si{\micro\henry}\times 2.07}{4.75}
  = \SI{4.80}{\micro\second}, &
  t_{\mathrm{dem}} &= \frac{10 \times 11\,\si{\micro\henry} \times 2.07}{192.5}
  = \SI{1.19}{\micro\second},\\
  t_{\mathrm{on}} + t_{\mathrm{dem}} &= \SI{5.99}{\micro\second}
  = 0.89\,T, &
  t_{\mathrm{idle}} &= \SI{0.71}{\micro\second}.
\end{align*}

\textbf{DCM holds, but with a $10.5\,\%$ idle fraction this is the thinnest
margin in the section.} Demagnetization is set by the secondary and is
line-independent, while on-time scales as $1/V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, so the bottom of
the window stretches $t_{\mathrm{on}}$ to four times $t_{\mathrm{dem}}$ and
leaves the idle interval whatever remains. Away from this corner the margin
grows quickly: duty falls to 0.25 at \SI{13}{\volt} input, and at lighter load
the current the ballasts draw falls faster than the demagnetization slope
degrades.

Crossing into CCM would invalidate this section outright: the
energy-packet loss model, the single-pole plant of \cref{sec:hv-comp}, the
absence of rectifier recovery loss (\cref{sec:hv-rect}), and the
slope-compensation argument below all assume DCM. \SI{0.71}{\micro\second} is
also short enough that turn-on lands at an arbitrary point on the drain's
ring-down, costing a few milliwatts at most since turn-on in DCM is at zero
current.

The 0.72 duty cycle does not bear on stability: the $D > 0.5$ threshold for
subharmonic oscillation is a CCM result, depending on a peak-current
perturbation propagating into the next cycle through a nonzero starting current,
and in DCM the current returns to zero every cycle. The omission of $R_{SL}$
(\cref{sec:hv-cs}) therefore stands for as long as DCM does.

Duty at the worst corner remains under the controller's $\approx$0.91 maximum
near this frequency, and $t_{\mathrm{on}} \ge \SI{4.8}{\micro\second}$ is well
above the $\approx\SI{165}{\nano\second}$ minimum on-time (\cref{sec:lm5156h}).

\subsection{Current sensing and current limit}\label{sec:hv-cs}

\subsubsection{CS filter}\label{sec:hv-cs-filter}

With the \SI{100}{\milli\volt} (93--107) current-limit threshold of
\cref{sec:lm5156h} and \des{R}{HVCS} $= \SI{39}{\milli\ohm}$ (declared in
\cref{sec:hv-op-inputs}):
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\mathrm{typ}} &= \frac{\SI{100}{\milli\volt}}{\SI{39}{\milli\ohm}}
    = \SI{2.56}{\ampere},\\
  I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\min} &= \frac{\SI{93}{\milli\volt}}{39\,\si{\milli\ohm}\times1.01}
    = \SI{2.36}{\ampere}, &
  I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\max} &= \frac{\SI{107}{\milli\volt}}{39\,\si{\milli\ohm}\times0.99}
    = \SI{2.77}{\ampere}.
\end{align*}
The CS pin taps the sense node through a filter, $\des{R}{HVCSF}$ in series with
$\des{C}{HVCSF}$ to ground; no $R_{SL}$ is used, so the comparator's threshold
stays duty-independent. The timing criteria for that (on-time above $2R_FC_F$,
off-time above $3R_FC_F$) are met a thousandfold over at the duty extremes of
\cref{tab:hv-eta-line}, so two other requirements set $\tau =
\des{R}{HVCSF}\des{C}{HVCSF}$.

\textbf{Upper bound: limit-shift error.} The filter delays the sampled current by
$\Delta I_{\mathrm{delay}} = (V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/L_p)\,\tau$, which the controller
reads as extra peak current before its comparator trips, an apparent upward
shift of the current limit. Keeping that shift under half the
$I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\min}$--$I_{\mathrm{lim}}^{\max}$ spread above
(\SI{0.205}{\ampere} half-spread) bounds
\begin{align*}
  \tau < \frac{\SI{0.205}{\ampere}}{(V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/L_p)_{\max}}
       = \frac{\SI{0.205}{\ampere}}{\SI{1.44}{\ampere\per\micro\second}}
       = \SI{142}{\nano\second},
\end{align*}
evaluated at the fastest observed slope (\SI{13}{\volt}, \SI{9}{\micro\henry}).

\textbf{Lower bound: spike attenuation.} The leading-edge current spike the filter
exists to remove (gate current and $C_{oss}$ discharge returning through the
sense resistor at turn-on, duration tens of nanoseconds) would pass through
largely unfiltered if $\tau$ were well under that duration.

$\des{R}{HVCSF} = \SI{100}{\ohm}$ and $\des{C}{HVCSF} = \SI{470}{\pico\farad}$,
both standard values inside TI's recommended per-component ranges (\SIrange{10}{200}{\ohm},
\SIrange{100}{2000}{\pico\farad}), sit inside this window:
\begin{align*}
  \tau = \des{R}{HVCSF}\des{C}{HVCSF} = 100 \times 470\,\si{\pico\farad} = \SI{47}{\nano\second},
\end{align*}
comparable to the spike duration and well under the \SI{142}{\nano\second}
ceiling: $\Delta I_{\mathrm{delay}} \approx \SI{40}{\milli\ampere}$, under half
the tolerance-band spread. Combined with an assumed \SI{50}{\nano\second}
comparator/driver delay, total overshoot past the threshold at the fastest slope
is $\Delta I_{\mathrm{ovs}} \approx \SI{0.14}{\ampere}$, used in
\cref{sec:hv-cs-sense}.

\subsubsection{Sense resistor and limit thresholds}\label{sec:hv-cs-sense}

\des{R}{HVCS} carries the switch current to ground; with the current-limit
thresholds of \cref{sec:hv-cs-filter} and the filter's overshoot $\Delta I_{\mathrm{ovs}}
\approx \SI{0.14}{\ampere}$, the sense resistor must satisfy two opposing
constraints:
\begin{align*}
  \text{power delivery:}\quad
    R_{S} &< \frac{\SI{93}{\milli\volt}}{I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(L\min)}}
          = \frac{93\,\si{\milli\volt}}{2.29\,\si{\ampere}} = \SI{40.6}{\milli\ohm},\\
  \text{core protection:}\quad
    R_{S} &> \frac{\SI{107}{\milli\volt}}{I_{\mathrm{sat}} - \Delta I_{\mathrm{ovs}}}
          = \frac{107\,\si{\milli\volt}}{3.0 - 0.14\,\si{\ampere}} = \SI{37.4}{\milli\ohm},
\end{align*}
a \SI{3.2}{\milli\ohm} nominal window; neither bound depends on
$V_{\mathrm{out}}$. \SI{39}{\milli\ohm} (declared in \cref{sec:hv-op-inputs})
sits inside it, \SI{1.6}{\milli\ohm} under the power-delivery ceiling and
\SI{1.6}{\milli\ohm} above the core-protection floor. Both bounds are
worst-case-additive: minimum datasheet threshold, \des{R}{HVCS} at its own
tolerance extreme, and, on the power-delivery side, $L_p$ at its $-10\,\%$
extreme, all stacked simultaneously in the same direction.

Within that window \SI{39}{\milli\ohm} is the only standard value.

\textbf{Tolerance combination for this check.} Exceeding this margin damages
nothing: the cycle-by-cycle current limit terminates each switching cycle early,
delivering less energy than the voltage loop is asking for. The failure mode is
a brightness dip. Treating independent tolerance contributors as if they all
landed at their worst extreme simultaneously is needlessly pessimistic for such
a check, so root-sum-square (RSS) combination is used. \des{Q}{HVSW}'s turn-off
overlap (\cref{sec:hv-op-losses}) belongs in the same combination: the datasheet
bounds it as a part-to-part spread (\SIrange{36}{54}{\nano\second}, independent
of threshold, \des{R}{HVCS}, and $L_p$), which makes it a tolerance term like
the others.

Isolating each contributor's effect on the margin
$M = I_{\mathrm{lim,min}} - I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{(L\min)}$ one at a time from the
all-nominal baseline ($M_{\mathrm{nom}} = \SI{0.389}{\ampere}$, using
$\SI{100}{\milli\volt}/\SI{39}{\milli\ohm}$ against $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$ at
$L_p=\SI{10}{\micro\henry}$, $t_{\mathrm{ovl}}=t_{\mathrm{ovl}}^{\mathrm{typ}}$):
\begin{align*}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{threshold}} &= \SI{-0.180}{\ampere}, &
  \Delta_{R_S} &= \SI{-0.025}{\ampere}, &
  \Delta_{L_p} &= \SI{-0.118}{\ampere}, &
  \Delta_{t_{\mathrm{ovl}}} &= \SI{-0.026}{\ampere}.
\end{align*}
Worst-case-additive sums these directly: $M_{\mathrm{wc}} = 0.389 - 0.180 - 0.025
- 0.118 - 0.026 = \SI{+0.040}{\ampere}$ ($+1.7\,\%$ of $I_{\mathrm{lim,min}}$).
RSS combines the same four in quadrature:
\begin{align*}
  M_{\mathrm{RSS}} &= M_{\mathrm{nom}} - \sqrt{\Delta_{\mathrm{threshold}}^2 +
    \Delta_{R_S}^2 + \Delta_{L_p}^2 + \Delta_{t_{\mathrm{ovl}}}^2}
    = 0.389 - 0.218 = \SI{+0.171}{\ampere}
    \quad (+7.2\,\% \text{ of } I_{\mathrm{lim,min}}),
\end{align*}
The threshold specification dominates the combination. \textbf{The RSS treatment
is scoped to this one check}; every voltage- and thermal-rating margin elsewhere
remains worst-case-additive, because those checks guard against destructive
outcomes.

Both treatments leave a margin, so the result does not rest on the choice
between them; what the choice sets is how much. The margin is nonetheless the
narrowest current-mode result in the section, and what consumes it is the bias
draw of \cref{sec:hv-house-pump}: it raises input power, and with it the
commanded peak the limit must sit above.

\paragraph{What hiccup changes about that.}\label{sec:hv-cs-hiccup}
On the LM51561H, sixty-four \emph{consecutive} limited cycles trip the fault
timer (\cref{sec:hv-ss}), after which the converter stops for
\SI{219}{\milli\second} and retries. A board that exhausts this margin at the
top of its trim range on a \SI{5}{\volt} contract blinks at roughly
\SI{4.6}{\hertz}.

Three effects soften that boundary. The trip needs \emph{sustained} limiting and
eight unlimited cycles reset the count, so near the boundary the converter
limits intermittently and never reaches 64 in a row. The condition requires the
design-max envelope, the largest ballast trimmed to the top of its window
(\cref{sec:hv-budget}). And trimming down, or moving one row up
\cref{tab:hv-ballast-divider}, reduces the commanded peak directly.

\textbf{The hardware user guide must record this}: if the display blinks at the
top of the trim range on a \SI{5}{\volt} supply, the response is to trim down or
select a larger ballast, not to suspect a fault. A bench measurement of the
current limit against a real transformer would settle the margin properly and is
the most useful single measurement on this converter.

On the other side, the maximum clamped peak plus overshoot reaches
$2.77 + 0.14 = \SI{2.91}{\ampere}$ against the \SI{3.0}{\ampere} transformer
rating: a 3\,\% margin, acceptable because the \SI{3.0}{\ampere} figure is a
soft-saturation point, with $L$ already specified at \SI{9}{\micro\henry}
there.

\des{R}{HVCS} dissipation is $I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2 R_S = 1.04^2 \times 0.039 =
\SI{42}{\milli\watt}$ at the design-max envelope, trivial in a 2512.

\subsection{RCD clamp}\label{sec:hv-clamp}

At turn-off the leakage inductance ($L_{\mathrm{lk}} \le \SI{0.150}{\micro\henry}$,
\cref{sec:da2032}) carries $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$ into a winding that is not coupled
to the secondary, so \des{D}{HVCL}, \des{C}{HVCL}, and \des{R}{HVCL} give it a
destination other than the drain's parasitic capacitance: \des{D}{HVCL} steers
it into \des{C}{HVCL}, and \des{R}{HVCL} empties \des{C}{HVCL} between cycles so
the clamp settles at a steady voltage. The network returns to
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, which leaves \des{C}{HVCL} standing off only
$V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ where a return to ground would require $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} +
V_{\mathrm{cl}}$, and puts \des{R}{HVCL}'s discharge current back on the input
rail.

\subsubsection{Leakage energy and the sizing current}

Per cycle the leakage inductance delivers $E_{\mathrm{lk}} = \tfrac{1}{2}
L_{\mathrm{lk}} I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2$ into the clamp. Two currents matter:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Normal design-max operation}, $I_{\mathrm{pk}} =
    \SI{2.18}{\ampere}$ (\cref{sec:hv-op-converge}):
    $E_{\mathrm{lk}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\times\SI{0.15}{\micro\henry}\times 2.18^2
    \approx \SI{0.355}{\micro\joule}$, $E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}}
    \approx \SI{53.0}{\milli\watt}$. This sets the clamp's steady dissipation
    carried in the loss budget (\cref{sec:hv-op-losses}).
  \item \textbf{Worst case}: the current limit bounds the current at turn-off,
    so the sizing case is the guaranteed-maximum
    clamped peak including the CS filter's overshoot, \SI{2.91}{\ampere}
    (\cref{sec:hv-cs-sense}). Hiccup bounds how long the converter holds that
    peak (\cref{sec:hv-ss}) but not the peak itself.
    $E_{\mathrm{lk}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\times\SI{0.15}{\micro\henry}\times 2.91^2
    \approx \SI{0.635}{\micro\joule}$, $E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}}
    \approx \SI{94.9}{\milli\watt}$.
\end{itemize}
Clamp sizing controls a voltage rating (\des{Q}{HVSW}'s drain, below), a
destructive failure mode, so it uses the worst-case corner, per the
tolerance-treatment scoping in \cref{sec:hv-cs-sense}; the loss budget uses the
normal-operation figure.

\subsubsection{Clamp energy multiplier}

The clamp absorbs more than the bare leakage energy. While \des{D}{HVCL}
conducts, the leakage
inductor's current decays linearly from $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$ to zero, driven by the
difference between the clamped drain and the reflected secondary voltage
$V_{\mathrm{refl}} = \SI{19.25}{\volt}$ (\cref{sec:hv-op-converge}) that the
coupled winding holds at its own terminal:
\begin{align*}
  t_1 = \frac{L_{\mathrm{lk}} I_{\mathrm{pk}}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}}.
\end{align*}
The charge delivered is the area under that ramp, $Q_{\mathrm{cl}} =
\tfrac{1}{2} I_{\mathrm{pk}} t_1$, but it arrives at $V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ rather
than at the smaller $V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}$ that drives the
current down, and the two integrate the same ramp:
\begin{align*}
  E_{\mathrm{cl}} = V_{\mathrm{cl}} Q_{\mathrm{cl}}
    = \tfrac{1}{2} L_{\mathrm{lk}} I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2 \cdot
      \frac{V_{\mathrm{cl}}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}}
    = E_{\mathrm{lk}} \cdot
      \frac{V_{\mathrm{cl}}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}}.
\end{align*}
At steady state this equals \des{R}{HVCL}'s dissipation, giving the design
equation the rest of this section solves:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{V_{\mathrm{cl}}^2}{\des{R}{HVCL}}
    = E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}}
      \frac{V_{\mathrm{cl}}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}}.
\end{align*}

\subsubsection{Target clamp voltage}

$V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is bounded below by clamp effectiveness and above by switch
stress. Below roughly $2\times$ the reflected voltage, a clamp does not compress
the spike by much (conventional guidance for this topology); above, the ceiling
is set by how much of \des{Q}{HVSW}'s \SI{100}{\volt} rating
(\cref{sec:hv-op-inputs}) can be spent on $V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ once
$V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}}$ and \des{D}{HVCL}'s forward drop are also charged against
it:
\begin{align*}
  V_{DS,\mathrm{pk}} = V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}} + V_{\mathrm{cl}} + V_{F(\des{D}{HVCL})}.
\end{align*}
Semiconductor voltage stress is derated to 80\,\% of the rated maximum
throughout this section, commercial practice where no formal reliability
specification applies: $m = 20\,\%$. \des{D}{HVCL}'s forward drop is not fixed
by a part choice here, so \SI{1.5}{\volt} is assumed, toward the high end of
what small fast rectifiers exhibit at amp-level pulse currents, so the margin
below is not overstated. With $V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}} = \SI{13}{\volt}$:
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\max} = (1-m)\times\SI{100}{\volt} - V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}}
    - V_{F(\des{D}{HVCL})} = 80 - 13 - 1.5 = \SI{65.5}{\volt}.
\end{align*}
That is $3.4\times$ the reflected voltage, clear of the $2\times$ floor, so the
ceiling binds. Since the multiplier $V_{\mathrm{cl}}/(V_{\mathrm{cl}} -
V_{\mathrm{refl}})$ shrinks toward $1$ as $V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ grows, raising the
clamp voltage trades switch margin for lower loss, and the target is set at the
ceiling, $V_{\mathrm{cl}} = \SI{65.5}{\volt}$.

\subsubsection{\des{R}{HVCL}}

Solving the energy-balance equation for \des{R}{HVCL} at the target, with the
worst-case-corner clamp power:
\begin{align*}
  \des{R}{HVCL} = \frac{V_{\mathrm{cl}}(V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}})}
    {E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}}}
    = \frac{65.5 \times 46.25}{\SI{94.9}{\milli\watt}} \approx \SI{31.9}{\kilo\ohm}.
\end{align*}
Rounding down to the standard value $\des{R}{HVCL} = \SI{30}{\kilo\ohm}$
preserves margin; re-solving the same equation for the resulting clamp voltage
at each of the two operating points,
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{cl}}^2 - V_{\mathrm{refl}} V_{\mathrm{cl}}
    - E_{\mathrm{lk}} f_{\mathrm{sw}} \des{R}{HVCL} = 0
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{lim}} = \SI{63.8}{\volt}, \qquad
  V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{dm}} = \SI{50.7}{\volt}
\end{align*}
(sustained current-limit corner and normal design-max operation
respectively). At the corner, switch stress is
$V_{DS,\mathrm{pk}} = 13 + 63.8 + 1.5 = \SI{78.3}{\volt}$ against the
\SI{100}{\volt} rating: $21.7\,\%$ margin, meeting the $20\,\%$ target. In
normal operation the clamp rests at \SI{50.7}{\volt},
$2.6\times$ the reflected voltage, still above the $2\times$ effectiveness
floor, dissipating $V_{\mathrm{cl}}^2/\des{R}{HVCL} \approx
\SI{85.5}{\milli\watt}$: the figure carried in the loss budget
(\cref{sec:hv-op-losses}). At the current-limit corner dissipation rises to
\SI{136}{\milli\watt}, but hiccup holds that condition to a $0.2\,\%$ duty
(\cref{sec:hv-ss}), so its average contribution is under a milliwatt and normal
operation sets the power rating: $\ge\SI{0.18}{\watt}$ under $50\,\%$ derating.
The voltage rating is unaffected, since the corner's clamp voltage appears on
every cycle of every burst: $\ge\SI{80}{\volt}$.

\subsubsection{\des{C}{HVCL}}

Both the energy-balance equation and the switch-margin check above treat
$V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ as a single fixed number. If \des{C}{HVCL} lets the clamp
voltage ripple appreciably, the drain's actual peak stress each cycle exceeds
that number by roughly the ripple amplitude, eroding a margin that is thinnest
at the worst-case corner, so the bound is evaluated there. Bounding ripple to
$2\,\%$ of $V_{\mathrm{cl}}$ keeps the error negligible without demanding an
oversized capacitor.

The charge delivered to the clamp each cycle follows from the multiplier
relation above ($E_{\mathrm{cl}} = V_{\mathrm{cl}} Q_{\mathrm{cl}}$):
\begin{align*}
  Q_{\mathrm{cl}} = \frac{E_{\mathrm{lk}}}{V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}}
    = \frac{\SI{0.635}{\micro\joule}}{44.55} \approx \SI{14.3}{\nano\coulomb}.
\end{align*}
At a $2\,\%$ ripple target ($\Delta V \le \SI{1.28}{\volt}$ at
$V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{lim}} = \SI{63.8}{\volt}$):
\begin{align*}
  \des{C}{HVCL}^{\min} = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\Delta V}
    = \frac{\SI{14.3}{\nano\coulomb}}{\SI{1.28}{\volt}} \approx \SI{11.2}{\nano\farad}.
\end{align*}
Rounding up to $\des{C}{HVCL} = \SI{12}{\nano\farad}$ gives actual ripple
$Q_{\mathrm{cl}}/\des{C}{HVCL} \approx \SI{1.19}{\volt}$ ($1.9\,\%$) and a
discharge time constant $\tau = \des{R}{HVCL} \des{C}{HVCL} \approx
\SI{360}{\micro\second}$, about $54$ switching periods: the near-DC node the
treatment above assumes. Its voltage rating follows the corner clamp voltage,
$\ge\SI{80}{\volt}$.

\subsubsection{\des{D}{HVCL} requirements}

\des{D}{HVCL}'s requirements follow from the numbers above; no particular part
is specified:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Reverse voltage.} It must block $V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}} +
    V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{lim}} = 13 + 63.8 = \SI{76.8}{\volt}$ during the
    on-time; at the same $20\,\%$ voltage-derating standard used throughout
    this section, that requires a rated reverse voltage of at least
    \SI{96}{\volt}, i.e.\ a $\ge\SI{100}{\volt}$-class part.
  \item \textbf{Forward current.} The leakage current decays from the worst-case
    \SI{2.91}{\ampere} peak to zero over $t_1 = L_{\mathrm{lk}}
    I_{\mathrm{pk}}/(V_{\mathrm{cl}} - V_{\mathrm{refl}}) \approx
    \SI{9.8}{\nano\second}$, a \SI{14.3}{\nano\coulomb} pulse averaging
    $\approx\SI{2.1}{\milli\ampere}$ over a switching period: far short of the
    pulse widths surge ratings are specified against, so any rectifier meeting
    the voltage requirement satisfies it.
  \item \textbf{Reverse recovery.} \des{D}{HVCL} must block again before the
    next turn-on, and the off-interval never falls below
    \SI{4.0}{\micro\second} (\cref{sec:hv-dcm}). A fast
    ($\le\SI{500}{\nano\second}$) or ultrafast ($\le\SI{75}{\nano\second}$) part
    clears that; a standard-recovery part is comparable to the window itself and
    is excluded.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Switch (\des{Q}{HVSW}) stress and losses}\label{sec:hv-switch}

Drain stress is set by the clamp, and the binding case is the sustained
current-limit corner the clamp is sized at (\cref{sec:hv-clamp}):
\begin{align*}
  V_{DS,\mathrm{pk}} &= V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}} + V_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{lim}}
    + V_{F(\des{D}{HVCL})} = 13 + 63.8 + 1.5 = \SI{78.3}{\volt}
\end{align*}
a $21.7\,\%$ margin against the IRL540N's \SI{100}{\volt} rating, meeting by
construction the $20\,\%$ derating target the clamp was sized to. In normal
design-max operation the drain peaks at $13 + 50.7 + 1.5 \approx \SI{65}{\volt}$
($35\,\%$ margin), and the part is avalanche-rated besides. Peak current
\SIrange{2.2}{2.9}{\ampere} against \SI{36}{\ampere} leaves more than an order
of magnitude. The \SI{6.85}{\volt} gate rail fully enhances a logic-level gate
specified at \SI{5}{\volt}, well inside the $\pm\SI{16}{\volt}$ $V_{GS}$ rating.

Total switch dissipation at design-max, at the typical turn-off overlap
($t_{\mathrm{ovl}}^{\mathrm{typ}}=\SI{36}{\nano\second}$, \cref{sec:hv-op-losses}):
conduction \SI{60}{\milli\watt} + turn-off \SI{140}{\milli\watt} + $C_{oss}$ few
\si{\milli\watt} $\approx \SI{0.20}{\watt}$, a rise of $\approx\SI{12}{\kelvin}$
on a free-standing vertical TO-220 at $\theta_{JA} \approx
\SI{62}{\kelvin\per\watt}$. Because this check bears on thermal survival rather
than typical operation, it is repeated at the guaranteed-worst-case turn-off
overlap ($t_{\mathrm{ovl}}^{\max}=\SI{54}{\nano\second}$): conduction rises
slightly with the higher commanded peak current to \SI{62}{\milli\watt},
turn-off to \SI{215}{\milli\watt}, for a total $\approx\SI{0.28}{\watt}$ and a
rise of $\approx\SI{17}{\kelvin}$: thermally unremarkable either way.

\textbf{Turn-off overlap remains the single largest loss in the converter}
despite the speedup network. \des{D}{HVGOFF} and \des{R}{HVGOFF} cut the
effective discharge resistance from
$\des{R}{HVGON}+R_{\mathrm{int}}\approx\SI{11.5}{\ohm}$ to
$\des{R}{HVGOFF}+R_{\mathrm{int}}\approx\SI{4.8}{\ohm}$, and overlap from the
\SIrange{75}{114}{\nano\second} $\des{R}{HVGON}$ alone would leave to the
\SIrange{36}{54}{\nano\second} used throughout, at the cost of two small
through-hole parts. $\des{R}{HVGON}$ governs turn-on only, \des{D}{HVGOFF} being
reverse-biased in that direction.

\subsection{Output rectifier (\des{D}{HVRECT})}\label{sec:hv-rect}

Reverse stress occurs during the on-time:
\begin{align*}
  V_R &= V_{\mathrm{out}} + n V_{\mathrm{BUS,max}}
       = 191.5 + 10 \times 13 = \SI{321.5}{\volt}
\end{align*}
at the design-max envelope (\cref{sec:hv-achieved-range}; this rating assumes the
builder never tunes past $V_{\mathrm{max}}$, per \cref{sec:hv-divider-split}),
plus secondary-side ring, under \SI{345}{\volt} against the UF4005's
\SI{600}{\volt}: margin $\ge 42\,\%$.
Forward stress: $I_{F,\mathrm{avg}} = \SI{15}{\milli\ampere}$ (\cref{sec:hv-budget})
and $I_{F,\mathrm{pk}} = I_{\mathrm{pk}}/n = \SI{0.22}{\ampere}$ against a
\SI{1}{\ampere} average rating. Conduction loss $\approx V_F I_{\mathrm{out}}
= \SI{15}{\milli\watt}$. The $\approx\SI{75}{\nano\second}$ recovery is
immaterial: in DCM the diode current reaches zero and the junction sweeps out
during the idle interval, so the switch never hard-commutates it.

\subsection{Input and output capacitance}\label{sec:hv-caps}

\subsubsection{Input}

\des{C}{HVI1} (\SI{100}{\micro\farad}, radial polarized, polymer aluminum) is the
bulk reservoir for the pulsed primary current; \des{C}{HVI2}
(\SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} ceramic) closes the high-frequency primary loop;
\des{C}{HVBIAS} serves the BIAS pin (\cref{sec:hv-house-bias}). The primary
draws triangular pulses of RMS \SI{1.04}{\ampere} (\cref{sec:hv-op-converge})
against a \SI{0.74}{\ampere} DC average, leaving $\sqrt{1.04^2 - 0.74^2} =
\SI{0.73}{\ampere}$ RMS of ripple in the capacitor bank. A polymer part carries
that at milliohm ESR; \textbf{a general-purpose aluminum electrolytic would not:
its \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz}
ripple rating (typically \SIrange{0.3}{0.5}{\ampere} for a small
\SI{100}{\micro\farad} can) is marginal even with the ceramic sharing current,
so the polymer family is a requirement here.} Bus ripple:
\begin{align*}
  \Delta V \approx
  \frac{(I_{\mathrm{pk}}/2 - I_{\mathrm{in}})\,t_{\mathrm{on}}}{C_{\mathrm{in}}}
  + I_{\mathrm{pk}}\,\mathrm{ESR}
  \approx \frac{\SI{1.57}{\micro\coulomb}}{\SI{105}{\micro\farad}} + 2.18\times0.04
  \approx \SI{102}{\milli\volt}\ \text{pk--pk},
\end{align*}
unobjectionable on a USB-PD input.

\subsubsection{Output}

$C_{\mathrm{out}} = \des{C}{HVO1}\,(\SI{4.7}{\micro\farad}$, radial polarized, polymer
aluminum$) + \des{C}{HVO2}\,(\SI{100}{\nano\farad}\ \text{film})$.

The load is constant-current tubes behind large ballasts, so the rail needs
neither hold-up nor transient reservoir, only switching-ripple control.
Capacitive ripple per cycle:
\begin{align*}
  \Delta V_{\mathrm{cap}} &=
  \frac{I_{\mathrm{out}}\,(T - t_{\mathrm{dem}})}{C_{\mathrm{out}}}
  = \frac{15.4\,\si{\milli\ampere} \times 5.6\,\si{\micro\second}}
         {\SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}}
  = \SI{18}{\milli\volt},
\end{align*}
exceeded by the ESR step $I_{s,\mathrm{pk}}\times\mathrm{ESR} \approx 0.22 \times
0.5 \approx \SI{108}{\milli\volt}$ pk--pk ($0.06\,\%$ of the rail) at
\des{C}{HVO1}'s $\approx\SI{0.5}{\ohm}$ ESR, with the film part shunting the
fast edges.
Through a \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm} ballast (\cref{sec:hv-load-digit}) this modulates
tube current by $\sim$\SI{11}{\micro\ampere} at \SI{150}{\kilo\hertz},
optically invisible. The small capacitance is sufficient, and beneficial: it
keeps the stored energy at \SI{69}{\milli\joule}, the start-up rise time at
\SI{17}{\milli\second} (\cref{sec:hv-ss}), and the bleed-down inside seconds
(\cref{sec:hv-bleeder}).

\textbf{\des{C}{HVO1}'s voltage rating must be specified deliberately.} Polymer
aluminum parts come at a fixed ladder of ratings, and the \SI{160}{\volt} step
falls short of the \SI{191.5}{\volt} design-max envelope
(\cref{sec:hv-achieved-range}), so \SI{250}{\volt} is the requirement,
$\ge$23\,\% headroom at full trim. The \SI{0.5}{\ohm} ESR assumed above is
carried as a selection requirement on the part.

\subsection{Bleeder (\des{R}{HVBLEED}) and minimum load}\label{sec:hv-bleeder}

\des{R}{HVBLEED} serves two requirements: it must discharge $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ to
a safe voltage within a bounded time after shutdown, and it must keep the
converter loaded enough to avoid pulse-skipping at light load.

\subsubsection{Discharge}\label{sec:hv-bleeder-discharge}

Stored energy is modest, $\tfrac{1}{2} C_{\mathrm{out}} V^2 =
\tfrac{1}{2}\times\SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}\times 170^2 = \SI{69}{\milli\joule}$
(\cref{sec:hv-caps}), but the node must still bleed down after shutdown.

IEC 62368-1 classifies $\le\SI{60}{\volt}\,\mathrm{dc}$ as ES1, safe for an
ordinary person to touch, and requires a capacitor's stored voltage to decay to
that limit within \SI{2}{\second} of disconnection. The tubes do most of the
work themselves, each conducting branch behaving as its ballast in series with
its own maintaining voltage and so pulling the rail toward that voltage until it
stops conducting. The digits go first, at
$\SI{145}{\volt} + \SI{2.5}{\volt} \approx \SI{148}{\volt}$, four
\SI{10}{\kilo\ohm} ballasts taking the rail there in \SI{28}{\milli\second}.
The colons carry it the rest of the way: with the INS-1 maintaining at or below
\SI{55}{\volt} (\cref{sec:ins1}) and its cathode grounded, the colon branch
stops \emph{below} the \SI{60}{\volt} ES1 limit. The tubes alone therefore
determine whether the limit is reached, and the resistive path
$\des{R}{HVBLEED} \parallel R_{\mathrm{div}}$ sets only how fast.

Two \SI{240}{\kilo\ohm} ballasts against $\des{R}{HVBLEED} = \SI{1.5}{\mega\ohm}$
in parallel with the feedback divider ($R_{\mathrm{div}} \approx
\SI{1.70}{\mega\ohm}$, \cref{sec:hv-divider}) give
\begin{align*}
  \des{R}{HVBLEED} \parallel R_{\mathrm{div}} &= 1.5\,\si{\mega\ohm} \parallel
    1.70\,\si{\mega\ohm} = \SI{797}{\kilo\ohm}, \\
  \tau &= \frac{C_{\mathrm{out}}}{2/\SI{240}{\kilo\ohm} + 1/\SI{797}{\kilo\ohm}}
       = \SI{0.50}{\second}, &
  V_\infty &= \frac{2\times\SI{55}{\volt}/\SI{240}{\kilo\ohm}}
                   {2/\SI{240}{\kilo\ohm} + 1/\SI{797}{\kilo\ohm}}
            = \SI{47.8}{\volt},\\
  t_{148\rightarrow 60\,\mathrm{V}} &= \tau
    \ln\frac{148 - 47.8}{60 - 47.8} = \SI{1.05}{\second}.
\end{align*}
Total elapsed time from shutdown to \SI{60}{\volt} is
$0.03 + 1.05 \approx \SI{1.08}{\second}$, a \SI{46}{\percent} margin under IEC
62368-1's \SI{2}{\second} window. That figure is for a populated board. With no
tubes in their sockets the resistive path is the whole discharge and takes
\SI{4.0}{\second}, which is the condition a builder is in while bench-trimming
the rail with the tubes removed (\cref{sec:hv-divider-split}).

The discharge requirement does not select \des{R}{HVBLEED} here. Even the
divider alone, with no bleeder at all, reaches \SI{60}{\volt} in
\SI{1.33}{\second}; what sets the value is the minimum-load requirement below,
which caps \des{R}{HVBLEED} at \SI{2.47}{\mega\ohm}. \SI{1.5}{\mega\ohm} is the
standard value under that cap, and clears both requirements.
Dissipation, $170^2/\SI{1.5}{\mega\ohm} = \SI{19}{\milli\watt}$ at nominal and
\SI{24}{\milli\watt} at the design-max envelope, is negligible against any axial
part. The standing voltage is the constraint. \des{R}{HVBLEED} sits across the
whole rail, so the figure that matters is the design-max envelope's
\SI{191.5}{\volt}, and the \SI{20}{\percent} derating applied elsewhere in this
section puts the requirement at $\ge\SI{239}{\volt}$ of limiting element
voltage. That is past
what a DIN0207 element is normally rated for, so a DIN0309 body is specified
--- the only resistor in this section selected on element voltage.

\subsubsection{Minimum load}\label{sec:hv-bleeder-minload}

The controller cannot deliver less than one minimum-width pulse per cycle. At
\SI{13}{\volt} input and the $\approx\SI{165}{\nano\second}$ minimum on-time:
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{\min} &= \frac{V_{\mathrm{BUS}} t_{\mathrm{on,min}}}{L_p}
    = \frac{13 \times 165\,\si{\nano\second}}{\SI{10}{\micro\henry}}
    = \SI{0.21}{\ampere},\\
  P_{\min} &= \tfrac{1}{2} L_p (I_{\mathrm{pk}}^{\min})^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}\,\eta
    \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\times\SI{10}{\micro\henry}\times 0.046 \times
      149.4\,\si{\kilo\hertz} \times 0.833
      \approx \SI{29}{\milli\watt}.
\end{align*}
Below this load the output would creep upward, or the controller would skip
pulses. The fixed preload, bleeder $170^2/\SI{1.5}{\mega\ohm} =
\SI{19}{\milli\watt}$ plus divider \SI{17}{\milli\watt}
(\cref{sec:hv-divider}), totals \SI{36}{\milli\watt}, a $1.26\times$ margin over
$P_{\min}$: the rail stays regulated even before the tubes strike. This is the
binding requirement on \des{R}{HVBLEED}: the divider's \SI{17}{\milli\watt}
alone falls short, and covering the remainder takes
$170^2/\SI{12}{\milli\watt} = \SI{2.47}{\mega\ohm}$ or less.

\subsection{Loop compensation}\label{sec:hv-comp}

\subsubsection{Plant}

A peak-current-mode DCM flyback is, to first order, a controlled current source
into $C_{\mathrm{out}} \parallel R_L$. At the nominal point $R_L =
V_{\mathrm{out}}/I_{\mathrm{out}} = 170/\SI{10.77}{\milli\ampere} =
\SI{15.8}{\kilo\ohm}$ (\cref{sec:hv-budget}) and $C_{\mathrm{out}} =
\des{C}{HVO1} + \des{C}{HVO2} = \SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}$ (\cref{sec:hv-caps}).

Modulator gain: the controller's PWM comparator sees COMP through its internal
attenuation $G_{\mathrm{COMP}} = 0.142$ and compares it against the sensed current
plus the fixed internal slope ramp of slope $S_e = \SI{40}{\milli\volt}\times
f_{\mathrm{sw}} = \SI{5975}{\volt\per\second}$ (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{20}). Against
the sensed on-slope $S_n = R_S V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/L_p = 0.039\times9/\SI{10}{\micro\henry}
= \SI{35100}{\volt\per\second}$ (\cref{sec:hv-cs}):
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\partial I_{\mathrm{pk}}}{\partial v_c}
    &= \frac{G_{\mathrm{COMP}}}{R_S\,(1 + S_e/S_n)}
     = \frac{0.142}{0.039 \times 1.17}
     = \SI{3.11}{\ampere\per\volt}.
\end{align*}
Since $I_{\mathrm{out}} = \tfrac{1}{2} L_p I_{\mathrm{pk}}^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}
\eta / V_{\mathrm{out}}$, the plant transconductance and its output resistance
follow:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\partial I_{\mathrm{out}}}{\partial I_{\mathrm{pk}}}
    &= \frac{2 I_{\mathrm{out}}}{I_{\mathrm{pk}}}
     = \frac{2 \times 10.77\,\si{\milli\ampere}}{1.75}
     = 12.3\,\si{\milli\ampere\per\ampere},\\
     g_{ps} &= 12.3\,\si{\milli\ampere\per\ampere} \times \SI{3.11}{\ampere\per\volt}
     = \SI{38.3}{\milli\siemens} \text{ (per volt of COMP)},
\end{align*}
and because $I_{\mathrm{out}} \propto 1/V_{\mathrm{out}}$ at fixed peak current,
the converter presents its own output resistance equal to $R_L$, so the loaded
plant sees $R_L/2$ and one dominant pole:
\begin{align*}
  G_0 &= g_{ps}\,\frac{R_L}{2} = 0.0383 \times 7890 = 302\ (\SI{49.6}{\decibel}),
  &
  f_p &= \frac{2}{2\pi R_L C_{\mathrm{out}}}
       = \frac{2}{2\pi \times 15.8\,\si{\kilo\ohm} \times \SI{4.8}{\micro\farad}}
       = \SI{4.2}{\hertz}.
\end{align*}
The DCM right-half-plane zero and the output capacitor's ESR zero
($\approx\SI{67}{\kilo\hertz}$ at \des{C}{HVO1}'s \SI{0.5}{\ohm}) both lie far
above the crossover derived below and are neglected.

\subsubsection{Compensator and loop}

The target crossover $f_c$ sits above the plant pole $f_p = \SI{4.2}{\hertz}$
and below $f_{\mathrm{sw}}/10 \approx \SI{15}{\kilo\hertz}$, standard
peak-current-mode practice. The load is quasi-static (no multiplexing, each tube
has a dedicated driver, so digit changes move milliamps at minutes cadence) and
the input rail is pre-regulated USB-PD, so nothing requires pushing $f_c$ toward
that upper bound; a target in the low hundreds of hertz is adopted, with a
standard Type-II placement: the zero near $0.7$--$0.8\,f_c$ to boost phase at
crossover, the high-frequency pole at least $10\times$ above $f_c$ to attenuate
switching ripple on COMP.

The compensator's mid-band gain, set by $g_m \des{R}{HVCOMP}$ with the
\SI{2}{\milli\siemens} transconductance error amplifier (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{6}),
fixes where the loop crosses 0\,dB against the plant and divider gains above,
and the $\des{R}{HVCOMP}\des{C}{HVC1}$ and $\des{C}{HVC1}/\des{C}{HVC2}$ ratios
fix the zero and high-frequency pole. Standard values that realize the target
are $\des{R}{HVCOMP} = \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm}$ in series with $\des{C}{HVC1} =
\SI{100}{\nano\farad}$ on COMP, shunted by $\des{C}{HVC2} =
\SI{220}{\pico\farad}$:
\begin{align*}
  f_z &= \frac{1}{2\pi \des{R}{HVCOMP} \des{C}{HVC1}} = \SI{159}{\hertz},
  &
  f_{hp} &= \frac{1}{2\pi \des{R}{HVCOMP}\,\frac{\des{C}{HVC1}\des{C}{HVC2}}{\des{C}{HVC1}+\des{C}{HVC2}}}
          = \SI{72.5}{\kilo\hertz},
\end{align*}
with integrator behavior below $f_z$ (the amplifier's
$g_m R_O = 2\,\si{\milli\siemens} \times 10\,\si{\mega\ohm} = \SI{86}{\decibel}$
DC gain, \ds{lm5156h.pdf}{25}, makes static regulation error negligible). The
divider contributes $H = V_{\mathrm{REF}}/V_{\mathrm{out}} = 1/170$.

Solving $|T(f)| = 1$ for the product of divider, compensator, and plant
(numerically, with all three break frequencies in place) confirms the target:
\begin{align*}
  f_c &\approx \SI{193}{\hertz} \approx \frac{f_{\mathrm{sw}}}{772},\\
  \varphi_m &= 180^\circ - \arctan\frac{f_c}{f_p} - 90^\circ
    + \arctan\frac{f_c}{f_z} - \arctan\frac{f_c}{f_{hp}}
    = 180 - 88.8 - 90 + 50.5 - 0.2 \approx 52^\circ.
\end{align*}
The zero lands at $0.82 f_c$ and the high-frequency pole $375\times$ above
crossover, both inside their targets, and the phase never approaches
$-180^\circ$ until far past $f_{hp}$, where loop gain is tens of dB down. The
modulator constants ($G_{\mathrm{COMP}}$, $S_e$, $g_m$) are datasheet typicals
without tolerance bands; a bench transient or Bode check should verify them.

\subsection{Soft start}\label{sec:hv-ss}

The datasheet's soft-start formulas apply only to boost and SEPIC topologies;
the boost formula assumes an input-to-output pass-through path a flyback does
not have. From the pin's behavior (\cref{sec:lm5156h}): \des{C}{HVSS} is charged
by $I_{SS} = \SI{10}{\micro\ampere}$, and the error amplifier regulates FB to
the lower of $V_{SS}$ and $V_{\mathrm{REF}}$, giving a reference-ramp time
$t_{SS} = \des{C}{HVSS} V_{\mathrm{REF}} / I_{SS}$.

Whether the output tracks that ramp depends on whether the converter can deliver
enough current to keep up. Running flat out at its own current limit, it charges
$C_{\mathrm{out}}$ from \SI{0}{\volt} to $V_{\mathrm{out}}$ (the tubes draw
nothing until strike, so no load current competes) in
\begin{align*}
  t_{\mathrm{rise}} &\approx
  \frac{\tfrac{1}{2} C_{\mathrm{out}} V_{\mathrm{out}}^2}
       {\tfrac{1}{2} L_p I_{\mathrm{lim}}^2 f_{\mathrm{sw}}\,\eta}
  = \frac{\SI{69}{\milli\joule}}{\SI{4.1}{\watt}}
  \approx \SI{17.0}{\milli\second}.
\end{align*}
$\des{C}{HVSS}$ must be large enough that $t_{SS}$ exceeds $t_{\mathrm{rise}}$;
otherwise the output falls behind the commanded ramp and the error amplifier
saturates into the current limit. Setting $t_{SS} = t_{\mathrm{rise}}$ gives the
minimum:
\begin{align*}
  \des{C}{HVSS}^{\min} &= \frac{t_{\mathrm{rise}} I_{SS}}{V_{\mathrm{REF}}}
  = \frac{\SI{17.0}{\milli\second} \times \SI{10}{\micro\ampere}}{\SI{1.00}{\volt}}
  \approx \SI{170}{\nano\farad}.
\end{align*}
\paragraph{Hiccup makes the sizing mandatory, and the margin larger.}
The LM51561H specified here enables hiccup mode overload protection, which the
LM5156H option omits (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{2}). A fault timer counts current-limited
cycles, but only once soft start has finished; 64 of them stop the converter and
pull SS down, it restarts after 32{,}768 cycles, and eight consecutive
unlimited cycles reset the count (\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{6,25}). At
\SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz} that is a \SI{428}{\micro\second} fault window and a
\SI{219}{\milli\second} retry interval, a fault duty of $0.2\,\%$. The datasheet
states the requirement directly: soft start must be long enough not to trigger
hiccup once it finishes.

Because the converter charges $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ at its current limit, an
output that has not arrived by the end of soft start is by definition still
current-limited, and \SI{428}{\micro\second} later the converter hiccups. So
$t_{SS}$ must exceed not $t_{\mathrm{rise}}$ but $t_{\mathrm{rise}}$ at its own
worst case. That figure carries real spread: $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ at $+20\,\%$,
the current limit at its \SI{93}{\milli\volt} minimum (which enters squared),
and efficiency at the pessimistic turn-off overlap together give
$t_{\mathrm{rise}}^{\max} \approx \SI{24.5}{\milli\second}$ against the
\SI{17.0}{\milli\second} nominal.

Sizing against the nominal figure alone would select \SI{220}{\nano\farad},
whose worst-case $t_{SS}$ is \SI{17.8}{\milli\second}: seven milliseconds short,
and a board at that corner hiccups on every power-up. $\des{C}{HVSS} = \SI{470}{\nano\farad}$ is specified instead,
giving $t_{SS} = \SI{47.0}{\milli\second}$ nominal. Stacking
$\des{C}{HVSS}$'s own tolerance ($\pm10\,\%$, typical for this class of ceramic)
against $I_{SS}$ and $V_{\mathrm{REF}}$'s datasheet ranges
(\SIrange{9}{11}{\micro\ampere} and \SIrange{0.99}{1.01}{\volt},
\ds{lm5156h.pdf}{6}), worst-case-additive:
\begin{align*}
  t_{SS}^{\min} &= \frac{\SI{470}{\nano\farad}\times0.9 \times \SI{0.99}{\volt}}
  {\SI{11}{\micro\ampere}} \approx \SI{38.1}{\milli\second},
\end{align*}
\SI{13.5}{\milli\second} clear of $t_{\mathrm{rise}}^{\max}$. The full range is
\SIrange{38.1}{58.0}{\milli\second}, imperceptible next to the tubes' own
warm-up.

Hiccup protection is wanted here. The failure it guards against, a sustained
short or a shorted tube socket during assembly, would otherwise hold
\des{Q}{HVSW} and \des{T}{HV} at the current limit indefinitely
(\cref{sec:hv-clamp}).

\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:hv-summary}

Assumptions carried through this section: driver on-drop
\SIrange{1.0}{2.5}{\volt} at operating currents (spec point is
\SI{7}{\milli\ampere}); IRL540N commodity-typical $R_{DS(\mathrm{on})}$ and $Q_G$
figures ($Q_{GD}$ and the resulting turn-off overlap are sourced from the
datasheet instead, \cref{sec:hv-op-losses}); \des{D}{HVGOFF}'s forward drop
($\approx\SI{0.5}{\volt}$, estimated from its single tabulated point at
\SI{2}{\ampere} since no lower-current value is given, \cref{sec:hv-op-losses});
\des{D}{HVCL}'s forward drop (\SI{1.5}{\volt} assumed, toward the high
end so drain-stress margin is not overstated, \cref{sec:hv-clamp});
core-loss allowance \SI{50}{\milli\watt} (DA2032 uncharacterized at
\SI{149.4}{\kilo\hertz}); comparator delay \SI{50}{\nano\second}; the VCC
regulator's dropout below its \SI{8}{\volt} specification point
(\cref{sec:hv-house-pump}); and \des{Q}{HVSW}'s output capacitance, left
unquantified here, which sets the drain ring-down interval that
\cref{sec:hv-dcm}'s idle margin must cover.

\paragraph{The largest identified efficiency lever.}\label{sec:hv-summary-lever}
The bias draw and turn-off overlap together account for
\SIrange{297}{400}{\milli\watt} of a \SIrange{578}{612}{\milli\watt} budget
across the input window, and both scale with \des{Q}{HVSW}'s gate charge: the
first through $Q_G$ directly, the second through $Q_{GD}$. The charge pump
(\cref{sec:hv-house-pump}) supplies that charge from the reflected shelf,
\SIrange{24}{32}{\volt} against the input's \SIrange{4.75}{13}{\volt}, so gate
charge is worth roughly three times what it would be on a converter biased
directly from its input. A through-hole
logic-level switch of the same voltage class at \SI{20}{\nano\coulomb} rather
than \SI{40}{\nano\coulomb}, and at comparable $R_{DS(\mathrm{on})}$, would move
design-max efficiency from \SI{83.3}{\percent} to about \SI{87.1}{\percent}. The IRL540N is
specified because that combination is not sourceable in a through-hole package,
which this design requires. Nothing above depends on the choice, since in DCM
the energy packet sets the peak current.

  single fixed ballast holds rated current across the required trim range, so
  ballast and \des{R}{HVD3} are selected as a pair from
  \cref{tab:hv-ballast-divider}. The

Three entries carry a requirement that a part chosen on value alone will miss.
\des{D}{HVSTART}'s forward drop is what keeps BIAS above the controller's
minimum during start-up at the bottom of the input window, so a silicon
rectifier substituted for the Schottky does not work. The three
charge-metering capacitors, \des{C}{HVPUMP}, \des{C}{HVCSF}, and
\des{C}{HVCL}, are specified by dielectric as well as by value: each sets a
charge or a time constant that a class-II ceramic's voltage coefficient would
move with operating point. And \des{R}{HVBLEED} is a safety part, setting the
discharge time \cref{sec:hv-bleeder-discharge} holds against IEC 62368-1, so a
substitution must carry its element voltage rating unchanged.
