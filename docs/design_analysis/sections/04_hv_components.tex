\section{170~V converter: driving component characteristics}
\label{sec:hv-components}

The 170~V anode rail is produced by a current-mode flyback built
around a TI LM51561H controller and a Coilcraft DA2032-AL flyback
transformer. This section records the datasheet characteristics the
converter derivation of \cref{sec:hv-converter} draws on; that section
assigns the designators.

\paragraph{Current limit has no slope compensation as designed.} The
100~mV current-limit threshold is a fixed DC level at the CS pin; the
datasheet's peak-current-limit equation only subtracts a
slope-compensation term if the optional external \(R_{SL}\) resistor
is fitted, and TI recommends omitting it for accurate current
limiting versus \(V_{IN}\). A separate, always-on slope ramp
(\(V_{SLOPE}=40\,\text{mV}\times f_{RT}/f_{SYNC}\)) is summed only into
the regulation (PWM/COMP) comparator, to prevent subharmonic
oscillation (a different comparator from the current-limit path).
\Cref{sec:hv-cs} sizes the current-sense resistor against the flat
100~mV threshold, not a slope-adjusted figure.

\paragraph{No flyback-specific soft-start formula.} The datasheet
gives soft-start timing equations for boost and SEPIC topologies only.
Its own non-isolated flyback reference design (Fig.\ 10--12) wires
FB/SS/COMP the same way as the boost case, but the boost formula
assumes an input-to-output pass-through path that a flyback
transformer does not provide; \cref{sec:hv-ss} therefore derives
soft-start timing from the SS pin's charging mechanism rather than
from either published formula. On the LM51561H that timing is not a
convenience: the datasheet requires soft start to outlast the output's rise,
or hiccup trips on every power-up.

\paragraph{EN and UVLO are distinct comparators on one pin.} Pin 14
(``EN/UVLO/SYNC'') carries two separate thresholds: EN (\(\approx\)0.5~V)
gates shutdown$\leftrightarrow$standby, and UVLO
(\(\approx\)1.5~V/1.45~V) gates standby$\leftrightarrow$run. An
external resistor divider from the input rail to this pin, sized
against the UVLO threshold and hysteresis current, sets the
converter's input undervoltage lockout point.

\paragraph{Maximum duty cycle bounds the achievable turns ratio.} The
frequency-dependent \(D_{MAX}\) limit (interpolated to roughly
91--92\% typical near 150~kHz) caps how much the transformer's
reflected voltage can dominate the switching waveform at minimum input
voltage; \cref{sec:hv-dcm} checks the design's duty cycle against this
limit.

Pin assignment: secondary on pins 1 and 12, primary windings on pins
3--10; dot marks pin 1. No recommended operating frequency range is
published beyond the 100~kHz condition used for the \(L_P\) and
interwinding-capacitance measurements; the 150~kHz operating point
used in this design is not independently characterized in the
datasheet, and \cref{sec:hv-converter} notes this as an extrapolation.
No direct saturation current is published either; the datasheet's
proxy is the ``inductance at \(I_{pk}\)'' figure (\SI{9.0}{\micro\henry}
min at \SI{3.0}{\ampere}), which this document treats as the effective
peak-current rating. The isolation ratings (1500~V\textsubscript{rms}
winding-to-winding, 750~V\textsubscript{rms} winding-to-core) presume
an isolated application. Tying the secondary return to the primary
ground forgoes that isolation, and nothing in the datasheet cautions
against doing so: the windings stay galvanically separate whatever the
secondary return is referenced to.
