\section{MCU and digital interfaces}
\label{sec:esp32-mcu}

The board's control logic is an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 module. It drives the BCD
inputs of the four decoder/drivers (\cref{sec:bcd-driver}), reads the flyback's
power-good signal (\cref{sec:hv-house-pgood}), and exposes a serial port for
firmware download and console access. This section records the module's
characteristics and those of the logic buffer that isolates that port, then
derives the three digital interfaces the MCU presents to the rest of the board.
The module's supply current is the 3.3~V rail's dominant load and is carried
forward to \cref{sec:3v3-converter}.

\paragraph{Not specified in this module datasheet.} Brown-out/POR
threshold for VDD33 undervoltage detection, any GPIO absolute
maximum voltage beyond the recommended \(V_{IH}\) maximum
(\(V_{DD}+0.3\)~V), any aggregate limit on total GPIO current summed
across pins, and any characterization of \(V_{OL}\) or \(V_{OH}\) at
a defined load current. The first two live in the ESP32-S3 chip-level
datasheet, which this module document references but does not
reproduce; the load-current behavior of the output stage is addressed
by assumption in \cref{sec:esp32-bcd}.

\paragraph{GPIO pins are not tolerant of voltages above the supply.} The
recommended input maximum is \(V_{DD}+0.3\)~V, the same ceiling as VDD33's
own absolute maximum, so a pin driven above roughly \SI{3.6}{\volt} forward-biases
its clamp structure into the supply rail. This governs every external
interface the module presents and is the requirement
\cref{sec:esp32-uart} exists to satisfy.

\paragraph{The regulator must tolerate the full Wi-Fi TX transient,
not just steady-state draw.} Espressif's stated minimum external
supply-current capability (\(\geq\)0.5~A) follows from the 355~mA
Wi-Fi TX peak plus margin; a converter sized only for the module's
idle/modem-sleep current (tens of mA) would brown out during a
transmit burst. \Cref{sec:3v3-converter} sizes the 3.3~V rail against
this requirement.

Four of these figures are the ones the interface depends on, and a substitute
part must hold all four: an input recommended to \SI{5.5}{\volt}
independent of \(V_{CC}\); a power-down leakage specified with
\SI{5.5}{\volt} applied to either terminal at \(V_{CC}=0\), which is what
closes the back-drive path into an unpowered board; Schmitt-trigger
thresholds with hysteresis; and a push-pull output.

\paragraph{Input tolerance and output tolerance are not symmetric.} The input
is recommended to \SI{5.5}{\volt} in all supply states. The output is
recommended only to \(V_{CC}\) while the part is powered, and to
\SI{5.5}{\volt} only when \(V_{CC}=0\). An output driven above its own supply
while the part is running forward-biases into the supply rail, which is why
the outbound line of \cref{sec:esp32-uart} carries a series resistor and the
inbound line does not.

\subsection{BCD drive interface}
\label{sec:esp32-bcd}

Sixteen GPIOs drive the four decoder/drivers' BCD inputs, four per package.
The MCU runs at \SIrange{3.0}{3.6}{\volt} and the drivers are 5~V TTL parts
(\cref{sec:bcd-driver}), so both logic levels must be checked in the
driving direction. The checks below use the MCU's own recommended supply
minimum, \SI{3.0}{\volt}, rather than the narrower range the 3.3~V rail
achieves, so the result holds anywhere in the module's specified supply
window.

\paragraph{High level.} The MCU must hold a driver input above the TTL
\(V_{IH}\) of \SI{2.0}{\volt} while sourcing that input's high-level current,
at most \SI{80}{\micro\ampere} on the B, C, and D inputs:
\begin{align*}
  V_{OH} &\geq 0.8\,V_{DD} = 0.8\times\SI{3.0}{\volt} = \SI{2.40}{\volt}
  \quad\text{against}\quad V_{IH} = \SI{2.0}{\volt},
\end{align*}
a \SI{0.40}{\volt} margin at the supply floor, widening to
\SI{0.64}{\volt} at \SI{3.3}{\volt}. The \(V_{OH}\) figure is specified into a
high-impedance load; \SI{80}{\micro\ampere} is \(0.2\,\%\) of the pin's
\SI{40}{\milli\ampere} source rating, so the specification applies without
correction. This is the direction that fails in general when 3.3~V logic
drives 5~V parts, and it passes here only because TTL thresholds are
referenced to fixed voltages rather than to a fraction of the 5~V supply.

\paragraph{Low level.} The MCU must hold a driver input below the TTL
\(V_{IL}\) of \SI{0.8}{\volt} while sinking that input's low-level current, at
most \SI{3.2}{\milli\ampere} on the B, C, and D inputs. The module's
\(V_{OL}\) specification is given only into a high-impedance load, and
\SI{3.2}{\milli\ampere} is not that, so the requirement is restated as a bound
on the pin's effective pull-down impedance:
\begin{align*}
  R_{\mathrm{pd}} &\leq \frac{\SI{0.8}{\volt}}{\SI{3.2}{\milli\ampere}}
    = \SI{250}{\ohm}.
\end{align*}
\emph{Assumption:} the pin's \SI{28}{\milli\ampere} sink rating implies a
pull-down impedance far below that bound. Reading the rating pessimistically,
as the pin only just meeting its \(0.1\,V_{DD}\) output specification at the
full rated sink current, gives \(0.30/0.028 \approx \SI{11}{\ohm}\) and a
low-level output of \SI{35}{\milli\volt} at \SI{3.2}{\milli\ampere}, an order
of magnitude inside the requirement. The interface is not sensitive to this
assumption: it survives any pull-down impedance up to \SI{250}{\ohm}.

\paragraph{Aggregate low-level current.} Worst case, all sixteen lines low:
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{BCD,sink}} &= 4 \times \left(\SI{1.6}{\milli\ampere}
    + 3\times\SI{3.2}{\milli\ampere}\right) = \SI{44.8}{\milli\ampere},
\end{align*}
sourced from the 5~V rail through the drivers' input structures and returned
through the MCU's ground. It is not a load on the 3.3~V rail. It is also
already inside the drivers' own supply-current figure rather than additional
to it, because that figure's test condition is inputs grounded
(\cref{sec:bcd-driver}), which is the same all-inputs-low state: the 5~V
budget of \cref{sec:5v-load} therefore counts it once, not zero times or
twice. Per pin, \SI{3.2}{\milli\ampere} against a \SI{28}{\milli\ampere}
rating is unremarkable; the module datasheet publishes no aggregate GPIO
current limit against which to check the \SI{44.8}{\milli\ampere} total, so
none is claimed.

\paragraph{Interface state before firmware runs.} MCU GPIOs are
high-impedance from reset until firmware configures them, leaving the driver
inputs floating. A floating TTL input reads high, so all four BCD inputs read
as binary 1111, which the decoder maps to an all-outputs-off state
(\cref{sec:bcd-driver}): the tubes are blanked rather than displaying an
arbitrary digit. Floating inputs are noise-sensitive and the displayed state
during this window is not guaranteed, but no state the decoder can reach
exceeds a rating, and the window closes when firmware drives the lines.

\paragraph{GPIO count.} Nineteen GPIOs are required: sixteen for the BCD
inputs, two for the serial port (\cref{sec:esp32-uart}), and one input for
power-good (\cref{sec:esp32-pgood}). Assignment must avoid holding any
boot-strapping pin at a level that selects an unintended boot mode through
the \SI{3}{\milli\second} strapping hold time after reset release; the count
is far below the module's available pins, so this constrains which pins are
chosen rather than whether enough exist.

\subsection{Serial header}
\label{sec:esp32-uart}

J501 exposes the MCU's UART (universal asynchronous receiver/transmitter) for
firmware download and console access, driven by a commodity USB-to-serial
adapter. The header's supply pin feeds the 3.3~V converter's input and is
treated in \cref{sec:3v3-diodeor}; this subsection covers the two signal
lines.

\paragraph{The signal requirement is set by the adapter population, not by
this design.} USB-to-serial adapters ship with signaling referenced either to
3.3~V or to 5~V, and on the common bridge families that selection is not
independent of the supply pin's setting. The MCU's pins are not tolerant above
\(V_{DD}+0.3\)~V (\cref{sec:esp32}), so an adapter signaling at 5~V drives a
GPIO roughly \SI{1.4}{\volt} past its recommended maximum. Neither a series
resistor nor any choice of power topology addresses this: the adapter's
transmit line reaches the MCU pin directly, and a resistor bounds the
resulting clamp current without bringing the pin voltage back inside its
rating. The requirement is therefore that no external signal reach an MCU pin
directly.

\paragraph{Buffers.} Both lines pass through 74LVC1G17-class buffers
(\cref{sec:lvc1g17}): U502 inbound from the header to the MCU, U503 outbound
from the MCU to the header. Both take their supply from the 3.3~V rail, which
is a requirement of the arrangement and not an incidental choice; the first
item below derives it. Together they satisfy the requirement in both
directions:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inbound.} The part specifies its input and output ranges
    independently. The input is recommended to \SI{5.5}{\volt} whatever the
    supply is, carrying no $V_{CC}$ term at all, while the output is recommended
    only to $V_{CC}$ and has a clamp current specified above it
    (\cref{tab:lvc1g17}). Overvoltage tolerance is therefore a property of the
    input structure, already present at any supply in range rather than
    conferred by a higher one, and the supply's only effect is to set the
    output level.
    That asymmetry is what makes the part a translator, and it makes the supply
    rail the whole of the design decision: at \SI{3.3}{\volt}, a 5~V-signaling
    adapter is inside specification at the input, and the MCU pin cannot see
    more than \SI{3.3}{\volt} whatever the adapter presents. Supplying the same
    part from 5~V would add nothing at the input, which was already
    \SI{5.5}{\volt} capable, and would put 5~V on a pin recommended to
    $V_{DD}+\SI{0.3}{\volt}$: the very failure this subsection exists to
    prevent, and without even the current limiting a bare series resistor
    would have provided.
  \item \textbf{Outbound.} U503 isolates the MCU's transmit pin from the
    connector entirely, so a miswired or reversed cable reaches a buffer
    rather than the module.
  \item \textbf{Unpowered board.} The buffers' power-off leakage is
    \SI{2}{\micro\ampere} with \SI{5.5}{\volt} on either terminal at
    \(V_{CC}=0\), so signals driven into an unconnected board cannot
    back-charge the 3.3~V rail through the interface. This is the failure a
    bare series resistor leaves open, and it is why the partial-power-down
    specification is a selection requirement rather than a convenience.
  \item \textbf{Signal integrity.} The Schmitt-trigger input tolerates the
    slow, noisy edges an arbitrary length of user-supplied cable presents;
    hysteresis is at least \SI{0.31}{\volt} at \SI{3.0}{\volt} supply.
    Propagation delay is at most \SI{5.5}{\nano\second}, two orders of
    magnitude below a bit period at the 921600~baud rate flashing tools
    commonly use.
\end{itemize}
The buffers also place their own \SI{2}{\kilo\volt} human-body-model rating
between the connector and the module. Each buffer takes a local
\SI{100}{\nano\farad} supply decoupling capacitor (C504, C505), standard
practice for a logic package.

\paragraph{Idle-state pull-ups.} A CMOS input left floating drifts across its
threshold, drawing crossover current and producing indeterminate output.
U502's input floats whenever no adapter is attached, and U503's input floats
while the MCU is in reset, so both are pulled to the 3.3~V rail: R502 on the
header side of U502, R504 on the MCU side of U503. The serial idle state is
high, so this also presents an idle line rather than noise to a receiver that
is listening. The pull-up must hold its input above the
\SI{1.71}{\volt} worst-case positive-going threshold against the input's
\SI{1}{\micro\ampere} leakage, bounding it at
\((3.0-1.71)/\SI{1}{\micro\ampere} \approx \SI{1.3}{\mega\ohm}\); it must also
not burden whatever drives the line low. \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm} sits two orders of
magnitude inside the upper bound and draws \SI{330}{\micro\ampere} when the
line is pulled low. Specification: $R502 = R504 = \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm}$.

\paragraph{Outbound series resistor.} U503's output is exposed at the
connector, and unlike its input it is recommended only to \(V_{CC}\) while
powered (\cref{sec:lvc1g17}). Two fault cases bound it. Shorted to ground, the
output current must stay inside the \SI{50}{\milli\ampere} rating; targeting
half of that for margin gives
\begin{align*}
  R_{503} &\geq \frac{\SI{3.3}{\volt}}{\SI{25}{\milli\ampere}}
    = \SI{132}{\ohm}.
\end{align*}
Driven externally to 5~V, the output clamps near \(V_{CC}+0.5 =
\SI{3.8}{\volt}\) and the same resistor holds the clamp current to
\((5-3.8)/\SI{150}{\ohm} = \SI{8}{\milli\ampere}\), a sixth of the
\SI{50}{\milli\ampere} clamping rating. Against signal integrity,
\SI{150}{\ohm} into a generous \SI{50}{\pico\farad} of connector and cable
capacitance gives \SI{7.5}{\nano\second}, immaterial at any serial rate.
Specification: $R503 = \SI{150}{\ohm}$.

\paragraph{Level compatibility at the adapter.} U503 drives through R503 into
the adapter's receive input. At the buffer's \SI{100}{\micro\ampere}
light-load specification the output sits within \SI{0.1}{\volt} of the 3.3~V
rail, and the series resistor drops nothing measurable into a logic input's
leakage, so the adapter sees close to \SI{3.2}{\volt}. Adapters in this class
present TTL-referenced inputs, whose \SI{2.0}{\volt} high-level threshold it
clears with margin. An adapter presenting CMOS thresholds referenced to a 5~V
supply would require \(0.7\times 5 = \SI{3.5}{\volt}\) and would not be
driven reliably; this is a constraint on adapter selection, satisfied by the
TTL-level adapters the interface targets.

\paragraph{Resulting requirement on the adapter.} Signaling level is
unconstrained: 3.3~V and 5~V adapters are both in specification, in both
directions. What remains is that the adapter present TTL-compatible input
thresholds, as above, and the supply-pin requirements derived in
\cref{sec:3v3-diodeor}.

\subsection{Native USB}
\label{sec:esp32-usb}

The module brings out the ESP32-S3's own USB pins, which its USB Serial/JTAG
peripheral drives without external silicon. Routed to the Type-C receptacle
through the switch of \cref{sec:pd-data}, they carry firmware download, console
and debug over the same connector that powers the board, and they do so at a
point in the sequence where the serial header cannot: \cref{sec:pd-sequence}
brings the MCU up on any source, including a host port that offers nothing but
\SI{5}{\volt}.

The link is full speed. The Serial/JTAG controller is a ``USB Full-speed
device'' (\ds{esp32-s3-wroom-1\_wroom-1u\_datasheet\_en.pdf}{22}), and the
chip's other USB block is a full-speed OTG interface
(\ds{esp32-s3-wroom-1\_wroom-1u\_datasheet\_en.pdf}{21}); there is no
high-speed transceiver on the part. The pair therefore runs at
\SI{12}{\mega\bit\per\second}, which is the fact \cref{sec:pd-data} rests on when
it puts the controller and the MCU on one pair: at high speed the controller's
pin capacitance alone would exceed the whole budget.

This path does not displace the serial header of \cref{sec:esp32-uart}. The two
answer different needs. The header works with the module out of any system
context, needs nothing of the front end, and survives firmware that has
reconfigured or disabled the USB peripheral; native USB needs a working
front end and a running peripheral, and gives in exchange a single cable.

\paragraph{The pins take no buffers.} \Cref{sec:esp32-uart} requires that no
external signal reach an MCU pin directly, and derives buffers from it, because
an arbitrary USB-to-serial adapter may signal at 5~V. That reasoning does not
transfer. USB~2.0 is a bidirectional half-duplex pair driven to the MCU's own
rail, so there is no level to translate and no direction a unidirectional buffer
could take. What the pins need instead is protection against the connector
rather than against the signaling, which \cref{sec:pd-esd} places at the
receptacle where it belongs.

\paragraph{The pins glitch at power-up.} Both are driven high twice while the
chip starts, for about \SI{60}{\micro\second} each, inside windows of
\SI{3.2}{\milli\second} and \SI{2}{\milli\second}, per the schematic checklist
of Espressif's hardware design guidelines. The switch of \cref{sec:pd-data} is
open through that interval and holds the glitches on the MCU's side of itself,
which is a second reason its unpowered state must be the open one and not only a
convenience at attach.

\paragraph{Switch control.} One GPIO drives the data switch's enable. Two
requirements bound which pin it may be:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item It must not be a strapping pin. The strapping pins are sampled at reset
    (\cref{sec:esp32-reset}), and a pin that both selects boot mode and holds
    the data switch open would couple the two.
  \item Its reset state must be high impedance, so that \des{R}{USBSW}
    (\cref{sec:pd-data}) holds the switch open through reset and through any
    interval in which firmware is not driving it. The controller owns the pair
    at attach and must keep owning it until the MCU deliberately takes it.
\end{itemize}
The same reasoning governs the display inhibit of \cref{sec:hv-inhibit}, whose
pull-up holds the tubes dark under exactly the same conditions. Both are
outputs whose safe state is the one a high-impedance pin produces, and both are
therefore assigned from the module's general-purpose pins rather than from the
strapping set.

\subsection{Power-good input}
\label{sec:esp32-pgood}

The flyback controller's PGOOD pin is open-drain, pulled up by R206 to this
rail and sized in \cref{sec:hv-house-pgood}; an MCU GPIO configured as an
input reads the same node. The pin releases to high impedance when the
flyback's output is good and its supply and undervoltage-lockout conditions
are met, and pulls low otherwise (\ds{lm5155.pdf}{22}), so the node reads high
for power-good.

Both levels clear the MCU's input thresholds. High: with the pin released, the
node sits at the rail less the drop across R206 from the GPIO's input leakage,
which is negligible against \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}, so effectively \(V_{DD}\)
against a \(V_{IH}\) of \(0.75\,V_{DD}\). Low: the controller sinks the
pull-up current, \SI{33}{\micro\ampere}, at a saturation voltage far below the
\(0.25\,V_{DD}\) input threshold. If the GPIO's internal
\SI{45}{\kilo\ohm} pull-up is left enabled it parallels R206 to
\SI{31}{\kilo\ohm} and raises the sink current to
\SI{106}{\micro\ampere}, still two orders of magnitude inside the controller's
\SI{1}{\milli\ampere} sink limit, so the interface is correct whether or not
firmware disables it.

The signal's only consumer is powered by the same rail as its pull-up, so it
is valid whenever anything is running to read it, and the load it places on
the 3.3~V rail (\SI{33}{\micro\ampere}, and only during start-up and fault
windows) is four decades under the rail's design load.

\subsection{Reset and boot configuration}
\label{sec:esp32-reset}

The module requires EN held below its \(0.25\,V_{DD}\) threshold while the
supply ramps and released afterward (\cref{sec:esp32}). Espressif's reference
network answers that with an RC delay, a pull-up to the rail against a
capacitor to ground, which approximates the requirement by waiting a fixed
interval instead of watching the rail. A direct measurement is available here
and is used: the 3.3~V converter's power-good output drives
this node directly (\cref{sec:3v3-house}), already terminated by
\des{R}{3V3PG}, its own \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm} pull-up.

The substitution is exact, not approximate, and it holds because the
node cannot outrun the rail that feeds it. EN is pulled up to the converter's
own output, so it can only rise as that output does, and PG holds it at ground
until the output passes \SI{94.5}{\percent} of its programmed value. EN is
therefore low across the whole ramp by construction and released at the moment
the rail is measurably good, which is the requirement itself rather than a
timer standing in for it. No separate pull-up is needed: \des{R}{3V3PG}
terminates PG because PG is open-drain, and the same resistor is what returns
EN high. Steady-state current is the \SI{33}{\micro\ampere} that resistor
carries only while PG is asserted.

Supervision comes with it at no cost. If the rail later falls below
\SIrange{90}{94.5}{\percent}, PG reasserts and holds the module in reset until
regulation returns, where an RC network alone would leave it running on a
collapsing supply.

\paragraph{The capacitor stays, for a different reason.} \(C503 =
\SI{100}{\nano\farad}\) to ground is not a delay element here and is not sized
as one. Its job is the reset switch, which shorts EN to ground mechanically and
chatters on release; without capacitance on the node each bounce is a reset
edge. Against \des{R}{3V3PG} it gives \(\tau = \SI{10}{\milli\second}\), long
enough to swallow the bounce of any tactile switch and too short to shift a
release PG has already gated. It also gives a
high-impedance node some immunity to injected noise. The full
\SI{1}{\micro\farad} of the reference network would serve neither purpose
better and would slow the switch's own assertion for no return.

Download-mode entry requires GPIO0 held low across a reset release and for the
\SI{3}{\milli\second} strapping hold time that follows
(\cref{sec:esp32}). The serial header carries no modem-control lines, so no
automatic entry path exists and the design requires a manual means of
asserting GPIO0 and EN together. GPIO0's internal weak pull-up selects normal
boot with no external component, so only the asserting path is a requirement,
not a pull-up.
