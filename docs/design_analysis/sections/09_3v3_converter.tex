The rail supplies the ESP32 module, \des{R}{HVPG} (the flyback's power-good
pull-up, \cref{sec:hv-house-pgood}), and \des{R}{3V3PG}, which terminates this
converter's own power-good output and doubles as the MCU's reset pull-up
(\cref{sec:esp32-reset}). Two features set it apart from the 5~V rail of
\cref{sec:5v-converter}: a large, transient-heavy load dominated by Wi-Fi
transmit bursts, and an input that is the OR of $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ with an
externally supplied header voltage of either \SI{3.3}{\volt} or \SI{5}{\volt}
nominal. That input can sit below the output, so the topology is buck-boost; a
\SI{3.3}{\volt} adapter would otherwise leave the rail under the ESP32's
\SI{3.0}{\volt} minimum.

\subsection{Load budget}\label{sec:3v3-load}

The dominant load is the MCU module. From \cref{sec:esp32}: the worst published
sustained condition is Wi-Fi transmit at \SI{355}{\milli\ampere} (802.11b,
\SI{20.5}{dBm}, rated at 100\,\% duty cycle), and Espressif's own
regulator-sizing requirement is an external supply capable of
$\geq\SI{0.5}{\ampere}$. Typical operation draws far less.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{BCD drive lines.} Sixteen GPIOs drive the four K155ID1s' BCD inputs. A TTL
input driven high draws $I_{IH} \leq \SI{40}{\micro\ampere}$ (A input) or
\SI{80}{\micro\ampere} (B/C/D inputs) from the driving pin (\cref{sec:esp32-bcd}); a
line driven low \emph{sinks} current sourced from the drivers' 5~V rail and adds nothing
to VDD33. Worst case, all sixteen lines high:
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{BCD}} &= 4 \times (40 + 3\times80)\,\si{\micro\ampere}
    = \SI{1.12}{\milli\ampere},
\end{align*}
\item \emph{Pull-ups.} \des{R}{HVPG} (the flyback's power-good) and \des{R}{3V3PG} (this
converter's, \cref{sec:3v3-house}) each contribute at most
\SI{33}{\micro\ampere}, and only during power-not-good windows.
\Cref{sec:esp32-pgood,sec:esp32-reset} quantify them.
\item \emph{Serial buffers.} The two logic buffers of \cref{sec:esp32-uart} draw
\SI{4}{\micro\ampere} each at most, plus their idle-state pull-ups at
\SI{330}{\micro\ampere} each while a line is held low.
\end{itemize}

The design totals are therefore
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{load,max}} &= 355 + 1.12 + 0.07 + 0.7
    \approx \SI{357}{\milli\ampere} \approx \SI{0.36}{\ampere},\\
  P_{\mathrm{out,max}} &= 3.3 \times 0.357 = \SI{1.18}{\watt}.
\end{align*}

Housekeeping contributes under \SI{2}{\milli\ampere} of that, negligible against
the MCU, so Espressif's $\geq\SI{0.5}{\ampere}$ figure is adopted as the design
load without modification.

\subsection{Operating point}\label{sec:3v3-input}

The converter's input is the OR node of \cref{sec:3v3-diodeor}, which spans
disjoint bands depending on which source is present:
\begin{description}
  \item[Bus-powered, full operation] a \SI{9}{\volt} or \SI{12}{\volt} contract,
    $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ \SIrange{8.5}{13}{\volt} through \des{Q}{3V3BUS}, giving
    \SIrange{8.48}{13.0}{\volt} at the node.
  \item[Bus-powered, limited operation] the \SI{5}{\volt} contract of
    \cref{sec:pd-classes}, $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ \SIrange{4.75}{5.5}{\volt}, giving
    \SIrange{4.71}{5.47}{\volt} at the node.
  \item[Header-powered] an externally supplied \SI{3.3}{\volt} or \SI{5}{\volt}
    through \des{Q}{3V3HDR}, giving \SI{3.15}{\volt} at worst (\cref{sec:3v3-diodeor} derives
    both figures).
\end{description}
All three bands lie inside the part's \SIrange{2.0}{16}{\volt} running range,
and the node's \SI{13.0}{\volt} maximum leaves $1.54\times$ margin to the
\SI{20}{\volt} absolute maximum on VIN. Both branches of
\cref{sec:3v3-diodeor} are series switches, so the bus-powered bands track the
bus rail to within \SI{25}{\milli\volt}. The header-powered band must
additionally clear the \SI{3.0}{\volt} start-up requirement, which that section
treats.

Duty cycle at the extremes of each band:
\begin{align*}
  \text{buck: } D &= \frac{V_{OUT}}{V_{IN}}
    &&\SI{13.0}{\volt}: 0.254; \quad \SI{8.48}{\volt}: 0.389; \quad
      \SI{4.71}{\volt}: 0.701, \\
  \text{boost: } D &= \frac{V_{OUT}-V_{IN}}{V_{OUT}}
    &&\SI{3.15}{\volt}: 0.045.
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Minimum duty cycle in buck mode.} The part specifies a
\SI{30}{\percent} minimum duty in buck mode, relaxed to \SI{20}{\percent} when
the effective output capacitance is at least \SI{40}{\micro\farad} and the
effective inductance falls between \SIrange{0.7}{1.8}{\micro\henry}
(\cref{sec:tps63070}). At the maximum bus input the duty is
\SI{25.4}{\percent}, below the general figure and above the relaxed one, so the
conditions attached to the relaxed figure become requirements on \des{L}{3V3}
and on the output capacitance. This is the binding constraint at the top of the
bus range.

\subsection{Inductor}\label{sec:3v3-inductor}

The recommended effective inductance is \SIrange{0.7}{2.8}{\micro\henry} with a
nominal of \SI{1.5}{\micro\henry} (\cref{sec:tps63070}), and the minimum-duty
relaxation of \cref{sec:3v3-input} narrows the effective value to
\SIrange{0.7}{1.8}{\micro\henry}. Against the datasheet's anticipated effective
tolerance of $+20/-30\,\%$, a \SI{1.5}{\micro\henry} nominal part spans
\SIrange{1.05}{1.8}{\micro\henry} effective, meeting the narrowed range with no
margin at its upper end. \SI{1.5}{\micro\henry} nominal is adopted.

\paragraph{Peak current.} Boost and buck operation peak under different
conditions, and both are checked. In boost mode the datasheet's peak-current
expression applies, evaluated at the minimum input that mode sees,
\SI{3.15}{\volt}, and at the full \SI{0.5}{\ampere} capability point:
\begin{align*}
  I_{PEAK} &= \frac{I_{OUT}}{\eta(1-D)} + \frac{V_{IN} D}{2 f L}
    = \frac{0.5}{0.9\times0.955} + \frac{3.15\times0.045}
      {2\times\SI{2.4}{\mega\hertz}\times\SI{1.5}{\micro\henry}}
    = \SI{0.602}{\ampere}.
\end{align*}
In buck mode the peak is the load plus half the ripple, worst at the maximum
input and at the tolerance extremes that maximize ripple (effective inductance
\SI{1.05}{\micro\henry}, oscillator at its \SI{2.1}{\mega\hertz} minimum):
\begin{align*}
  \Delta I_L &= \frac{V_{OUT}(V_{IN}-V_{OUT})}{V_{IN} L f}
    = \frac{3.3\,(13.0-3.3)}{13.0\times\SI{1.05}{\micro\henry}\times\SI{2.1}{\mega\hertz}}
    = \SI{1.12}{\ampere},
  &
  I_{PEAK} &= 0.5 + \frac{1.12}{2} = \SI{1.06}{\ampere}.
\end{align*}
The buck corner is the larger of the two. Applying the datasheet's recommended
\SI{20}{\percent} saturation margin gives $I_{\mathrm{sat}} \geq
\SI{1.27}{\ampere}$ for normal operation. Under fault, the converter's own
average input current limit caps the draw at \SI{4.15}{\ampere}, so a part
rated at or above that figure cannot be saturated by anything the converter can
produce. Specification: $I_{\mathrm{sat}} \geq \SI{4.2}{\ampere}$, DCR
$\leq\SI{50}{\milli\ohm}$.

\paragraph{Right-half-plane zero.} Boost operation introduces a right-half-plane
zero, which the datasheet asks to keep above \SI{400}{\kilo\hertz} so it does not
degrade phase margin at crossover. It falls lowest at the minimum input and
maximum load:
\begin{align*}
  f_{RHPZ} &= \frac{(1-D)^2 V_{OUT}}{2\pi I_{OUT} L}
    = \frac{0.955^2 \times 3.3}
      {2\pi \times 0.5 \times \SI{1.5}{\micro\henry}} = \SI{638}{\kilo\hertz},
\end{align*}
clearing the recommendation by $1.6\times$ at the worst corner and by
$2.2\times$ at the \SI{0.357}{\ampere} sustained load. The margin comes from the
small boost duty cycle: the header-powered case lifts \SI{3.15}{\volt} to
\SI{3.3}{\volt}.

\subsection{Output voltage programming}\label{sec:3v3-fb}

The adjustable variant regulates its feedback pin to \SI{800}{\milli\volt}, so
the divider ratio sets the output:
\begin{align*}
  \des{R}{3V3D1} &= \des{R}{3V3D2}\left(\frac{V_{OUT}}{\SI{0.8}{\volt}}-1\right)
    = \des{R}{3V3D2}\times 3.125.
\end{align*}
The divider impedance is bounded from both directions. The feedback pin draws up
to \SI{100}{\nano\ampere} of leakage (\cref{sec:tps63070}); that current flows in
\des{R}{3V3D1} and offsets the output by $I_{lkg}\des{R}{3V3D1}$ irrespective of
the ratio, which favors small resistors. Against that, the divider's own current
is quiescent draw that never reaches the load. The datasheet bounds the lower leg
at \SI{400}{\kilo\ohm} to keep at least \SI{2}{\micro\ampere} flowing, and
recommends lower values than that for accuracy.

Taking $\des{R}{3V3D2} = \SI{39}{\kilo\ohm}$ puts \SI{20.5}{\micro\ampere} through the
divider, an order of magnitude above the floor and small against the
converter's own \SIrange{54}{103}{\micro\ampere} quiescent current, while
holding the leakage offset to
$\SI{100}{\nano\ampere}\times \des{R}{3V3D1} = \SI{12}{\milli\volt}$. The exact upper
leg is then \SI{121.9}{\kilo\ohm}; the two standard values bracketing it give
\begin{align*}
  \des{R}{3V3D1} = \SI{120}{\kilo\ohm} &\rightarrow V_{OUT} = \SI{3.262}{\volt}, &
  \des{R}{3V3D1} = \SI{130}{\kilo\ohm} &\rightarrow V_{OUT} = \SI{3.467}{\volt}.
\end{align*}
Only the lower value is viable: at \SI{3.467}{\volt} the Power Save Mode offset
alone carries the rail to \SI{3.57}{\volt}, against an MCU recommended maximum
of \SI{3.6}{\volt} with no absolute-maximum headroom above it
(\cref{sec:esp32}). Programming below \SI{3.3}{\volt} is the favorable direction
regardless, that mode's tolerance being asymmetric at $+3/-1\,\%$
(\cref{sec:tps63070}). Specification:
$\des{R}{3V3D1} = \SI{120}{\kilo\ohm}$, $\des{R}{3V3D2} = \SI{39}{\kilo\ohm}$, both 1\,\% and from
one resistor series so their temperature coefficients track and the ratio does
not drift; $V_{OUT} = \SI{3.262}{\volt}$ programmed.

\subsection{Output capacitance, transient response, and rail accuracy}\label{sec:3v3-cout}

Three requirements bound the output capacitance, all stated in effective terms
because ceramic capacitance falls with DC bias:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Filter ratio.} The adjustable variant requires effective
    capacitance in \si{\micro\farad} at least ten times the effective inductance
    in \si{\micro\henry} (\cref{sec:tps63070}): at the \SI{1.8}{\micro\henry}
    upper extreme, $\geq\SI{18}{\micro\farad}$.
  \item \emph{Minimum duty relaxation.} \Cref{sec:3v3-input} requires
    $\geq\SI{40}{\micro\farad}$ effective, the binding one of the three.
  \item \emph{Recommended range.} \SIrange{15}{470}{\micro\farad} total at the
    output pin, which bounds the specification from above.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Assumption:} the output ceramics retain $\geq70\,\%$ of nominal
capacitance at \SI{3.3}{\volt} bias. The nominal values do not establish this;
it is a separate selection requirement on the parts. Capacitance density and
bias derating trade against each other, and a single part carrying the whole
bulk value can fail the retention figure where two smaller ones meet it. Meeting
\SI{40}{\micro\farad} effective then requires \SI{57}{\micro\farad} nominal.

The datasheet's own filter table pairs a \SI{1.5}{\micro\henry} inductor with
\SI{47}{\micro\farad}, \SI{68}{\micro\farad}, or \SI{100}{\micro\farad} of
nominal output capacitance, and separates bulk capacitance from a small-body
part at the output pin (\cref{sec:tps63070}). Specification:
\des{C}{3V3O3} $= \SI{47}{\micro\farad}$ and \des{C}{3V3O2} $= \SI{22}{\micro\farad}$ as bulk,
\SI{69}{\micro\farad} together and so at the table's \SI{68}{\micro\farad}
entry, plus \des{C}{3V3O1} $= \SI{10}{\micro\farad}$ at the pin.
That is \SI{79}{\micro\farad} nominal and \SI{55}{\micro\farad} effective,
clearing the binding requirement by $1.4\times$. Adding the module-local
\SI{22}{\micro\farad} of \cref{sec:esp32-mcu} brings the node to
\SI{101}{\micro\farad} nominal, still inside the \SI{470}{\micro\farad}
ceiling.

\paragraph{Load-step droop (Wi-Fi transmit burst onset).} The bounding step is
idle-to-transmit, $\Delta I = \SI{0.355}{\ampere}$. The inductor's slew limit
does not bind here as it did on the 5~V rail at \SI{1.1}{\mega\hertz}
(\cref{sec:5v-converter}): at the minimum bus-powered input,
\begin{align*}
  \frac{dI_L}{dt} &= \frac{V_N - V_{OUT}}{L}
    = \frac{8.48-3.3}{\SI{1.5}{\micro\henry}} = \SI{3.45}{\ampere\per\micro\second},
\end{align*}
so the current reaches the new load in \SI{103}{\nano\second}, costing
\SI{18}{\nano\coulomb} and under \SI{1}{\milli\volt}. The dominant term is
control-loop response, for which the datasheet publishes no bandwidth.
\emph{Assumption:} the loop commands full inductor current within
\SI{5}{\micro\second}, carried over from \cref{sec:5v-converter}'s treatment and
conservative for a converter switching more than twice as fast. Then
\begin{align*}
  \Delta V_{\mathrm{droop}} &\approx
    \frac{\Delta I \cdot t_{\mathrm{resp}}}{C_{\mathrm{out}}} + \Delta I \cdot \mathrm{ESR}
    = \frac{0.355 \times \SI{5}{\micro\second}}{\SI{55}{\micro\farad}}
      + 0.355\times\SI{2.5}{\milli\ohm}
    \approx 32 + 1 \approx \SI{33}{\milli\volt}.
\end{align*}
Power Save Mode works in favor here: it holds the output about
\SI{1}{\percent} high at light load, so the step down into PWM starts from above
the nominal value (\cref{sec:tps63070}).

\paragraph{Accuracy stack against the load's window.} On the adjustable variant
the part specifies its regulation tolerance at the feedback pin
(\cref{sec:tps63070}), so the divider's ratio tolerance and the feedback leakage
of \cref{sec:3v3-fb} add to it at the output. The rail spends
most of its life in Power Save Mode, whose $+3/-1\,\%$ feedback tolerance is the
wider of the two modes and therefore the one to check. The $\pm1\,\%$ divider
resistors carry a $\pm1.5\,\%$ ratio error to the output. Leakage contributes
$\pm\SI{12}{\milli\volt}$. Output ripple in Power Save Mode is
\SI{50}{\milli\volt} pk-pk at \SI{22}{\micro\farad} effective
(\cref{sec:tps63070}), scaling inversely with capacitance to
$\approx\SI{20}{\milli\volt}$ at the specified \SI{55}{\micro\farad}. Stacking
these with the transient droop above:
\begin{align*}
  V_{\mathrm{rail}}^{\max} &= 0.824\left(1+\frac{121.2}{38.61}\right)
    + 0.012 + 0.020 = \SI{3.44}{\volt}, \\
  V_{\mathrm{rail}}^{\min} &= 0.792\left(1+\frac{118.8}{39.39}\right)
    - 0.012 - 0.020 - 0.033 = \SI{3.12}{\volt},
\end{align*}
against the MCU's \SIrange{3.0}{3.6}{\volt} window (\cref{sec:esp32}):
\SI{116}{\milli\volt} of margin below and \SI{157}{\milli\volt} above. The upper
margin is the one to watch, the MCU's absolute maximum being equal to its
recommended maximum. It is met with the full Power Save Mode offset, worst-case
divider ratio, leakage in the adverse direction, and ripple all stacked.

\subsection{Input capacitance}\label{sec:3v3-cin}

The part requires at least \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} effective at its input pin,
with \SI{10}{\micro\farad} nominal recommended (\cref{sec:tps63070}), and the
input capacitor must be large enough that supply dips shorter than the
undervoltage-lockout circuit's response time do not reach the pin. At the
\SI{13.0}{\volt} node maximum a \SI{25}{\volt}-rated ceramic loses roughly half
its nominal capacitance to bias, so meeting \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} effective at
that corner requires about \SI{10}{\micro\farad} nominal. Specification: \des{C}{3V3I1},
\des{C}{3V3I2} $= 2\times\SI{10}{\micro\farad}$, \SI{25}{\volt} X7R or X5R, giving
\SI{20}{\micro\farad} nominal and roughly \SI{10}{\micro\farad} effective at the
worst bias, \des{C}{3V3I2} being the pin-local part and \des{C}{3V3I1} the bulk. The
\SI{13.0}{\volt} node maximum sets a floor of \SI{16.3}{\volt} on the rating
under the \SI{20}{\percent} derating used throughout; \SI{25}{\volt} is taken
instead so the bias derating assumed above is achievable.

\des{C}{3V3VAUX} $= \SI{100}{\nano\farad}$ bypasses the VAUX pin, the value the
datasheet requires. VAUX supplies internal loads only and must carry no other
connection. It is a device output, reaching at most \SI{7}{\volt}
(\cref{sec:tps63070}), so its bypass needs $\geq\SI{10}{\volt}$ under the same
derating. The \SI{16}{\volt} grade common in small bodies meets that.

The buck-boost's input current is discontinuous in boost mode but continuous in
buck mode, and the bus-powered case is always the latter. Average input current
at the minimum bus-powered node voltage is
\begin{align*}
  I_{IN} &= \frac{P_{\mathrm{out,max}}}{\eta\,V_N^{\min}}
    = \frac{1.18}{0.85 \times 8.48} = \SI{0.164}{\ampere}
  \quad (\SI{0.229}{\ampere}\ \text{at the \SI{0.5}{\ampere} capability point}),
\end{align*}
drawn from $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ through \des{Q}{3V3BUS}, the largest of the
logic-rail loads on the bus budget. \des{Q}{3V3BUS} adds $I_{IN}^2 R_{DSon}
\approx \SI{5.6}{\milli\watt}$ of series loss at that point;
\cref{sec:3v3-diodeor} takes up the choice of switch over diode.

\subsection{Input path and header-powered operation}\label{sec:3v3-diodeor}

The converter's input node is fed from two sources: $V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ through
\des{Q}{3V3BUS}, and the serial header's supply pin (\cref{sec:esp32-uart}) through \des{Q}{3V3HDR}.
Each source must be isolated from the other. Without isolation on the header
branch, a bus-powered board would present up to \SI{13.0}{\volt} to the supply
pin of an attached adapter, destroying it; without isolation on the bus branch,
a header-powered board would drive the entire bus rail and its bulk capacitance.
The node follows whichever source is higher, with the other branch blocking.

Both branches use MOSFETs, for different reasons. The header branch cannot
tolerate a diode drop at all, as derived below. The bus branch could tolerate
one but not afford it: at the \SI{0.24}{\ampere} it carries in
full operation a Schottky dissipates \SI{92}{\milli\watt}, and in limited
operation \SI{0.44}{\ampere} at \SI{0.35}{\volt} is \SI{154}{\milli\watt}
together with \SI{29}{\milli\ampere} of port current spent on the drop instead
of delivered to the load (\cref{sec:pd-classes} budgets that current against a
\SI{500}{\milli\ampere} port). That is the largest avoidable loss in the
low-voltage design, and it is what puts a transistor in the bus branch.

\paragraph{Permissible drop in the header branch.} The converter needs
\SI{3.0}{\volt} at its input to start
(\cref{sec:tps63070}). \emph{Assumption:} a \SI{3.3}{\volt} adapter presents at
least \SI{3.2}{\volt} at the header under the download-mode load. This is a
requirement on external equipment, and \cref{sec:3v3-adapter} restates it as
such. The budget between that figure and the converter's
start-up minimum is \SI{200}{\milli\volt}, and the header branch's series
element consumes part of it:
\begin{align*}
  V_N^{\mathrm{hdr}} &= \SI{3.2}{\volt} - I_{IN}R_{DSon}, &
  I_{IN} &= \frac{V_{OUT}I_{OUT}}{\eta V_N}
    = \frac{3.3\times0.1}{0.9\times3.15} = \SI{0.12}{\ampere}
\end{align*}
at the bounding \SI{100}{\milli\ampere} download-mode load. Reserving
\SI{150}{\milli\volt} of margin above the start-up threshold caps the drop at
\SI{50}{\milli\volt}, so $R_{DSon} \leq \SI{0.42}{\ohm}$.

That requirement excludes a diode. A Schottky drops \SIrange{0.25}{0.35}{\volt}
at this current, exceeding the entire \SI{200}{\milli\volt} budget on its own
and leaving the node near \SI{2.9}{\volt}, so the converter would not start from
a \SI{3.3}{\volt} adapter. \des{Q}{3V3HDR} is therefore a P-channel MOSFET,
source on the converter's input node and drain on the header supply pin, so that
its body diode conducts from header to node and blocks the reverse direction
that would back-power an adapter.

The \SI{0.42}{\ohm} figure is a target, not a hard limit; it reserves
\SI{150}{\milli\volt} of the budget. Start-up fails only when the drop consumes
all \SI{200}{\milli\volt} of it, at $\SI{0.2}{\volt}/\SI{0.12}{\ampere} =
\SI{1.67}{\ohm}$. The distinction matters because the on-resistance has to be
met at roughly \SI{3}{\volt} of gate drive, which is all this node affords;
parts of this class are usually characterized at \SI{4.5}{\volt}.

\paragraph{Cross-referenced gates.} Each switch must be off when its own source
is the lower of the two and on when it is the higher. That is a comparison
between the two sources, and referencing each gate to the \emph{other} branch's
supply performs it with no control circuit: \des{Q}{3V3HDR}'s gate ties to
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ through \des{R}{3V3HDR} and \des{Q}{3V3BUS}'s gate ties to the
header supply pin through \des{R}{3V3BUS}. Each transistor's source is the shared
node, so each sees
\begin{align*}
  V_{GS} &= V_{\mathrm{other}} - V_N, & V_N &\approx \max(V_{\mathrm{BUS}},
    V_{\mathrm{HDR}}),
\end{align*}
which is negative, and the transistor on, exactly when its own branch is the one
carrying the node. The arrangement is symmetric; the two branches differ only in
the part each one takes.

\Cref{tab:3v3-oring} evaluates the arrangement over the operating
configurations. In every one but the last,
a switch is enhanced by at least the difference between the two sources and the
other sits at $V_{GS} \approx 0$, positive by the millivolts its partner drops.
Positive is off on a P-channel, and $V_{GS} = 0$ is the condition at which
off-state drain leakage is specified, so the blocking branch's leakage comes
from a datasheet figure. The off-state margin is smaller than a diode-fed node
would give, where the blocking gate would sit a forward drop above its source.
Offsetting that, the threshold is at least \SI{0.65}{\volt} away in the same
direction, and recedes further as either source rises.

\paragraph{Required separation between the sources.} The last row is the
exception, and it follows from passive cross-referenced ORing generally rather
than from the parts chosen. \des{Q}{3V3HDR} conducts
only while $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} < V_N - |V_{GS(\mathrm{th})}|$, and when the header
is the higher source $V_N$ is the header voltage itself, so the condition
becomes a requirement that the bus rail sit a full threshold below the adapter.
A \SI{5}{\volt} contract and a \SI{5}{\volt} adapter do not satisfy it, and
neither does a \SI{5}{\volt} contract against a \SI{3.3}{\volt} adapter in the
other direction, \des{Q}{3V3BUS} needing $V_N > V_{\mathrm{HDR}} +
\SI{2.1}{\volt}$ at the worst-case threshold while the node offers
\SIrange{4.71}{5.47}{\volt}. Both switches then sit in subthreshold and the node
is fed through the body diode of whichever branch is higher, at $V_N \approx
\max(V_{\mathrm{BUS}}, V_{\mathrm{HDR}}) - V_{SD}$. The converter regulates from
there without difficulty, its running minimum being \SI{2.0}{\volt}; the rail
itself is unaffected.

The configuration instead puts the whole input current through a body diode, and
which one depends on the adapter's setting. The two branches are not equally
able to carry it:
\begin{description}
  \item[\SI{3.3}{\volt} adapter.] The bus is the higher source and
    \des{Q}{3V3BUS} conducts, through its channel if its threshold is at the
    favorable end of the specified band and through its body diode if not. At
    the unfavorable end, and at the diode's specified maximum drop, the node
    sits at \SI{3.49}{\volt} and the part carries \SI{0.40}{\ampere} for
    \SI{0.50}{\watt} against a \SI{1.08}{\watt} allowance, giving
    $T_j = \SI{98}{\celsius}$ at a \SI{40}{\celsius} ambient. The configuration
    is inefficient but within rating.
  \item[\SI{5}{\volt} adapter.] The header is the higher source and the current
    passes through \des{Q}{3V3HDR}'s body diode, on the branch sized only for
    the \SI{0.12}{\ampere} download-mode load. At
    the specified maximum $V_{SD}$ the node falls to \SI{3.80}{\volt} and the
    part carries
    \begin{align*}
      I_{IN} &= \frac{1.39}{3.80} = \SI{0.366}{\ampere}, &
      P &= I_{IN}V_{SD} = \SI{0.44}{\watt}.
    \end{align*}
\end{description}

The second case is what sizes \des{Q}{3V3HDR}. Its source-drain diode must carry
\SI{0.37}{\ampere} continuously and the package must dissipate
\SI{0.44}{\watt} at a \SI{40}{\celsius} ambient, which against a
\SI{125}{\celsius} junction limit bounds the thermal resistance at
$(125-40)/0.44 = \SI{193}{\kelvin\per\watt}$. A SOT-23 clears that; an SC-70
does not. The degraded configuration therefore sizes this transistor, and
\cref{sec:3v3-parts} carries the requirement in that form. Every other
configuration in \cref{tab:3v3-oring} clears by more than \SI{3}{\volt} of
source separation and is unaffected. \Cref{sec:3v3-adapter} states the
combination as a restriction on use in addition to the rating.

\paragraph{Rating consequences of the cross reference.} A gate referenced to the
opposite branch stands off that branch's full voltage when its own branch is the
live one, so the two requirements are asymmetric. \des{Q}{3V3BUS}'s gate rests
near ground while the node runs to \SI{13.0}{\volt}, so it needs
$|V_{GS}| \geq \SI{16.3}{\volt}$ under the \SI{20}{\percent} derating used
throughout. That figure governs its selection: small P-channel parts cluster at
$\pm\SI{12}{\volt}$ and $\pm\SI{20}{\volt}$ is uncommon among them, the thin
gate oxide that lowers the threshold being the same oxide that limits the
rating. \des{Q}{3V3HDR} faces only what an adapter can present,
\SI{4.99}{\volt}, for $|V_{GS}| \geq \SI{6.2}{\volt}$, which nearly any part
satisfies. Referencing either gate to ground would leave its transistor on while
its own branch was the lower source, the failure the cross reference prevents.

\des{R}{3V3HDR} and \des{R}{3V3BUS} $= \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}$ are series gate
resistors, not pull-ups. Each forms a low pass with its transistor's input
capacitance, so a fast transient on the reference rail reaches the gate slowly
while the node stays where its own input capacitance holds it. With
\des{Q}{3V3BUS}'s \SI{563}{\pico\farad} input capacitance the time constant is
\SI{56}{\micro\second} --- slow against a hot-plug event, but in the harmless
direction, since it delays turn-\emph{off} of a branch whose body diode blocks
the same direction the channel would. Gate leakage across either resistor
offsets the gate by \SI{10}{\milli\volt} at \SI{100}{\nano\ampere}, negligible
against volts of drive. No gate-to-source resistor is used: in parallel with the
series resistor it would divide the node voltage onto the gate and leave the
transistor only partly enhanced.

\paragraph{Discharge requirement on both reference rails.} Each reference node
goes undriven whenever the other source is the one running, and each has a
leakage path charging it from the live side, so neither rests at ground unaided.
Both need a discharge path, and the bound on each follows from what a rise on
that node would cause.

On the bus rail the consequence is loss of gate drive. \des{Q}{3V3HDR} is
enhanced by $V_{\mathrm{BUS}} - V_N$; holding that at or beyond the
$-\SI{2.5}{\volt}$ at which its on-resistance is specified caps
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ at \SI{0.66}{\volt}. The charging current is
\des{Q}{3V3BUS}'s off-state drain leakage, specified as
$I_{DSS}\leq\SI{800}{\nano\ampere}$ at $V_{GS}=0$ and the full
$-\SI{30}{\volt}$ of drain-source voltage (\ds{DMP3099L.pdf}{2}), against the
\SI{3.16}{\volt} it actually stands off. Junction leakage roughly doubles per
\SI{10}{\kelvin}, giving \SI{2.3}{\micro\ampere} at a \SI{40}{\celsius}
ambient, so
\begin{align*}
  \des{R}{BUSBLEED} &\leq \frac{\SI{0.66}{\volt}}{\SI{2.3}{\micro\ampere}}
    = \SI{287}{\kilo\ohm}.
\end{align*}
Specification: \des{R}{BUSBLEED} $= \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}$, holding the rail at
\SI{0.23}{\volt} with a $2.9\times$ margin on the bound and far inside the
\SI{2.0}{\volt} at which \des{Q}{3V3HDR} would actually lose enhancement, its
threshold maximum being \SI{1.15}{\volt}. Its cost is
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}/R = \SI{130}{\micro\ampere}$ and \SI{1.69}{\milli\watt} at
the \SI{13.0}{\volt} top of the window. Any other continuous load on the bus
rail parallels this one and only helps; the bound is written for the bleeder
alone so that it does not depend on another subsystem keeping its own divider.

The bus branch being a transistor is what allows \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm} here. A
Schottky in that position leaks tens of microamps at temperature --- and is
poorly characterized at \SI{3}{\volt} by parts specified at their full reverse
rating --- which would have forced a resistor small enough to dissipate
\SI{16.4}{\milli\watt} standing. The MOSFET's $I_{DSS}$ is some thirty times
smaller and is a specified figure.

On the header branch the consequence is exposure. \des{Q}{3V3BUS} references the
header's supply pin itself, which
\des{Q}{3V3HDR}'s off-state drain leakage charges. Bus-powered with nothing
attached, that pin is otherwise undriven, and a microamp into the few picofarads
of pin and trace takes it to the node voltage in microseconds. The stored energy
is under a nanojoule at \SI{13.0}{\volt} and an adapter's own decoupling absorbs
it on contact, so plugging in does no damage. What remains is that a connector
pin marked as a \SI{3.3}{\volt} supply sits at the top of the bus window.
Requirement: hold it below one silicon forward drop, \SI{0.5}{\volt}, so it
cannot inject current into an attached supply rail through any protection
structure it meets.

\des{Q}{3V3HDR}'s off-state drain leakage is required to be
\SI{1}{\micro\ampere} at $T_j = \SI{25}{\celsius}$ and \SI{10}{\micro\ampere} at
\SI{150}{\celsius}, figures parts of this class carry routinely
(\ds{PMF170XP.pdf}{4}). The switch dissipates nothing while off, so $T_j$ is the
ambient, and log-linear interpolation gives \SI{1.3}{\micro\ampere} at
\SI{40}{\celsius}. Adding \des{Q}{3V3BUS}'s \SI{100}{\nano\ampere} of gate
leakage through \des{R}{3V3BUS},
\begin{align*}
  \des{R}{3V3BLEED} &\leq \frac{\SI{0.5}{\volt}}{\SI{1.4}{\micro\ampere}}
    = \SI{357}{\kilo\ohm}.
\end{align*}
Specification: \des{R}{3V3BLEED} $= \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}$, holding the pin at
\SI{140}{\milli\volt} with a $3.6\times$ margin on the bound. The
\SI{150}{\celsius} leakage figure would put the pin near \SI{1}{\volt}, but a
device dissipating nothing in a \SI{40}{\celsius} ambient does not reach that
junction temperature. Attached, the resistor costs \SI{50}{\micro\ampere} from a
\SI{5}{\volt} adapter and \SI{32}{\micro\ampere} from a \SI{3.3}{\volt} one,
three parts in ten thousand of the \SI{0.12}{\ampere} the branch carries.

Each bleeder also establishes a condition the other switch's rating assumes.
\des{Q}{3V3HDR}'s $V_{DS}$ requirement is written against the node's full
\SI{13.0}{\volt}, which is the drain-to-source difference only if the drain is
held at ground, and \des{R}{3V3BLEED} is what holds it there. The corresponding
obligation on the bus rail falls outside this converter, and it bounds an
impedance without naming a component.
\Cref{sec:pd-bleed} settles whether the front end meets it as a by-product of
its own detach behavior: the port controller's integrated discharge is
event-driven against a detach-timing requirement, not a continuous impedance,
while the requirement raised here is continuous. \des{R}{BUSBLEED} is therefore
populated.

\paragraph{Parts meeting these bounds.} \des{Q}{3V3HDR} is selected against two
requirements. Conduction is undemanding: \SI{0.42}{\ohm} guaranteed at
\SI{2.5}{\volt} of gate drive while carrying \SI{0.12}{\ampere}, which small
SC-70 and SOT-323 parts meet comfortably. The PMF170XP (\ds{PMF170XP.pdf}{6})
specifies $R_{DSon} \leq \SI{300}{\milli\ohm}$ at $V_{GS} = -\SI{2.5}{\volt}$
and $T_j = \SI{25}{\celsius}$, which its normalized temperature curve carries to
\SI{321}{\milli\ohm} at the \SI{43}{\celsius} junction that \SI{6}{\milli\watt}
produces in a \SI{40}{\celsius} ambient, for a \SI{38}{\milli\volt} drop and a
header-powered node at \SI{3.16}{\volt}, \SI{161}{\milli\volt} above the
start-up threshold. The body diode governs instead: \SI{0.37}{\ampere}
continuous and \SI{0.44}{\watt} dissipated require a package of SOT-23 class or
larger, which that part is not, its source-current rating being
\SI{0.4}{\ampere} and its dissipation allowance \SI{290}{\milli\watt} before
derating. Specification: a P-channel MOSFET meeting both requirements, in SOT-23
or larger, where the conduction figure and the \SI{6.2}{\volt} gate rating are
both readily met.

\des{Q}{3V3BUS} is selected on on-resistance at the gate drive limited operation
affords, \SI{4.75}{\volt}, and on the \SI{16.3}{\volt} gate rating derived
above. Holding the node drop to \SI{50}{\milli\volt} at the
\SI{0.41}{\ampere} that band draws requires $R_{DSon}\leq\SI{120}{\milli\ohm}$
hot, so $\leq\SI{110}{\milli\ohm}$ at \SI{25}{\celsius}. The DMP3099L
(\ds{DMP3099L.pdf}{1}), a \SI{30}{\volt} P-channel MOSFET in SOT-23, specifies
$\SI{99}{\milli\ohm}$ maximum at $V_{GS} = -\SI{4.5}{\volt}$ and
$\SI{65}{\milli\ohm}$ at $-\SI{10}{\volt}$, and its normalized temperature curve
gives 1.08 at the \SI{42}{\celsius} junction \SI{18}{\milli\watt} produces, so
\SI{107}{\milli\ohm} guaranteed anywhere in the bus range and less where the
drive exceeds \SI{4.5}{\volt}. The requirement is met with \SI{11}{\percent} to
spare, the tightest figure in this subsection. The remaining ratings clear by
wide margins: $V_{DS} = \SI{30}{\volt}$ against \SI{6.3}{\volt} of reverse
blocking, $|V_{GS}| = \SI{20}{\volt}$ against \SI{16.3}{\volt},
$I_D = \SI{2.9}{\ampere}$ at \SI{70}{\celsius} against \SI{0.41}{\ampere},
\SI{18}{\milli\watt} dissipated against a \SI{1.08}{\watt} allowance, and a
body diode that carries the degraded configuration's \SI{0.40}{\ampere} at
$T_j = \SI{98}{\celsius}$.

\paragraph{Bus-powered operation.} The node sits at
\SIrange{8.48}{13.0}{\volt}, \des{Q}{3V3BUS} conducting with
\SIrange{8.3}{12.9}{\volt} of gate drive and dropping \SI{25}{\milli\volt} at
the \SI{0.5}{\ampere} capability point. \des{Q}{3V3HDR}'s gate sits at
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$, which is that same \SI{25}{\milli\volt} above its source, so
the transistor is off and its body diode is reverse-biased by the difference
between the node and whatever an attached adapter presents. No forward path
exists from the node onto an adapter's supply pin.

\paragraph{Header-powered operation, adapter at \SI{3.3}{\volt}.} With
$V_{\mathrm{BUS}}$ absent, \des{Q}{3V3BUS} blocks the node from feeding the bus
--- its gate is pulled to the adapter's own voltage, above its source, and its
body diode is oriented to conduct only from bus to node --- and \des{Q}{3V3HDR}
conducts. The node sits at $\geq\SI{3.15}{\volt}$, above the \SI{3.0}{\volt}
start-up requirement, and the converter boosts to \SI{3.3}{\volt} with a duty
cycle of \SI{4.5}{\percent}. The converter regulates the node it is given, so
the rail holds its programmed value wherever in its tolerance band the adapter
sits.

Input current exceeds output current in boost mode, so the adapter must supply
more than the MCU draws: \SI{0.12}{\ampere} at the bounding
\SI{100}{\milli\ampere} download-mode load. Adapters that derive their
\SI{3.3}{\volt} from a bridge chip's internal regulator are commonly limited
near \SI{50}{\milli\ampere} and will not support this; one with its own
regulator generally will. Once started, the converter sustains operation down to
\SI{2.0}{\volt} of input while its output holds above \SI{3.0}{\volt}, so a
sagging adapter does not drop the board at the start-up threshold.

\paragraph{Header-powered operation, adapter at \SI{5}{\volt}.} The node sits at
\SI{4.99}{\volt}, the converter bucks at a duty cycle of \SI{66}{\percent}, and
the rail regulates identically. The case is permitted but not required.

\paragraph{Resulting requirement on the adapter.}\label{sec:3v3-adapter} The
supply pin must present at least \SI{3.2}{\volt} and supply at least
\SI{0.12}{\ampere}; either the \SI{3.3}{\volt} or the \SI{5}{\volt} setting
satisfies the voltage requirement, and \cref{sec:esp32-uart} leaves the
signaling level unconstrained. Attaching or detaching the adapter while USB-C
power is present is safe in either order, since the node follows the higher
source and no forward path exists onto the adapter's supply pin in any
configuration of \cref{tab:3v3-oring}.

One combination is excluded, and the exclusion is a restriction on use: an
adapter attached while the board holds a \SI{5}{\volt} contract instead of a
\SI{9}{\volt} or \SI{12}{\volt} one. The two sources are then within a threshold
of each other, neither switch enhances, and the input path reverts to body-diode
conduction as derived above. The board runs, but the branch carrying the node
dissipates a forward drop, and at a \SI{5}{\volt} adapter setting that branch is
\des{Q}{3V3HDR}, the smaller of the two parts. Serial work should therefore be
done either on a \SI{9}{\volt} or \SI{12}{\volt} contract, where the bus rail is
separated from any adapter by more than \SI{3}{\volt}, or on the header alone
with USB-C detached. Both are ordinary conditions; a \SI{5}{\volt} contract is
the fallback when negotiation yields nothing better, and nothing selects it
deliberately.

\subsection{Enable, soft start, and dissipation}\label{sec:3v3-house}

EN and PS/SYNC tie to the converter's input node through
$\des{R}{3V3EN} = \SI{10}{\kilo\ohm}$. The pin's threshold tolerance
(\SIrange{0.77}{0.83}{\volt}, \cref{sec:tps63070}) spans a wider band than the
gap between the converter's \SI{3.0}{\volt} start-up minimum and the
\SI{3.15}{\volt} the input path guarantees, so a divider-programmed lockout
cannot cleanly separate the two; the part's internal undervoltage lockout
together with the source requirements of \cref{sec:3v3-diodeor} keeps operation
inside the specified window. \des{R}{3V3EN} therefore programs nothing and
carries only the pins' \SI{0.2}{\micro\ampere} input current, two microvolts of
drop. Its purpose is to let either pin be pulled low externally without
contending with the input node; that is the board's only means of disabling this
converter to exercise the other rails alone. With both
pins on one node the override is single-valued: pulling the node low takes EN
with it, so the reachable states are enabled in Power Save Mode, and off.
Forced-PWM is not reachable. PS/SYNC ties high, selecting Power Save Mode: the
rail idles at modem-sleep currents for nearly all of its operating life, and the
accuracy stack of \cref{sec:3v3-cout} absorbs that mode's wider tolerance with
\SI{157}{\milli\volt} to spare. VSEL ties low, leaving the output at the value
\des{R}{3V3D1}/\des{R}{3V3D2} program, and FB2, which VSEL would switch in to
raise that value, ties to ground. The datasheet permits either treatment ---
``leave the pin open or connect to GND if not used'' (\ds{tps63070.pdf}{4}) ---
and ground is taken: a defined node is preferable to a floating one at a pin
that reaches the feedback network, and it removes an unterminated input from the
electrical rule check.

PG is an open-drain output, terminated by $\des{R}{3V3PG} = \SI{100}{\kilo\ohm}$
to the rail. The pull-up sits with the converter, so PG presents a defined logic
level at this subsystem's boundary without depending on a resistor placed
elsewhere in the design.

As a status bit the signal would be useless: the only device on the board that
could read it draws its own supply from this rail, and so cannot be running to
receive a fault indication about that rail. PG instead drives the MCU's enable
node (\cref{sec:esp32-reset}), which uses it in the window where the MCU is off.
PG releases above
\SIrange{94.5}{98.5}{\percent} of the output and reasserts below
\SIrange{90}{94.5}{\percent} (\cref{sec:tps63070}), which at the programmed
\SI{3.262}{\volt} is \SIrange{3.08}{3.21}{\volt} rising and
\SIrange{2.94}{3.08}{\volt} falling. The rising band sits above the MCU's
\SI{3.0}{\volt} supply minimum (\cref{sec:esp32}), so the module is released
only into a rail that is already in specification. \des{R}{3V3PG} is the only
pull-up on that node (\cref{sec:esp32-reset}), so the current PG sinks while
asserted is $\SI{3.262}{\volt}/\SI{100}{\kilo\ohm} = \SI{33}{\micro\ampere}$, a
thirtieth of the pin's \SI{1}{\milli\ampere} rating, at a saturation voltage far
below the module's $0.25\,V_{DD}$ reset threshold. That current is also the
resistor's entire load on the rail, and it flows only while power is not good.

Soft start is internal and load-dependent, \SI{400}{\micro\second} bucking and
\SI{850}{\micro\second} boosting at the datasheet's characterization points,
which are this rail's two operating cases almost exactly. The output-node
capacitance is \SI{101}{\micro\farad} nominal including the module-local part,
\SI{71}{\micro\farad} effective, so the charging current is
\begin{align*}
  I_{\mathrm{chg}} &= C_{\mathrm{out}}\frac{dV}{dt}
    = \SI{71}{\micro\farad}\times\frac{\SI{3.27}{\volt}}{\SI{400}{\micro\second}}
    = \SI{0.58}{\ampere}
\end{align*}
in the faster bucking case, against the \SI{1}{\ampere} start-up current limit
the part applies while power good is low. The MCU draws little until firmware
runs, so the margin holds; if it were exceeded, the current limit would simply
extend the ramp.

Converter dissipation at the conservative $\eta=0.85$:
\begin{align*}
  P_{\mathrm{diss}} &= P_{\mathrm{out,max}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-1\right)
    = 1.18 \times 0.176 = \SI{0.21}{\watt}, &
  \Delta T &= \theta_{JA}P_{\mathrm{diss}} = 63 \times 0.21 \approx \SI{13}{\kelvin},
\end{align*}
rising to \SI{0.29}{\watt} and $\Delta T \approx \SI{18}{\kelvin}$ at the
\SI{0.5}{\ampere} capability point: $T_J \leq \SI{58}{\celsius}$ at a
\SI{40}{\celsius} ambient, far below the \SI{125}{\celsius} junction limit.
Wi-Fi transmit at 100\,\% duty is already the sustained rating, so no
duty-cycle relief is needed to reach that conclusion. \des{Q}{3V3BUS}'s
\SI{5.6}{\milli\watt} sits in a separate package and does not enter this figure.

\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:3v3-summary}

This rail departs from the 5~V rail of \cref{sec:5v-converter} in two ways. Its
input can sit on either side of its output, which selects the buck-boost
topology and then propagates through the rest of the derivation: the
minimum-duty bound at the top of the bus range constrains both the inductance
and the output capacitance, and boost operation makes the right-half-plane zero
a check that has to be run at all. The other difference is that both branches of
its input OR carry transistors. On the header branch a diode is excluded
outright, the \SI{200}{\milli\volt} between an adapter's guaranteed output and
the converter's start-up threshold being smaller than any diode's forward drop.
On the bus branch a diode would function but costs \SI{92}{\milli\watt} in full
operation and \SI{29}{\milli\ampere} of a \SI{500}{\milli\ampere} port budget in
limited operation. That second choice carries a consequence: cross-referenced
gates arbitrate between two sources only while those sources are separated by a
threshold, so removing the loss adds an operating restriction
(\cref{sec:3v3-adapter}).
