\section{BCD decoder/driver characteristics}
\label{sec:bcd-driver}

Each IN-12B digit tube (\textsc{nx1}--\textsc{nx4}) is paired
one-to-one with a K155ID1 BCD decoder/driver
(\textsc{u1}--\textsc{u4}), a Soviet-made functional clone of the
Texas Instruments SN74141. Each of the driver's ten outputs is an
open-collector transistor wired directly to one cathode of its tube;
the selected (lit) output sinks current, pulling that cathode low,
while the other nine sit in a high-impedance off state. There is no
interposing high-voltage buffer transistor between driver and tube in
this design --- the driver's own output stage must tolerate whatever
voltage an unselected cathode presents.

\paragraph{Substitution.} No datasheet for the K155ID1 itself could be
located in \texttt{docs/datasheets/}. By explicit decision recorded
for this document, the K155ID1 is treated as electrically equivalent
to a generic TI SN74141, and the figures below are transcribed from
that part's datasheet (\texttt{SN74141\_TI.pdf}, Texas Instruments,
originally published 1972, revised 1983) instead. Every value in this
section is therefore a documented substitution, not a verified
characteristic of the part actually populated on the board.

\subsection{The off-state voltage question}
\label{sec:bcd-voltage-risk}

The datasheet's Absolute Maximum Ratings table gives no distinct
off-state output voltage limit --- it bounds only off-state output
\emph{current} (\SI{2}{\milli\ampere}). The only off-state voltage
figure anywhere in the datasheet is \SI{60}{\volt}, and it is a
\emph{guaranteed operating} point (Recommended Operating Conditions,
and corroborated by the \(V_{O(off)}\) electrical-characteristics row,
which promises no breakdown up to \SI{60}{\volt} under a
\SI{0.5}{\milli\ampere} test load) rather than a stated destructive
limit; the datasheet does not say what happens above it, only that
correct operation is guaranteed up to it. Two separate leakage-current
test points exist below that ceiling (\SI{50}{\micro\ampere} max at
\(V_O=\SI{55}{\volt}\), and \SI{5}{}/\SI{15}{\micro\ampere}
typ/max at \(V_O=\SI{30}{\volt}\) with all ten outputs off) and should
not be mistaken for the guaranteed standoff voltage itself.

This board's anode rail runs \SIrange{160}{196}{\volt}
(\cref{sec:hv-converter}), and with no buffer transistor between
driver and tube, an unselected cathode is exposed to something on the
order of the tube's maintaining voltage up to the full rail voltage
--- \SIrange{145}{196}{\volt} by the figures in \cref{sec:tubes} ---
2.5--3.3$\times$ the datasheet's guaranteed \SI{60}{\volt} standoff.

This is treated in this document as an accepted, community-validated
condition rather than an unresolved defect: direct cathode drive from
a 7441/74141/K155ID1-class open-collector decoder, run well above its
guaranteed off-state rating, is the standard topology used across
essentially all amateur IN-12/IN-14-class Nixie clocks, with a long
track record of field reliability at rail voltages in this same range.
Two effects plausibly explain the margin, neither confirmed from a
cited source: guaranteed datasheet minimums are worst-case
production-lot figures, not the actual breakdown point of any given
part, which in practice typically exceeds the guarantee by a wide
margin; and an unselected cathode inside a lit tube is not necessarily
pulled to the full anode potential the way a simple resistive
off-state model would suggest, since it floats within the tube's own
glow-discharge field rather than being driven there by an external
low-impedance source. This document records the substitution and the
margin explicitly rather than silently assuming the outputs are
safe: it is a documented, precedent-based judgment call, not a
datasheet guarantee, and it remains open pending a datasheet for the
actual populated part (K155ID1) that might state a higher verified
rating.
